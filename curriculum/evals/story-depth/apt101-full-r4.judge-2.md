## Frame

**A (AE101) — 95.** Frame: *agentic engineering is engineering: a feedback-control loop you verify and compound, and the human is the check outside it.*
- M1 the-machine-you-just-met: "A check from outside the session resets the chain." — the plant every later module reads through.
- M3 the-loop-half-filled: "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller**... Cut the feedback signal (a test, a check, a read) and it drifts." — the frame named explicitly.
- M5 what-packaging-is: "**A check is a position fix**. At a fix the wedge of possible states collapses to a point." — M5's packaging only makes sense through it.
- M5 the-gate-is-a-claim: "Green is a claim about the check, not a fact about the work." — the frame turned on its own instrument.
- Where it breaks: M5 the-gate-is-a-claim "Sutton's **bitter lesson**... Today's right procedure, your gates and workflow... is superseded too." and what-packaging-is "Whether this field ever settles into a real best practice is an open question." It names where it breaks, though more lightly than a full 100.

**B (APT101) — 93.** Frame: *building got cheap, deciding didn't: the trio's craft is the bet, the signal and the customer, and the agent amplifies whichever way the team already works.*
- Day 1 apt101-building-is-cheap: "Building got cheap, deciding didn't... Every other step is a decision... None of those got cheaper."
- Day 2 apt101-why-it-agreed: "A faster feature factory is still a feature factory... Agents amplify the way a team already works. They do not transform it."
- Day 2 apt101-each-of-you-makes-something: "Making got cheap. Knowing what good looks like did not."
- Day 3 apt101-three-jobs-rewritten: "Your insight is the choice it leaves you with... That is real strategy work, and it does not get cheaper when building does."
- Where it breaks, on the page: Day 3 apt101-where-you-go-from-here "This is where 'building got cheap, deciding didn't' stops being an edge on its own... What would change this training's mind: agents that start deciding well." That earns the 100 rung, but it is pulled down because Day 1 announces the frame in two lectures before any exercise (apt101-building-rationed-it, apt101-building-is-cheap), so Day 1 reads it before living it.

## Narrative

**A — 93.** In five sentences: An engineer fixes a trivial bug and learns the agent's account is a reconstruction. Plans look decided and aren't. They send a long task off un-packaged, and it comes back drifted, half-done and confidently reported. Reading the return through three lenses, they build checks against their own failure and re-send packaged, only to learn the gate is itself a claim. The narrator then shows the same failures happening to him while he wrote the module.
- The turn: M4 set-the-markers-send-it-off "send it off plainly and you find out what you can't steer yet." → M5 reading-the-return "The closing summary is not the artefact. The machine would rather produce something than admit nothing."
- The training's own failure: M6 story-of-module-6 "The agent I was working with had just finished writing those three modules. It still opened with the un-packaged shape."
- M5 diagnose-and-resend: "The un-packaged run was supposed to underdeliver. What came back is data, not blame."

**B — 91.** In five sentences: A trio arrives with a feature nobody used and paints a box that starts generic. They write a bet that can lose and send an overnight agent through their own door, briefed with their own look-for line. On Day 2 the digest agrees with them. Turned round, the same material gives a second headline, and the turn is that *the headline came from your question*. They build a judge that admits its limit, put a slice in front of five users who break their own Day 1 lines, and leave with a proposal their wider team will change.
- The turn, lived: Day 2 apt101-read-the-digest Phase 4 "run your digest once more from the same brief, with only your look-for line turned round... Each of you reads your two headlines aloud."
- Named after: Day 2 apt101-why-it-agreed "The headline came from your question as much as from your customers."
- Paid off: Day 3 apt101-five-users "says out loud the line in it the five users touched, and whether it held or broke... Your own line, not someone else's."
- Not 100: by design the turn is the student's failure, not the training's own. The narrator's "I wanted to be right" stands beside it, but the training never shows *its own* method failing as AE101's M6 does. Day 1's opening lectures also set out the thesis before the first exercise.

## Room

Do the exercises carry the story in B? Mostly yes. The turn is lived on every seat: each person's own look-for line, own reversed rerun and own Day 1 hypothesis broken in front of five users.

**Strongest beats**
1. Day 2 apt101-read-the-digest Phases 1–4. Each seat sees "the digest's headline, my expectation, my look-for line, and the hypothesis", quoted exactly, then "run your digest once more... with only your look-for line turned round." Every seat produces its own agreement and its own reversal before the lecture names it. The expect line is kept "outside the brief so the run never reads it" (Day 1 apt101-send-it-off), which makes the set-up honest.
2. Day 3 apt101-five-users Phase 4: "each of you reads your own Day 1 hypothesis where Claude quoted it, and says out loud the line in it the five users touched, and whether it held or broke... Your own line, not someone else's." A Day 1 plant is paid off in the student's own words, and all three seats act in the sessions ("The confusion is the finding").

**Weakest beats**
1. Day 2 apt101-catch-it-making-things-up Phases 1–3. One laptop runs it: "The team lead runs the benchmark", "**Don't open the briefing.**", "Don't read the four files as they land. The scorer reads them." Then "18 min, the three of you at the team lead's screen". The other two seats only ready a summary and bet one claim. For most of an hour the room watches a scoreboard it did not produce, and that is the longest exercise in the training.
2. Day 2 apt101-pick-the-outcome: "Claude's prompt treats `challenge.md` as your challenge; yours points at the bet". The product owner drives, and the other two "check". It is a 10-minute plumbing beat that exposes another training's scaffolding, and no seat's own line is at stake.
