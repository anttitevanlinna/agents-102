## Frame

The training's frame, stated in one sentence: **Every module is the same move — you decide what the agent gets, and generic-in-generic-out becomes specific-in-specific-out — first at the level of a prompt, then a memory, then a team of agents, then a check, then a loop, then an organisation.**

- M1's Big Idea is the frame stated in miniature: "With the right guardrails, you create output that's genuinely yours, not generic." Every later module only makes sense as this same claim scaled up: M2's memory is "guardrails at the knowledge level" (compounding.md: "Guardrails did this at the prompt level in Module 1. A memory does it at the knowledge level now. The mechanism is the same; only the shelf life changed"). The frame is explicitly, textually load-bearing across the corpus, not judge-inferred.
- Three modules that only make sense through it: (1) M1 (the frame's origin case: LinkedIn text becomes a genuinely-yours site only through added context); (2) M5 (grounded.md: a judge is "narrow on purpose," i.e. what it's given — its evidence — determines what it can see, same mechanism recast as detection rather than generation); (3) M7 (access-is-not-absorption.md: sharing fails when the *context* — "what you learned getting here" — cannot travel, because the frame's whole claim is that specificity, not the artifact, is the value, and specificity cannot be handed over as a file).
- Where the frame breaks, and the training says so: M4's security.md inverts the direction of the frame on purpose — every other module argues "give the agent more/better context to get a better result"; M4 argues the opposite (practice-of-risk.md: "Everything you have built so far gave the agent more... Now the move is to give it less"). The training does not paper over this; it names the inversion directly as "the move" and explains why both are correct ("Both moves are right, and the difference is what happens when the agent is wrong"). That is a stated fracture point, not a smoothed-over one.

## Narrative

The training as five sentences, with a turn: A person spends a week teaching an agent everything they know about a real problem — feeding it their voice, their sources, their team's arguments — and it gets visibly, measurably better at doing their thinking with them. Then it works almost too well: three retrieved stances get synthesized into an answer sharp enough to act on, and the training makes the protagonist sit with the fact that they cannot yet tell which parts of it are true. **The turn: "Everything you just did is the move this training teaches, run properly. It still handed you something you cannot vouch for" (three-minds-one-synthesis.md)** — competence produces doubt, not confidence, at the exact moment the system starts looking impressive. The rest of the training (M4's residual risk, M5's judge, M6's fixed yardstick) is the slow, unglamorous machinery built in response to that doubt, and even that machinery is shown to have its own blind spot (when-the-score-stops-moving.md). It ends not with the system being trusted, but with the builder being replaced by a describer, and the training refusing to call that a graduation.

Quoted turn: *"You have done this before... Everything you just did is the move this training teaches, run properly. It still handed you something you cannot vouch for. Hold the doubt. Name it to yourself. Don't fix it here."* (`curriculum/exercises/three-minds-one-synthesis.md`)

## Stance

Positions the training could lose a customer over, with where each is defended:

1. **"Certainty is a fantasy you inherited" / avoidance beats mitigation, even when that means recommending less agent, not more.** Defended by mechanism, not assertion: "Avoidance beats reduction. Scope beats patch. Don't-open beats mitigate... Every door you don't open is a residual risk you don't have to name, mitigate, monitor, re-test, or apologise for" (`practice-of-risk.md`). A vendor selling agent adoption has every incentive to say "more access, more automation"; M4 argues the opposite and gives the mechanism (blast radius, non-determinism, emergent capability) rather than a slogan.

2. **"You cannot share an agent; 'share the whole agent' is a vendor pitch and is NOT on the list."** Defended by mechanism: "An agent is context, a boundary, a set of checks and somebody who answers for what it does. The first three copy in an afternoon. The fourth does not copy at all" (`access-is-not-absorption.md`). This is a stance against the easiest, most sellable simplification of the training's own subject matter (curriculum/exercises/share-your-work.md explicitly rules it out as a candidate).

3. **"Don't pick a method because someone said so — including us."** Defended by naming its own authority as insufficient: "Nobody does. Not the framework authors, not the blog posts, not the deck on someone's slide" (`grounded.md`). The training refuses to let its own recommendation stand as authority; it insists the student re-derive the answer empirically on their own material every time.

4. **Held against the training's own commercial interest, explicitly flagged as such in the maintainer notes as the arc's single instance of this:** "We could have spent today building you one impressive agent to show on Friday... it would have demoed well and it would have been the wrong thing to sell you... What you take out of here is the ability to build the one you have not thought of. That is slower, it is harder to put on a slide" (`agents-building-agents.md` § Next). A trainer with an incentive to look impressive on the last day chooses not to.

## The pair

Stance is real and mechanism-backed throughout (M4's less-not-more, M7's you-cannot-share-an-agent, M8's against-interest closer). Self-doubt is also real and structurally load-bearing, not decorative: M3's synthesis doubt is explicitly told not to resolve ("Hold the doubt... Don't fix it here"), M4's residual risk stays named not solved, M5 is "mechanical rescue... not full closure," M6 has a whole lecture devoted to distrusting its own judge (`when-the-score-stops-moving.md`), and M7/M8 hold two unfilled first-person scar slots rather than fabricating a stand-in. Both qualities are present and mutually reinforcing — the stance earns its edge precisely because the training keeps admitting what it doesn't know, and the doubt earns its weight precisely because the training is willing to take positions rather than hedge everything into mush. This reads as **someone who has been there**: a practitioner confident enough to say "don't open that door" and "you cannot share an agent" while also confident enough to leave the scoreboard's own blind spot, and their own missing scar, visible on the page rather than papered over.
