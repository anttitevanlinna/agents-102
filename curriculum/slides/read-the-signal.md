## The agreed signal decides, especially when it says no
<!--slide:read-the-signal-->

The bet was written with its signal before anything was built: the number, the window, what would count. The slice has been in front of customers, and the signal is back.

Eric Ries names the decision: pivot or persevere. Stopping is the third option. Barry O'Reilly asks for the signal before the test "to reduce the bias of interpretation of results", and that bias pulls hardest when the people reading the result also built the slice. Decide on what you agreed, not on how the result feels.

Ries is also plain about what a slice is for. The minimum viable product "is not about creating minimal products". It is the version that teaches the most about customers for the least effort. A slice that came back no did its job.
