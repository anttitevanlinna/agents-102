# A101 story proposal — the model rotates, the written-down half carries

<!-- maintainer -->

Creative-diverge output per `trainings/agents-101/story-depth.md` § Dispatch shapes. Opening
bias: *the model rotates; what compounds is the organisation's learning rate, or it doesn't.*
Read against the full A101 corpus in student order plus the three AE101 bar lectures. Nothing
here edits curriculum. Drafted student lines are marked **DRAFT** and are card material, not
applied text.

---

## 1 · Frame

**Every part of the agent you assemble is a piece of judgement moved out of a head and onto
disk, which is why the work survives the model that runs it.**

This is not a new lens laid over A101. It is the name for the refrain the quartet judges found
and nobody had claimed: *in the full agent picture, this is the first piece: context. Later you
add tools, goals, checks, boundaries, and loops.* The anatomy assembles one part per module. The
frame says what the anatomy is made of. Each part is a thing your organisation knew and had
never written down anywhere a machine could read it.

The leader-shaped version, which is the version that earns the audience-fit gate: the model is
rented and replaceable; the written-down half is owned and compounds. Tools commoditise. The
files do not.

### The eight modules as cases of it

| M | The piece added | What got written down |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | context / guardrails | your taste. What "generic" means in your voice, in a file that survives the chat window |
| M2 | shelf life (memory) | what your organisation knows about one live challenge, pulled out of wikis and threads nobody re-reads |
| M3 | other agents | how your organisation divides work and disagrees with itself. Three stances, a split rule, a framework that makes it pick |
| M4 | the boundary | what your organisation will not permit. The doors you decided not to open, on record, with the residual named |
| M5 | the check | what your organisation counts as evidence. A measured judge that names its own limit |
| M6 | the loop | what your organisation means by better, running while nobody is in the chair |
| M7 | the interface | the part that is still **not** written down. Absorption is what happens in the other person's head, and no file reaches it |
| M8 | the writing-down, delegated | the rate itself. An agent that builds agents means the organisation writes itself down faster than you can |

M7 is the case that proves the frame rather than repeating it: it is the module where the
written-down thing stops working, because the receiving human is not a runtime.

### Where the frame breaks, as the training would say it to you

Two breaks, and they are the training's hardest material, not its footnotes.

**DRAFT (M2 or M7, one beat):** *What you have not written down, the agent cannot use. That
sounds like a to-do list. It isn't. Most of what you know about your own organisation has never
been written down by anyone, and a fair amount of it cannot be. You know which of your people
will quietly stall a rollout. You have never typed that sentence and you are not going to.*

**DRAFT (M6 or M8, one beat):** *Half of what you wrote down this week is judgement and half is
a workaround for the model we have today. You cannot tell which is which by looking at the file.
You find out when the model changes and half your rules stop earning their keep. When that
happens, the rule was never the asset. The writing down was.*

The second break is the one that keeps the frame from becoming a Bosser slogan. If the
written-down half were durable, the frame would be a promise. It isn't, so the frame is a
question, which is what the forward-looking anchor's top rung asks for: *what would change the
training's own mind.* This is the sentence that does it.

### Honest limits of this frame (read before picking it)

- It is a house thesis. Learning-rate-as-ceiling is Bosser's own strategic claim, so the
  confirmation-bias risk the theory plan flags is live here, worse than for any other bias in
  the diverge. The break above is the mitigation; without it this proposal is a pitch.
- **It is weakest exactly where A101 is strongest.** M3 and M5 are craft inside one session.
  Reading them as organisational learning is a stretch and a cold reader will feel the stretch.
  My honest read: at M3 and M5 the frame should go quiet and let the module be itself.
- It does not rescue the creativity arc. The working headline (control and creativity as one act)
  does that job better. My frame braids the orphaned creativity beats only as *variety you wrote
  down*, which is thinner than what they deserve. If the creativity arc is the priority, pick
  a different proposal or blend the M3 naming from one.
- For a leader whose organisation will not move in two quarters, "learning rate" is a frame that
  makes the whole week un-actionable. The governors are the counterweight, and they are the
  factor this proposal spends (see § 8).

## 2 · Narrative

The mood arc is fixed and the plot lives inside it: joy → compounding → unease → deeper unease →
rescue → leverage → generosity → awe, with M3 and M4 unresolved until M5 and M8 ending in
forward hunger.

**The training as a story, in five sentences.**

1. A leader brings one real challenge, feeds the machine enough of their own world that the
   output stops sounding like everyone else's, and gets the small shock of recognition that
   makes the week possible.
2. The system grows, three agents search and three decide, and the answer comes back at a
   distance they cannot close, then Module 4 shows them the thing reads every document as an
   instruction and that certainty was never on the table in the first place.
3. They stop waiting for certainty and build a check instead: four detectors on the same claim
   pool, one scoreboard, a judge narrow enough to defend, and for the first time the doubt has
   a shape and a size.
4. They put the judge in a loop, walk away, and come back to work that got sharper without them,
   which is the turn: the model did not improve while they were gone, the written-down thing did
   the work.
5. They try to hand it to one person, discover that access is not absorption and the people plan
   stalls on names, and leave carrying the question nobody in the room answers.

**The turn, quoted and extended.** The turn already exists on the page as a mood beat and is
never named. `exercises/eval-loop.md` closes Phase 3 with three words on their own line:

> And you weren't there.

That is the lived half. The named half is missing, and one drafted sentence supplies it.

**DRAFT (M6, immediately after the eval-loop close, or opening the M6 closer):** *Nothing about
the model changed while you were away. The same machine, the same week, the same rented
intelligence. What changed is that the thing you wrote down kept applying in a room you had
left.*

Why this is the turn and not M5: M5 is the rescue, and a rescue restores the leader's own
control. The turn is the moment control stops being personal. The leader hands a standard to a
system and the standard holds without them, which is the first beat in the week that is about
an organisation rather than a person. Everything after it (M7's handoff, M8's flywheel) is that
same move at a larger radius, and the question at the close is that move extrapolated past the
model's shelf life.

**What the plot costs the mood contract, checked beat by beat.** M3 and M4 gain nothing from
this frame and must not: the doubt is load-bearing and the frame stays silent there apart from
M4's one analogy break. M5 keeps its bounded rescue. M6 carries the turn AND the Bainbridge
counter-voice, which is a real mood risk (see § 8). M7's generosity survives because the frame's
break lands there as a finding about people, not a correction of the student. M8 ends on the
question, so forward hunger is structural rather than decorated.

## 3 · Point of view

### Who is telling this

A builder leader who ran this on their own organisation, got the technical half right, and
watched the organisational half not happen. Not a trainer explaining agents. Someone reporting
back from a rollout that half-worked, who is still inside the problem and says so.

The narrator A101 already has is close to this and never appears in an incident. Today they show
in asides (*"Damn, this is complex stuff"*), in admissions (*"Hold the doubt"*), and in one
self-implicating line (*"Nobody knows where agents are going. Not Anthropic, not your board, not
the people running this training"*). That last line is the narrator at full strength and it is
one sentence long.

### The incident SLOT

Not invented here. What follows is the specification Antti fills.

- **Module:** M7, placed in the new *Access is not absorption* lecture, after the three
  exercises, before the Key Concepts. Second choice is M2, in the Debrief, but M7 is right:
  it is the module whose finding is organisational, so the narrator's failure should be
  organisational too, and M7 currently has no lecture and therefore no voice at all.
- **Shape:** something the narrator built that worked for them, handed to a team, and that
  nobody used. First person, past tense, specific. Named artefact, named month, a number if
  there is one. The failure is the narrator's, not the team's, and the sentence that proves
  it is the one where the narrator says what they assumed.
- **What it must show, in order:** (a) the technical plan was fine and shipping it was easy;
  (b) what the narrator assumed about the receiving people, stated as a belief they held, not
  as a mistake they now describe; (c) what turned out to be true instead; (d) the specific
  thing the people around it already had to know for it to land, and did not; (e) no lesson
  sentence at the end. The lecture body draws the lesson two paragraphs later. A scar that
  explains itself is an anecdote.
- **What it must not be:** an agent that hallucinated. That is AE101's genre and it is a tool
  failure. This scar is about a working tool that no one picked up.
- **Length:** 12 to 18 lines, which is `story-of-module-6`'s shape compressed for an audience
  that reads less. Shorter than 10 and it reads as an admitted uncertainty again, which is
  precisely the 80-to-95 gap the quartet measured.

**The surrounding beat, drafted, with the slot marked.**

> **DRAFT — M7 lecture, `access-is-not-absorption.md`**
>
> ## You cannot share an agent
>
> You can share the context. You can share a skill. You can share the output. You can share an
> interface. Four shapes, and the list is short on purpose, because the fifth one people reach
> for does not exist.
>
> "Share the whole agent" is a vendor pitch. Here is the mechanism under that. Your agent is
> context plus rules plus memory plus a model. Three of those four are yours and the fourth is
> rented. Hand the whole thing over and your teammate receives your judgement with none of the
> reps that produced it, running on a model that will be replaced before they have built any of
> their own. They get the output of your learning and none of the learning.
>
> ## What I got wrong about this
>
> [ANTTI'S INCIDENT — 12 to 18 lines, first person. The thing you built, who you gave it to,
> what you assumed about them, what was actually true, what they would have needed to already
> know. No lesson sentence at the end.]
>
> ## People absorb what they already half know
>
> The uncomfortable finding has a forty-year-old name and it is not a management idea. An
> organisation takes in new knowledge in proportion to what it already holds. Not in proportion
> to what it is given. You can put the thing in front of someone and the gap you are looking at
> is not an access gap.
>
> You felt this in the exercise. Your technical plan filled quickly. Your people plan stalled
> on names.

Note the mood: M7 is generosity, so the incident is told without self-punishment and the beat
after it is a finding about how people work, not a correction of the reader. `check_strategy_tie_in`
§2 binds here.

### Register, three example sentences drawn from existing A101 lines

The narrator's voice is already in the corpus. These three are the calibration, and any drafted
line in this proposal that does not sit beside them is wrong.

1. *"A working snake game is a small, permanent, perfectly useless thing to own, which makes it
   more interesting than most of what you produce at work."* (`prework.md`) — dry, comic, zero
   flattery, and it lands a real point about ownership sideways.
2. *"Damn, this is complex stuff. It will still be complex when the next agent gets built."*
   (`lectures/practice-of-risk.md`) — the narrator refusing to make the work sound smaller than
   it is.
3. *"Nobody knows where agents are going. Not Anthropic, not your board, not the people running
   this training."* (`lectures/what-just-happened.md`) — the narrator including themselves in the
   doubt, in the same breath as the authority they are borrowing.

What the register is not: no *"why this matters for your career"*, no vendor futurism, no
sentence whose job is to make the reader feel advanced. The narrator's whole claim to attention
is that they have been wrong in public.

## 4 · Stance

Positions the training holds that could lose it a customer, with where each already sits and
what defends it. Two are argued today; three are asserted and are cheap to defend in a sentence.

### 4.1 "Share the whole agent" is a vendor pitch

**Where:** `personal-to-team.md` § Pick the sharing shape and § Key Concepts, plus
`exercises/share-your-work.md`: *"Share the whole agent" is a vendor pitch and is NOT on the
list.* Stated three times, defended zero times. Every quartet judge flagged it.

**Mechanism, drafted (two sentences, lands in the M7 lecture above):** *Your agent is context
plus rules plus memory plus a model. Three of those four are yours and the fourth is rented, so
handing over the whole thing gives your teammate your judgement with none of the reps that
produced it.*

Commercially this is the position that costs the most in the room: the buyer who came to buy a
platform rollout is being told the object they wanted to procure does not exist.

### 4.2 Certainty is a fantasy you inherited

**Where:** `lectures/practice-of-risk.md`. **Already defended**, with the strongest mechanism in
the corpus: non-determinism, the instruction set as attack surface, emergent capability, then
*"You don't get secure / not secure. You get safe enough, under these conditions, within these
limits, for now."* Leave it alone. This is the model for what the other three should look like.

### 4.3 Start with don't

**Where:** `lectures/when-to-split-an-agent.md`: *"Default to one agent with a good prompt"*,
*"A whole module just showed you multi-agent works. Next Monday, you will be tempted to apply it
to everything. Don't."* **Already defended**, with the bluff test: *"can I write one prompt that
produces the same quality of output?"* This is a training spending its own module's momentum
against itself, which is why judges keep finding it.

### 4.4 You do not pick a method because someone said so

**Where:** `lectures/grounded.md`: *"Not the framework authors, not the blog posts, not the deck
on someone's slide."* **Already defended** by the scoreboard, which is the mechanism made
physical. One sentence would sharpen the edge, because the current target is anonymous:

**DRAFT (M5, one sentence):** *That includes this training. We picked four detectors because
they fail in different directions, not because we know which one wins on your material, and we
do not.*

### 4.5 Reading about it is a different category of activity

**Where:** `lectures/what-just-happened.md`: *"Reading a McKinsey report on the agentic
enterprise is not the same category of activity as what you just did."* Asserted with a good
line and no mechanism. The mechanism is the frame:

**DRAFT (M1 closer, one sentence):** *A report hands you someone else's written-down judgement
about a model that will be replaced. You just produced a line of your own.*

### 4.6 The against-interest position, new, on the page

The quartet's smallest move 4 asks for this and the corpus does not have it. Two candidates;
I would ship the first and consider the second for M6.

**DRAFT (M8 closer, replacing the five-step upsell):** *There is a version of this week where we
build your agents for you and you go home with something that runs on Monday. It sells better
than this one and we do not offer it. The reason is not generous: an agent we build carries our
judgement about your work, and the judgement is the part that compounds. You would own the file.
We would own the thing that made it.*

**DRAFT (M6 or M8, the sharper one):** *Half of what you wrote down this week is a workaround for
the model we have today, and we cannot tell you which half. When the next one arrives and your
rules stop earning their keep, do not defend them.*

The second is against-interest twice over: it tells a buyer the product has a shelf life, and it
is the training's own answer to *what would change our mind*, which is the forward-looking
anchor's top rung that no A101 learning currently reaches.

### The pair

Stance and self-challenging together. A101 already reads as someone who has been there on the
risk material (`practice-of-risk` holds ground and doubts itself in the same lecture). The
proposal's job is to extend that pairing to two places where the training currently only holds
ground: sharing (4.1, which doubts nothing today) and the close (4.6, which currently pivots to
an upsell). Nowhere in this proposal does doubt get added without a position beside it, because
doubt alone reads as hedging and the leader audience prices hedging immediately.

## 5 · The learning-set as consequence

Five learnings. Three are on the page and under-braided; two are new work. The future thread is
learning 5 and it is also the braid, which is the trade this proposal makes deliberately: rather
than buy forward-looking with a sixth learning, it buys it by making one existing learning carry
the future.

**The braid in one line:** 1 and 5 fuse (the written-down half is both what is yours and what
the organisation can absorb); 2 and 4 fuse at the point where moving up a level means judging
judges instead of work; 3 is the register the whole week runs in and is what stops 5 from
becoming optimism.

---

### Learning 1 — "What in this is mine, and what is the model's?"

**Question carried:** when the model gets replaced, which half of what I built goes with it?

**Planted — M1**, `lectures/context-is-king.md`: *"Context is whatever you tell it. A fact. A
role. A preference. A constraint."* And the exercise proves it with the reader's own name on it.

**Complicated — M2**, `lectures/compounding.md`: *"Same plain-text form for the knowledge, same
plain-text form for the capability, same plain-text form as the guardrail you wrote in Module 1.
One kind of object, three jobs."* Plus the line that already carries the frame and is not marked
as carrying it: *"Fundamentals outlast tools. Platforms will churn."*

**Complicated — M4:** the boundary is yours too. What the agent may not reach is a decision your
organisation made, written where the next model inherits it.

**Complicated — M7:** the first time the mine/rented split does real work, because it is what
makes "share the whole agent" false rather than merely discouraged (§ 4.1).

**Paid off — M8:** the extension exercise. You described; the tool built. What you owned was the
description, and the description is written down.

**Governor:** *Could a competitor claim this?* Already on the page (`lectures/compounding.md`),
already re-fires unprompted, and it generalises cleanly from a memory page to any context the
leader funds.

**Counter-voice:** the one the step-0 judges asked for and A101 lacks. More context does not
always fix generic output. **DRAFT (M2, one beat):** *Sometimes you add the sharpest detail you
have and the page gets no better, because the page was not thin, it was aimed at the wrong
question. Context is not a volume control.*

**About the future:** a question. The rented half rotates on someone else's schedule; the owned
half is the only thing you can compound on purpose.

---

### Learning 2 — "Confident is not verified, and the check is a claim too"

A101's strongest learning today (depth 85 to 100) and the one place the corpus has a hole the
theory plan names outright: it never turns the scrutiny on the check.

**Planted — M1**, `getting-going.md` Debrief: *"The LLM is generous to a rules file it just
helped write."* The student feels sycophancy before anyone names it.

**Complicated — M4 pre-read**, `lectures/module-5-prework.md`: *"Asking the same model 'are you
sure?' is not a check on the world; it is another fluent answer."*

**Complicated — M5**, `lectures/grounded.md`: the compound-reliability arithmetic, then its
inverse. Mostly-right ten times over is mostly wrong; a loop flips the same compounding.

**Complicated — M6**, `lectures/when-the-score-stops-moving.md`: *"A flat score is real
information, but only about the frame the judge can see."* This is A101 half a step from
doubting its own instrument and stopping.

**Paid off — M6, new beat.** The one control law A101 lacks outright. **DRAFT (M6 opener, under
Mollick's question):** *Green means what the gate can see. A pass looks identical whether the
judge is calibrated, the generator learned to please it, or this session got lucky. You built
the judge. You are the only person who can tell which.*

**Governor:** *"I can't tell" is a real answer. What would close it?* Already on the page at M4
and it re-fires at M5 in the judge's known-limit line.

**Counter-voice:** the M5 judge naming its own blind spot, already there and now generalised.

**About the future:** a question. Better models will fabricate less and will not stop. The judge
gets cheaper to build and never stops being yours to calibrate.

---

### Learning 3 — "Certainty is unavailable; a repeatable loop replaces it"

A101's second-strongest. It needs no more words, only a position on the spine. It is the
register the other four are spoken in: this training does not promise safety, it hands over a
loop and a vocabulary for what is left.

**Planted — M3**, `exercises/three-minds-one-synthesis.md`: *"the answer sits at that uneasy
distance where you'd stake your reputation on some of it and not all of it… Hold the doubt."*

**Complicated — M4**: assess, mitigate, reassess residual, decide. Plus *"The best mitigation is
the one you don't need."*

**Complicated — M5**: bounded rescue, explicitly non-closing. *"Not full closure: your Module 3
doubt and your Module 4 residuals stay where they are."*

**Paid off — M6/M8**: the device reused at the close. Some of what did not resolve should not
resolve.

**Governor:** *What's the worst thing it could do with that access?* Already the M3 to M4 bridge.

**Counter-voice:** `practice-of-risk` already carries it. *"Some of your agents are going to be
wrong, in ways you won't catch."*

**About the future:** a question, and it is the cadence one. The residual used to be signed off
quarterly. Now it moves while you watch.

### Learning 4 — "The human moves one level up, and that is where the watching stops"

On the page as a payoff (M6 recap, M8 scale-shift) and never doubted anywhere. Step 0 scored
self-challenging 20 to 40 on it and called it A101's one silent counter-voice. This is the
cheapest large gain in the set: one beat.

**Planted — M2**, Debrief: *"You spot them by running the system and watching where it bends."*
The leader is still inside every loop and the module says so.

**Complicated — M5**: propose, double-check, apply. The human moves from doing to reviewing a
proposal with its evidence and its known limit attached.

**Complicated — M6**, `lectures/new-human-role-in-the-loop.md`: *"The new role is to decide the
shape of the loop."* And the governor that makes it a decision rather than a posture: *"Would
you let it send the mail?"*

**Paid off — M6, new counter-voice beat.** **DRAFT:** *Every clean result is two things at once.
It is real evidence the system is competent, and it is one more week since you last read one of
these yourself. Both are true and they move in opposite directions. The better it gets, the less
qualified you are to notice when it stops being good.*

**Complicated again — M8:** the same trade at room scale. Twenty agents proposing means twenty
proposals nobody read end to end, which is why the forum's citation rule exists and why the
human still picks.

**Governor:** *When did you last read one of these yourself?* New, and it is the leader-shaped
version of a move that fires in a meeting rather than at a keyboard.

**Counter-voice:** the beat above is the counter-voice. It is the whole point of the learning.

**About the future:** a question, and the least comfortable one. The higher you move, the harder
it is to tell a good answer from a fluent one, and the direction of travel is up.

---

### Learning 5 — "Access is not absorption; and will your organisation learn faster than the model changes underneath it?"

Today a single-module headline (step 0: depth 30 to 60, forward 0 to 20, stated at M7 and
restated inside M7). Under this frame it becomes the spine's outer edge and the carrier of the
future thread.

**Planted — M2**, and this is the move that makes it a learning rather than a headline: the
memory you build is your own absorptive capacity made literal. You can only put in what you have
already written down, and you discover at the wiki what your organisation never wrote.
**DRAFT (M2 Debrief, one line):** *What you haven't written down, it can't use. Notice how much
of what you know about this challenge has never been written down by anyone.*

**Seeded as a question — M2 Debrief.** **DRAFT:** *The model underneath this will change, more
than once, before your challenge is finished. The memory will not.*

**Complicated — M4:** what you refuse to write down is also a decision. The doors not opened are
the fastest-absorbed rule in the set because they need no training to follow.

**Complicated — M6:** the bitter-lesson question, already the best forward-looking beat A101 has
and currently asked once and dropped. `lectures/evals-as-steering.md`: *"will the bitter lesson
apply inside your company?"* Echo it here as the frame's own break (§ 1).

**Complicated — M7:** the payoff of the access half. *"The technical plan fills quickly; the
people plan stalls on names."* With the narrator's incident (§ 3) under it.

**Paid off — M8**, the callback the step-0 judges unanimously asked for, against the shared
folder. **DRAFT:** *Twenty agents published into one folder today and every one of them could
reach everything. You saw what came back. Access was never the constraint.*

**The close — M8**, the question, unanswered. **DRAFT:** *Will your organisation learn faster
than the model changes underneath it? We do not know. Nobody does. What is known is that the
organisations that will find out first are the ones already writing it down.*

**Governor:** *Who absorbs this, and what do they already know?* New, and it is the only governor
in the set that fires in a meeting about people rather than about agents, which is what the
theory plan's leader test asks for.

**Counter-voice:** two, and both are the frame's breaks (§ 1). What cannot be written down, and
the half of what you wrote that is a workaround for this model.

**About the future:** a question, seeded at M2, echoed at M6, asked at M8, never answered. The
backing-block guard AE101 uses on `the-2-frontiers` applies: nobody gets to answer this one in a
later pass.

## 8 · Cost

What this story gives up, said plainly, because a profile is a chosen shape and not a scoreboard.

**An open future costs applicability, and here is the exact bill.** The M8 close stops handing
over a next step. Today `agents-building-agents.md` § Next ends on a five-step path with a named
next product; under this proposal it ends on an unanswered question and a refusal. For a CEO who
came to buy a roadmap, that is the single most valuable paragraph in the week and it is being
deleted. Every drafted governor survives (five of the nine in the theory plan reach the page, up
from one on the skeleton), so the Tuesday moves are intact. What is gone is the arc-level answer.

**A counter-voice costs mood, and it lands on the best module.** Learning 4's counter-voice sits
inside M6, whose contracted mood is leverage, and its content is that leverage costs you the
ability to notice. That is a deliberate dent in the module that currently feels best, placed
there because that is the only beat where the student has just felt the leverage and can afford
the correction. If the story judge's per-beat mood scores drop on M6, this is the cause and the
call is Antti's, not the loop's.

**Depth costs breadth, and the thing crowded out is creativity.** Five learnings developed to the
anchors' upper rungs leaves no module-minutes for a sixth, and the sixth A101 most deserves is
the one the working headline names: three stances, the beige synthesizer, the Rory seat, the
framework that makes it pick. Under my frame those stay what they are today, excellent orphaned
beats, braided only weakly as *variety you wrote down*. **This is the honest weakness of the
learning-rate bias** and Antti should read it as an argument for blending rather than picking.

**Which factor I expect to move down: applicable in practice**, A101's current top at 83. Not
far, because the governors are the thing this proposal protects hardest, but the M8 close and the
removal of the skills-reuse block at M4 both trade a Monday answer for an open one. Expected
shape: forward-looking up substantially (44 is the floor being bought, and learning 5 reaches the
top rung with § 4.6's *what would change our mind* answered about the training itself);
self-challenging up (learning 4 gets its first counter-voice, learning 2 gets the gate beat);
earned-not-announced roughly flat, since the naming beats all sit after the moves but M2's
Debrief now carries a plant the student has not yet felt at organisational scale; depth up on
learnings 4 and 5, flat elsewhere; applicable down slightly.

**The gap this does not close.** Point of view was the widest quartet gap (80 vs 95) and this
proposal does not close it. It specifies the slot and writes the beat around it. Only Antti's
incident closes it, and if that incident does not exist or will not be told, the frame still
works and the point-of-view number does not move.
