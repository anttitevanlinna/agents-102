## Findings

### [Finding 1] EU AI Act Article 50 — Grandfathering provision clears key uncertainty; C2PA is the only deployed watermarking standard

**Sources:**
- https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/ — [regulatory text, fetched July 28 2026]
- https://www.lw.com/en/insights/ai-act-update-eu-resolves-to-change-rules-and-extend-deadlines — [domain trade publication / law firm analysis] (2026)
- https://compliancehub.wiki/eu-ai-act-marking-labelling-code-of-practice-article-50-2026/ — [domain trade publication] (2026)
- https://www.licentium.io/post/eu-ai-act-article-50-transparency-obligations-august-2026 — [domain trade publication] (2026)

**Date:** Fetched July 28, 2026
**Agent level:** company
**What:**

Cycle 170 (July 22) established the core Article 50 picture. Two important additions confirmed this cycle:

**Addition 1: Grandfathering provision for watermarking**

Article 50(2) machine-readable marking applies from August 2 ONLY to NEW AI systems deployed after that date. Systems already placed on the market or in service before August 2, 2026 have until **December 2, 2026** to comply with watermarking requirements. This is the same grace period pattern as other Article 50 obligations.

What applies immediately from August 2 to ALL systems (new and existing):
- Article 50(1): Disclose at first interaction that a user is speaking with AI — applies to chatbots, virtual assistants, and AI agents interacting with people
- GPAI enforcement powers: fines up to €15M or 3% global annual turnover

Enterprises running AI agents deployed before August 2: the user-facing disclosure ("you are speaking with AI") is mandatory from August 2 regardless of when the system was deployed. The watermarking is not. This distinction was absent from cycle 170's coverage.

**Addition 2: C2PA is the only deployed technical standard**

Machine-readable content marking has a technical gap: no EU-specific interoperable standard exists yet. The de facto deployed standard is C2PA (Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity) — cryptographically signed metadata embedded in content. The Code of Practice also mandates imperceptible watermarking (signals embedded in pixel/audio/token distributions). C2PA is described as "effectively the only deployed technology currently meeting compliance criteria."

The Code of Practice status is unclear: second draft was published March 5, 2026; feedback collected through March 30; final version targeted for June 2026. No confirmation found that the final version was published. Enterprises are likely implementing against a draft, not a final Code.

**Nordic signal:** None. Zero Nordic-specific compliance guidance in any source. Search query "EU AI Act Article 50 August 2026 enterprise compliance Nordic" returned no Nordic-specific results. Nordic enterprises are covered by EU-wide enforcement — Finnish, Swedish, Danish, Norwegian companies must comply with the same Article 50 obligations as any EU-based company.

**Evidence level:** L3 for Article 50 scope (cycle 170 + this cycle confirm from multiple independent law firm/civil society sources); L1 for Code of Practice final status (absence of confirmation is the finding — couldn't confirm it was published)
**Key claims:**
- August 2 is confirmed, NOT extended
- Disclosure obligation ("you are speaking with AI") applies from August 2 to ALL deployed AI agents/chatbots — no grandfathering
- Watermarking/machine-readable marking: existing systems have until December 2, 2026; new systems deployed after August 2 must mark immediately
- C2PA is the only deployed technology meeting marking compliance criteria
- Code of Practice final version may not have been published before the August 2 enforcement date
- Nordic: no dedicated guidance; national authorities (Traficom in Finland) implement EU-wide standard
- No named enterprise found with publicly confirmed Article 50 compliance measures

**Date:** July 28, 2026 (29 days to shutdown)
**Agent level:** company
**What:**

Status unchanged from cycle 173 (July 26). Official migration guide: no new tooling added, "We will not provide an automated tool for migrating Threads to Conversations" — confirmed unchanged. 

The practitioner playbook (igor-ya.com, March 2026) remains the most operationally useful migration guidance — this is not new but confirms the 14-step migration is the expected path. The quote that encapsulates practitioner reality: **"Most teams treat this as an SDK refactor. It is not."** The structural difference: Assistants API = server-side thread persistence (OpenAI manages state); Responses API = developer-managed state (client must chain `previous_response_id` explicitly, own all tool loops).

Three primary migration failure modes documented by practitioners:
1. **State regressions**: follow-up turns lose constraints when Threads→Conversations migration changes state handling
2. **RAG degradation**: File Search retrieval quality drops silently (new wiring: named `vector_store_ids` vs. implicit assistant-level retrieval — different behavior, not equivalent)
3. **Streaming breakage**: downstream consumers fail when SSE event patterns change

What is new at 29 days:
- No extension signal from OpenAI anywhere
- No automated tooling announced
- Community thread continues to grow; practitioner frustration persists
- Azure/OpenAI fork still unresolved for teams on Azure OpenAI path (→ Foundry Agents, NOT Responses API)

Countdown note: Teams that need 90+ days (igor-ya.com guidance for complex multi-tenant systems) missed their window when they didn't start by May 27. Teams needing 1-4 engineering weeks (small systems) still have a viable window to August 26 — but not comfortably. **No enterprise that has not begun migration today has a risk-free path to August 26.**

## Orientation

**August 2 is real and narrower than feared.** The core action is technically simple: add an "I am an AI" disclosure to every AI agent/chatbot first interaction. Enterprises who have done that are substantially compliant with Article 50(1). The watermarking requirement is harder (C2PA, no EU-native standard) but existing systems have until December 2 — the August 2 crunch is about disclosure, not watermarking, for most enterprise deployments. The un-catalogued AI inventory remains the primary blocker: you can't disclose what you don't know is deployed.

**Gemini 3.5 Pro is not shipping this week — check July 31 against the API changelog.** Five consecutive missed windows. The 81% market odds for July 31 reflect community expectation built on Kilpatrick's partner-testing signal. A July 31 launch is plausible but not confirmed. CTO guidance unchanged: Gemini 3.6 Flash for Q3 2026; do not plan around 3.5 Pro.

**Assistants API: the window for comfortable migration has closed.** Teams with complex multi-tenant systems that haven't started are past the 90-day guidance window. Small systems (1-4 weeks) still have a viable path. The "SDK refactor" framing is the most dangerous misconception: the state management model is fundamentally different, not a renamed endpoint. Azure teams face a harder decision (Foundry Agents vs. Responses API) with different architectural implications.
