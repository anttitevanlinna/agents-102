## Learning-set

### A. Generic in, generic out — the system is what you build around the model, and you steer it by shaping context, not by trusting the model
- One-line question the student carries out: *"What am I feeding it, and is that why the output is generic — or mine?"*
- Planted: M1, `lectures/context-is-king.md` — "Same words. Different answer... That's context." Payoff-in-miniature same module: `exercises/personal-site-with-guardrails.md` — "Generic output comes from generic context. The LLM didn't get better between Phase 1 and Phase 6. You did."
- Complicated (M2): context becomes persistent memory that sharpens rather than grows — `lectures/compounding.md`: "This is the same mechanism from Module 1: context shapes output, run at system scale... only the shelf life changed."
- Complicated (M3): the same memory is read/written by multiple concurrent agents — "The filesystem is the meeting room" (`multi-agent-systems.md` Big Idea).
- Complicated (M6): context/memory becomes fixed infrastructure the generator is scored against — "The judge has moved from object to yardstick" (`eval-loop.md`).
- Complicated (M8): the student stops writing context directly and starts describing gaps for an agent to build — "You become the describer; the agent becomes the builder" (`extend-your-system.md`).
- Governor: "name the framework, tune it, ask Claude to run it" (M1); "persistence + automation = system" (M2); "can I write one prompt that produces the same quality of output? If yes, you didn't need to split" (M3).
- Counter-voice: M1 Phase 5 — warm-session self-audit is "lovely about its own prior output," push for a cold read.
- Forward-look: implicit rather than an explicit open question at this stage; sharpens into an explicit question by M8.

### B. Confident output is not verified output — certainty is never available, you build a loop instead
- One-line claim the student carries out: *"Fluent doesn't mean true, and even my checks have blind spots — the discipline is the loop, not the feeling of being sure."*
- Planted: M3 Close, `exercises/three-minds-one-synthesis.md` — "is this actually right? You can't tell yet... Hold the doubt. Don't fix it here. Module 5 builds the tools to name what's off systematically."
- Complicated (M4): "I can't tell" becomes a first-class, acceptable verdict, and the answer is a repeatable loop, not a fix — `lectures/practice-of-risk.md`: "Certainty is a fantasy you inherited... You don't get certainty. You get a loop." Also directly ties to M1's self-grading worry: `lectures/module-5-prework.md` — "Asking the same model 'are you sure?' is not a check on the world; it is another fluent answer... Verification has to leave the generation loop and touch the source."
- Complicated (M5): "grounded" is distinguished from "accurate" — `lectures/grounded.md`: "Grounded means traceable to a real piece of evidence. Accuracy is a harder question, and an agent alone can't answer it." Partial, named-as-partial rescue: `output-quality.md` — "Your Module 4 residuals stay named, not solved... This module is the rescue. Not full closure."
- Complicated (M6): passing the check is not the same as being good — `lectures/when-the-score-stops-moving.md`: "The score went flat because the judge had nothing left to flag, not because the work had nothing left to improve... A grounded claim can still be vague, obvious, or useless, and this judge will pass it every time."
- Paid off (M8): the same discipline applied recursively to agent-to-agent communication — `exercises/joint-double-diamond.md`: "Without that rule, agents reading agents smoothly hallucinate each other's memory."
- Governor: Assess → Mitigate → Reassess residual → Decide, with "Accept the residual on record, or close the door... 'Hope it doesn't happen' is not one" (M4); candidates → benchmark → scorer → winner (M5); "a yardstick you rewrite is not a yardstick" (M6).
- Counter-voice: present at nearly every beat (see above) — this is the training's most self-doubting thread.
- Forward-look: genuine open question, not resolved — the training explicitly declines to close this loop even at the end of M8.

### C. Autonomy is earned rung by rung, not granted in one jump
- One-line claim the student carries out: *"More reach needs a clearer check and a smaller first step, not a bigger leap of trust."*
- Planted: M2, `lectures/first-scheduled-agent.md` — "Context tells the agent what world it is in. The guardrail tells it what not to do. The schedule makes it come back."
- Complicated (M3): the boundary widens from writing files to acting on other systems — `lectures/agent-that-takes-action.md`: "Writing a file changes your folder. Sending to Slack changes another system. Same agent, same context, same schedule, one new boundary."
- Complicated (M4): scope/split/filter/gate/review as concrete mitigations layered on the boundary — `lectures/practice-of-risk.md`.
- Formalized (M5): the autonomy ladder, six named rungs, `what-is-an-agent.md#the-autonomy-ladder` — "The move is not to jump from rung 1 to rung 6. The move is to earn the next rung."
- Paid off (M6): a concrete, personal decision — `lectures/new-human-role-in-the-loop.md`: "Would you let it send the mail?... The question is: have you designed the loop that would make sending it responsible?"
- Paid off again (M8): the human is explicitly placed at the decision layer of a room-scale agent forum — "The agents produced the options. The human picks. That's the thesis."
- Governor: propose → check → apply (M5); rung-by-rung earning test (M5/M6).
- Counter-voice: less explicit doubt-of-self here; mostly a disciplined ratchet rather than a doubted claim.
- Forward-look: genuine question ("would you let it...") rather than a prediction.

### D. You can't share an agent — you share a job done, and absorption (not access) is the hard part
- One-line claim the student carries out: *"The teammate doesn't want my agent, they want their job done — pick the smallest shareable form that moves their outcome, then test whether they'd actually switch."*
- Planted and largely self-contained in M7: `personal-to-team.md` — "You can't really share an agent. You can share context, a skill, the output, or an interface. The hard part is not access; it is absorption."
- Complicated once, at room scale, in M8: individual sharing becomes many agents in a governed forum, with a human still deciding — `joint-double-diamond.md`.
- Governor: "interview for the outcome. Pick the candidate against that outcome... Test the switch, not the solution."
- Counter-voice: built into the exercise mechanics rather than stated as lecture prose — e.g., "If the reason is merely 'we already have the platform,' send it back."
- Forward-look: the M7 pre-mortem ("six months from now, the teammate went back to the incumbent") is a genuine diagnostic question, not a prediction.
- Weaker depth than A/B/C: mostly one module's worth of development plus a single room-scale complication in M8, rather than a beat in nearly every module.

## Audience fit

The learning-set belongs to a builder leader (CEO/CTO/SVP), not a software-engineer IC. The challenge students bring is explicitly organisational and strategic — `exercises/name-your-challenge.md`: "Good: *The pricing redesign we're shipping in Q2*... *Our EU market entry*... *Rolling out agentic ways of working to 300 engineers*." The frameworks used throughout are leadership/strategy tools (Rumelt's strategy kernel, Roger Martin's assumption-test, Klein's pre-mortem, Jobs-to-be-Done, residual risk/ISO 31000) applied to the student's own real challenge, not to a code review. M7's "share the whole agent is a vendor pitch and is not on the list" and M8's room-scale strategy forum with a CTO/sponsor both assume the reader owns organisational decisions, not just code. The mechanics (plan mode, subagent launch phrasing, four-terminal-window setup) are IC-shaped in *texture* but are consistently framed as the leader's own hands-on practice ("your own live challenge"), not as a coding skill for its own sake — consistent with the training's stated goal of felt experience over vocabulary. Verdict: fits the gate.

## Weakest factor

Earned, not announced is the factor with the thinnest evidence, because the read surfaced a real, structural counter-example rather than an isolated slip: every one of the nine module files (`prework.md` excepted) opens with a "## Big Idea" line stating the module's thesis in full before the student has done anything that session — e.g. Module 4 tells the student "You can't tell if your agent is safe by looking at its output. You need a way to check" before the security demo or either exercise has run. This is not fatal (the exercises themselves reliably name after doing, and the Key Concepts sections at the close re-earn the same idea through evidence), but it means the module-level frame is announced, then earned twice — once by assertion, once by the work. The smallest concrete fix: replace the top-of-file "Big Idea" line with a question or an unresolved tension in the student's own words (mirroring what "Bring to Module N" sections already do at the *end* of files, e.g. "What do you expect will get lost between them?" in M3's opener), and move the flat declarative thesis to the Key Concepts section where the training already places it after the work is done.
