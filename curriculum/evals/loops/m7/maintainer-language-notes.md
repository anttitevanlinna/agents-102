# Maintainer's language notes (calls page answers)

Patterns the maintainer named while answering blind pairs and sentence cards. Each line: the pattern, then his example. Generators read this file; judges cite it.

1. Test for every sentence: does it help the student execute the learning on their own? If not, cut it.
2. No narration of what the student now has, did or felt ("You now have the teammate's job…", "You felt this an hour ago"). Narration adds no value.
3. No forward narration about later modules unless the current module needs it.
4. A "no / not" sentence that restates the point just made adds nothing ("A plan with no wall in it was written about a laptop").
5. An added sentence weaker than the one before it is fluff. Clear cut.
6. Short paragraphs a reader can take in at a glance. A long paragraph loses to the same content split.
7. First sentence of a slide must be clear on its own ("Note what's not on the list" was unclear).
8. No pronoun without a clear referent ("it"). No invented concepts ("agent file" is not a known concept). Say "option", not "shape".
9. Claims must match how agents work in Claude Code. The agentic act is the agent doing the work from context; "only what someone wrote down" frames it as people writing text for the agent, which is wrong. "You steer your agent with context."
10. No borrowed metaphors that don't belong ("lands", "muscle").
11. Never assume the student would skip work they chose ("actually read it"); no invented time anchors ("on Monday"). Instead tell them how to spot the right result (e.g. how to spot a report narrow enough).
12. Don't motivate by threat of the next module ("Come to Module 5 without… and you'll be reconstructing"). If a comparison is needed, compare with other students.
13. Not our style: "The story works…", "Modules add…". Prompts the student copies go in a prompt block, and we don't teach typing.
14. Correct domain claims: classic security is a risk-based approach.
15. Session start is always the Session widget (`**Session** *(verb, "name")*`, per training-architecture.md), never a body sentence like "Start a fresh session at…".
16. Scar story 1 closes: "Companies, even when they go agentic, work first and foremost on the human principle. Today, at least. Who knows about the future."

Overall rating 2026-11-01: Agents 101 language about 3 of 5, with bad language well outside the pairs shown.
17. Glanceable. The main training UX is Claude Code plus the prompts; the handbook is a secondary map of what to do next. Valid writing is still too wordy: compact, then compact again, as AE101 was. Yardstick: AE101 exercise slides median 66 words of prose (Agents 101: 109).
18. A closing sentence that adds little once the point is made goes.
19. No human verbs for what people grant agents ("Agents don't 'earn' autonomy").
20. A metaphor must fit its noun: "map is not a place to hang nor a container. Map is map."
21. A card without enough context gets "Neither": calls need the surrounding slide, not just the changed span.
22. Concrete sentences (2026-11-01, settled as "depends"): removing a concrete action can delete a real step ("A is removing callout of prework"), and keeping it at full length is too wordy ("B is too wordy"). Keep the action, say it in the fewest words. Not a hard alert on the judge.
23. Split a long sentence at its comma when the two halves are separate claims.
24. Stop saying "ship": "There is no shipping. We just have all the time better main."
