# Publishing workflow and boundaries

## Normal path

1. Salla chooses a topic from reader questions or her book notes.
2. She gathers public sources and writes a short position in her own words.
3. Claude may propose an outline, counterarguments, or a rough first draft.
4. Salla checks every factual and professional claim against the source.
5. She rewrites for her voice and records the final source links.
6. She publishes manually.

## Current evidence

The Google Doc version history usually shows several human edits after the AI
draft. There is no consistent checklist or retained approval note. Social posts
sometimes move from a newsletter draft to publication in one sitting, so the
evidence of editorial control is weaker even when Salla did edit them.

## Standing boundary

Public education is not individual psychological advice. Content must not imply
that a reader has a diagnosis, and it must not present a general exercise as a
substitute for care. The AI assistant must never receive client material.

## Open decision

Salla sometimes adds “drafted with AI assistance” because she considers it
transparent. She has not decided whether to use that notice consistently or
which cases are legal obligations rather than voluntary practice choices.
