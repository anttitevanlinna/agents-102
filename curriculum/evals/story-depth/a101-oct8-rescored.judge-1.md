## Frame

**A (94).** Frame: *agentic engineering is engineering* — a session is a feedback-control system, trust comes from checks, not from watching. Every module reads through it:
- M1 the-machine-you-just-met: "A check from outside the session resets the chain. A failing test does not care how confident the answer sounded."
- M3 the-loop-half-filled: "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller**. ... Cut the feedback signal (a test, a check, a read) and it drifts."
- M5 what-packaging-is: "**A check is a position fix**. At a fix the wedge of possible states collapses to a point."
- Where it breaks, on the page: M5 the-gate-is-a-claim: "Today's right procedure, your gates and workflow, yours or the agent's, is superseded too"; M6 agents-that-build-agents: "The goal is giving the system the right information, and nobody has that part figured out yet."
Clears 80 and names where it breaks; it misses 100 only because the far-half modules lean on borrowed laws (Goodhart, Deming) more than on the lens itself.

**B (83).** Frame: *the full agent picture* — context shapes output, and the leader adds one part at a time (context → memory → other agents → boundary → check → loop → interface) to an agent that stays theirs. It is stated in nearly every module and carries weight:
- M1 context-is-king: "In the full agent picture, this is the first piece: context. Later you add tools, goals, checks, boundaries, and loops."
- M2 compounding: "This is the same mechanism from Module 1: context shapes output, run at system scale."
- M4 practice-of-risk: "In the full agent picture, this is the boundary piece."
- M7 access-is-not-absorption: "In the full agent picture, this is the interface piece. It is the first one that has another person on the other side of it."
- Where it breaks: M4 practice-of-risk: "Everything you have built so far gave the agent more. ... Now the move is to give it less"; M8 where-is-this-all-going: "Which half of this is obsolete next year?"
It is load-bearing, and it names where it breaks. Two things cap it: it arrives as a repeated formula ("In the full agent picture, this is…") rather than a lens the student uses on their own, and M3's slot is thin ("other agents are part of the tool surface").

## Narrative

**A (94).** The five-sentence version: an engineer learns to steer a machine that agrees with them. They push a long task out un-packaged, and it comes back drifting. They read the return, package the task and send it again. Diffing the two runs, they cut a rule that didn't work. Then the author shows the same failure happening to himself. The turn is lived: M4 set-the-markers-send-it-off: "send it off plainly and you find out what you can't steer yet"; M5 reading-the-return: "The closing summary is not the artefact." The 100 anchor is met: the turn is the training's own failure, in M6 story-of-module-6: "The agent I was working with had just finished writing those three modules. It still opened with the un-packaged shape."

**B (85).** A leader makes a site that sounds like them, then builds a memory that sharpens itself. Three agents and three stances give an answer they "cannot vouch for." Security takes certainty away, and a benchmark gives back one judge they can defend. The loop then runs without them, and they find that what they learned is the one thing that won't hand over. The turn is lived in an exercise:
- M3 three-minds-one-synthesis: "Everything you just did is the move this training teaches, run properly. It still handed you something you cannot vouch for. Hold the doubt."
- M4 audit-your-agent: "The unease that remains is what the loop is supposed to produce, not what it failed to remove."
- M8 where-is-this-all-going turns the doubt on the training's own move: "The kernel came out of the same move as your first briefing: agents read, agents argued, something chose, and nothing in the room checked it."
Points lost: the rescue is announced before it is lived (M5 module Start here: "This module is the rescue."), and the end is named by the sponsor rather than reached by the student (M8 joint-double-diamond: "You are now agent builders").

## Point of view

**A (93).** Who tells it: Antti, a practitioner who built this training with the same agent and failed at it on the page. In M6 story-of-module-6: "Five taste reversals from me on Claude's confident recommendations"; "I drifted in every one of the ways this story just walked"; "The loop caught what I missed. A senior-persona sim caught what the loop missed." The narrator also shows up earlier, in M2 when-a-plan-is-good: "Read it assuming something in there is wrong; there usually is." The narrator's own failure is on the page, which is the 100 anchor. It sits in one late passage, so it does not reach 100.

**B (58).** The training is in second person throughout. A "we" surfaces in a few lines and never in a passage with a stated experience:
- M1 what-just-happened: "Nobody knows where agents are going. Not Anthropic, not your board, not the people running this training."
- M4 practice-of-risk: "Damn, this is complex stuff. It will still be complex when the next agent gets built."
- M8 agents-building-agents Next: "We could have spent today building you one impressive agent to show on Friday. It would have demoed well and it would have been the wrong thing to sell you."
That is above the 40 anchor (a voice in asides): the M8 closer has a position and a seller's self-awareness. It is short of 80, because no narrator says what they went through, and no failure of the narrator's own appears anywhere on the student surface.

## Stance

**A (92).** Positions a customer could walk away over, each defended:
- "Steer it as hard as you like: what it holds about your next run is a forecast." (M5 what-packaging-is), defended by "The missing evidence is local."
- "A rule in context is not a rule in the output." (M6 story-of-module-6), defended by a scar: four banned-word leaks across four LLM instances.
- "Green is a claim about the check, not a fact about the work." (M5 the-gate-is-a-claim), defended with Goodhart and a hold-out mechanism.
- "The agent yields if you push hard enough, so its agreement settles nothing." (M1 fix-tests-first).
No position on the page is held against the training's own commercial interest.

**B (89).**
- "*Share the whole agent* is a vendor pitch and is NOT on the list." (M7 share-your-work), defended by mechanism in access-is-not-absorption: "An agent is context, a boundary, a set of checks and somebody who answers for what it does. The first three copy in an afternoon. The fourth does not copy at all."
- "Certainty is a fantasy you inherited" (M4 practice-of-risk), defended by three mechanisms: non-determinism, the instruction set as attack surface, emergent capability.
- "Start with don't. ... Default to one agent with a good prompt." (M3 when-to-split-an-agent), defended by the "one prompt" test.
- Against its own commercial interest, on the page: M4 practice-of-risk: "It costs whoever sells you agents too, this training included"; M8 Next: "the wrong thing to sell you".
B has what A lacks, the anti-commercial line. It loses points because several positions rest on the word "plain" rather than on a scar ("It's plain design"; "the most plain thing in the room"), and because "hire" framing (M3 Big Idea "Hire three agents to search") cuts against the training's stated anti-hype stance.

## The pair

**A** reads as someone who has been there. Self-challenge: "The check you built is itself a claim that wants verifying" (M5 the-gate-is-a-claim). Stance holds alongside it ("A prediction is not a measurement").
**B** also reads as someone who has been there, but the voice is thinner. It doubts its own tools ("It is still nobody's job to grade the judge", M6 when-the-score-stops-moving; "nothing in the room checked it", M8) and holds ground ("vendor pitch", "this training included"). With no narrator, the doubt reads as the training's doubt, not someone's lived doubt. That is the POV gap again.

## Room (B only)

The exercises carry the turn. Joy, unease and rescue are lived in exercise beats, and the lectures name them afterwards ("You just did something a chat can't do", M2 compounding; "You have run this twice now without calling it anything", M6 new-human-role-in-the-loop).

**Strongest two beats**
1. M3 three-minds-one-synthesis, the close: "Three retrievers read plainly, three stances pushed, a framework held the synthesis together, and still, the answer sits at that uneasy distance where you'd stake your reputation on some of it and not all of it." The unease turn happens to the student, at their own desk, on their own challenge.
2. M2 build-your-challenge-memory Phase 3: "Second batch made the first batch better. Chat literally cannot do this." The student sees compounding before the compounding lecture names it.
(Close third: M7 design-the-sharing-plan: "A polished technical file beside a people file full of unnamed roles is the finding.")

**Weakest two beats**
1. M5 hallucination-bakeoff: "Your job is to set the benchmark up, start the run, and watch the scoreboard fill in"; "Twenty minutes of that was watching agents work." The rescue is watched, not done. The student never sets a verdict of their own beside the scorer, so M6's "nothing has been scoring the judge" lands as news rather than recognition.
2. M8 joint-double-diamond, the identity close: the sponsor reads a scripted line, *"You are now agent builders."* The hero's arrival is told to them by someone else. Next weakest: M8 extend-your-system asserts the compounding ("The second agent next week will take ten") instead of letting the student time it.
