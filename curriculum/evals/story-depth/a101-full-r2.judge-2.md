## Frame

**A — AE101: 93.** Frame in one sentence: *agentic engineering is engineering — feedback control, verification and compounding applied to a stochastic collaborator.* Modules that only make sense through it:
- M2 when-a-plan-is-good: "Push reach past what you can check and you have not delegated more. You are checking less." Plan reading is a calibration move, not a prompting trick.
- M3 the-loop-half-filled: "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller**... Cut the feedback signal (a test, a check, a read) and it drifts. Signal quality is part of the law: a flaky test is a closed loop that still drifts."
- M5 what-packaging-is: "**A check is a position fix**. At a fix the wedge of possible states collapses to a point."
- Where it breaks, on the page: M5 the-gate-is-a-claim "Sutton's **bitter lesson**... Today's right procedure, your gates and workflow, yours or the agent's, is superseded too"; M2 how-instructions-grow "This training stops short of the full system." Docked from 100 because M1–M2 carry long tooling stretches (rule-location table, /context) that read as reference, not as cases of the lens.

**B — A101: 80.** Frame in one sentence: *generic AI becomes your AI when you shape what surrounds it — the agent assembled one piece at a time (context, memory, other agents, boundary, check, loop, interface), each piece a decision about what you give it and what you keep back.* It is stated (M2 compounding: "*generic AI becomes your AI when you shape the context that surrounds it.*") and re-tagged in nearly every module:
- M2 compounding: "In the full agent picture, Module 2 adds shelf life."
- M4 practice-of-risk: "In the full agent picture, this is the boundary piece... More capability without boundaries is not progress. It is just more blast radius."
- M7 access-is-not-absorption: "In the full agent picture, this is the interface piece. It is the first one that has another person on the other side of it."
- Paid off M6 new-human-role: "You added those parts one at a time, and each one was a single decision: what you give it, and what you keep back." Where it breaks: M7 "The one piece you don't decide"; M8 "Which half of this is obsolete next year?... Nobody in this room knows yet."
Not higher because the "full agent picture" lines are a repeated tag, often one paragraph bolted to the end of a lecture, rather than a lens that changes how the module's exercise reads; M3's and M5's exercises would read the same without it.

## Narrative

**A: 90.** Five sentences: An engineer who already uses Claude Code wants to hand off real work. They learn the loop on small things — a bug, a plan, a skill — and every check returns in minutes. Then they send a task off un-packaged and walk away, and it comes back confident and partly wrong. They read the return through three failure lenses, package the same task and send it again, and learn the gate itself is a claim. The turn: the author shows the same failure in the making of the training — "Claude opened the session with a plan... Chat-shaped in the exact way M5 teaches you to notice... The agent I was working with had just finished writing those three modules. It still opened with the un-packaged shape." (M6 story-of-module-6). Lived turn: M4 set-the-markers-send-it-off "send it off plainly and you find out what you can't steer yet"; M5 what-packaging-is "An unchecked session arrives confident, and wrong... The success report comes from the wrong harbor." Docked: M1–M3 are episodic and a topic sequence; the plot only starts at M4.

**B: 80.** Five sentences: A leader who uses chat wants output that is genuinely theirs, and gets it — a site their neighbour recognises. They build a memory and agents around a challenge they're paid to move, and it compounds. Then a properly run multi-agent synthesis hands them an answer they cannot vouch for, and the security module tells them certainty was never on offer. They measure their way to one judge they can defend, put it in a loop, and learn that the judge also needs judging. They try to hand the system to a colleague, discover that access is not absorption, and leave with a flywheel and a question instead of a graduation. The turn is lived inside an exercise: M3 three-minds-one-synthesis "Everything you just did is the move this training teaches, run properly. It still handed you something you cannot vouch for. Hold the doubt." Deepened: M4 practice-of-risk "The feeling doesn't go away. Not with more testing, not with a better policy, not with a certification." Paid back at the close: M8 where-is-this-all-going "The kernel came out of the same move as your first briefing... nothing in the room checked it. Hold that doubt the way you held it then." Docked: the turn is announced ahead of time — M3 exercise "Module 5 builds the tools to name what's off systematically"; M5 module "This module is the rescue." — and in three modules (M4, M5, M6) the lecture runs before the exercise, so the turn is told before it is lived. It is never the training's own failure.

**B: 52.** Who is speaking: an unnamed trainer-practitioner in the second person who never tells you what they have been through. The narrator shows in lines:

Every failure on the page belongs to someone else (Schwartz in Mata v. Avianca, Deloitte — M4 module-5-prework) or to the student. There is no "I", no stated experience, no own failure. That is a voice in asides with conviction, just above the 40 rung.

## Room (B only)

Do the exercises carry the story? **Partly.** The joy beat (M1), the unease beat (M3) and the absorption beat (M7) are lived inside exercises and named afterwards. The deeper-unease, rescue and loop beats (M4–M6) are told by a lecture first and then run as procedure.

**Strongest beats**
1. **M1 personal-site-with-guardrails, Phase 1 → Phase 6.** "The baseline is boring on purpose. You need it boring so you can feel how much the later phases change." → "Iterate until looking at the screen makes you say *'yes, this is me.'*" → "The LLM didn't get better between Phase 1 and Phase 6. You did." The hero's first change happens with their own name on it, and the lecture after names it ("You felt context move" — what-just-happened).

It still handed you something you cannot vouch for. Hold the doubt. Name it to yourself. Don't fix it here." The plot turns inside an exercise, on the student's own crux.
(Runner-up: M7 design-the-sharing-plan, "A polished technical file beside a people file full of unnamed roles is the finding, not a finished rollout", then named after the fact in the lecture: "You felt this an hour ago... The people plan stalled on names.")

**Weakest beats**
1. **M6 eval-loop.** The result is scripted before it happens: "You will come back to a short run note: *round 1 flagged 11 claims, round 2 flagged 6...*". The hero is then sent away ("Now step away"), and the lecture evals-as-steering had already explained the loop. The beat that should be "the loop learned while I was gone" is told three times and lived zero.
2. **M8 joint-double-diamond, identity close.** "the sponsor... names what just happened, in one sentence, out loud: *'You are now agent builders...'* No certificate. No exercise." The hero's transformation is conferred by someone else's script, not recognised by the hero from their own files. (Runner-up: M4 author-security-skill is mostly runtime mechanics, "Press Save when skill-creator surfaces it", after practice-of-risk has already told the student what to feel.)
