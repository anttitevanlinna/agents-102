## Frame

**A — Agentic Engineering 101.** Frame: agentic engineering is engineering — a closed-loop controller whose quality is set by the checks outside it, compounding through what you write down. Every module reads through it.
- M1, the-machine-you-just-met: "A check from outside the session resets the chain. A failing test does not care how confident the answer sounded."
- M3, the-loop-half-filled: "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller**... Cut the feedback signal (a test, a check, a read) and it drifts. Signal quality is part of the law: a flaky test is a closed loop that still drifts."
- M4, ironies-of-automation: "The better the automation, the less you do the task by hand, and the worse you are at the moment you are needed most."
- M5, what-packaging-is: "**A check is a position fix**. At a fix the wedge of possible states collapses to a point."
- Where it breaks, M5, the-gate-is-a-claim: "Sutton's **bitter lesson**: built-in human knowledge wins today and loses to the next model. Today's right procedure, your gates and workflow... is superseded too." And M5, what-packaging-is: "today's playbooks are **candidates**."
Score 95: load-bearing in all six modules, and it names its own expiry.

**B — Agentic Product Teams 101.** Frame: building got cheap, deciding didn't. The team's work moves to choosing the outcome, placing the bet and reading the signal, around one loop (Outcome · Opportunities · Bet · Slice · Signal).
- M1, apt101-work-backwards: "Agents make the slice cheap. The loop can now turn in days, and the hard part moves to the decisions: choosing the outcome, hearing the opportunity, placing the bet, reading the signal even when it says no."
- M1, apt101-write-the-bet: "Agents make much of the building cheap, which shrinks the feasibility work. They do not settle the other three."
- M2, apt101-your-taste-is-the-ceiling: "Making got cheap. Knowing what good looks like did not."
- M3, apt101-slice-by-learning: "Specifying well is the harder half".
- Where it breaks, M3, apt101-what-each-of-us-is-for: "This is where 'building got cheap, deciding didn't' stops being an edge on its own. When rivals build as cheaply, speed is the entry price... What would change this training's mind: agents that start deciding well".
Score 84. It is stated, applied in each module and broken on the page, which is the 100-anchor clause. It is held back by M2's middle (fluent-is-not-true, the-check-that-found-them, what-the-agents-may-keep, start-with-dont). Those slides read through a grounding/risk lens carried over from another training ("In Mata v. Avianca, asking ChatGPT whether Varghese was real"; "A Confluence retriever"), not through deciding. For about a third of M2 the frame goes quiet.

## Narrative

**A.** Five sentences: An engineer learns that the machine mirrors them, and that a check outside the session resets a chain of mostly-right steps. They codify rules, plan with gates and author skills on the near half of the map, where feedback is quick. Then they send a task off un-packaged, and it comes back confident and partly wrong. The turn: they read the failure through three lenses, build a verifier against it and re-send the same task packaged. Then they diff the two runs and learn that the trainer's own session drifted the same way. The turn, M5 diagnose-and-resend: "The un-packaged run was supposed to underdeliver. What came back is data, not blame." Staging, M4 test-and-learn: "Session one goes now, un-packaged... Session two goes packaged, after you've read the return." The sea passage, M5 what-packaging-is: "An unchecked session arrives confident, and wrong... The success report comes from the wrong harbor." The training's own failure, M6 story-of-module-6: "The agent I was working with had just finished writing those three modules. It still opened with the un-packaged shape."
Score 93: the student lives the turn, and the training's own failure closes it.

**B.** Five sentences: A product trio learns that building is cheap and deciding isn't, writes a bet that can lose, and sets an agent to write an overnight digest from their customer material. The digest comes back wrong in places, a stretched claim or a smoothed disagreement, and the turn reframes that as the discovery. They write down what good means and learn that their own check is a claim that can be gamed. A slice comes back, and they decide on the agreed signal even when it says no. Taking it to the wider team proves harder than building it, and the frame admits where it stops holding. The turn, M2 apt101-when-the-run-went-wrong: "Expect an agent's first digest of your customer material to be wrong in places. That is the check working early, on something cheap." The thread carried into M3, apt101-a-check-is-a-claim: "'customers are unhappy' fails when one customer complained" (it echoes M2's "customers are unhappy with onboarding, and the only ticket behind it is one customer's complaint"). The second turn, M3 apt101-read-the-signal: "A slice that came back no did its job."
Score 62. A real thread with a turn now exists, but on this surface the turn is predicted ("Expect...") rather than staged as the trio's own failure. The heading-level shape of each lecture still carries most of the movement. Nothing in the handbook is the training's own failure as a plot beat.

## Stance

**A.** Positions it could lose a customer over, each defended:
- "The report is a hypothesis to check, not ground truth" (M1, the-machine-you-just-met). Mechanism: preference tuning, "Agreeable answers won the second round."
- "Ask for best practice and that is what answers: a well-read average of what other people published about other repos... **A prediction is not a measurement.**" (M5, what-packaging-is). Mechanism: frozen cutoff, local evidence.
- "A rule in context is not a rule in the output" (M6, story-of-module-6). Scar: "Same rule, same rules file, same task, four separate violations across four independent LLM instances."
- "The check you built is itself a claim... A gate nobody has verified is a gate trusted on vibes." (M5, the-gate-is-a-claim). Mechanism: Goodhart and optimization pressure.
- "Rules have a ceiling... The cure is not better rules; it is where the rules live." (M2, how-instructions-grow).
Against its own commercial interest: it shows the training's own production leaking (story-of-module-6), and "This training stops short of the full system" (M2, how-instructions-grow), "The field wires kits more ways than one; no way has won" (M6, composing-the-workflow).
Score 92.

**B.** Positions:
- "Building got cheap, deciding didn't" / "The risk now is that agents make the factory faster: more features shipped, the same nothing learned." (M1, apt101-work-backwards). Defended by the installer scar.
- "It costs whoever sells you agents too, this training included: an agent that reaches less is a smaller thing to sell, and it is still the right call." (M1, apt101-before-anything-goes-in). Held against its own commercial interest, on the page.
- "This isn't a bug that gets patched in the next release. It's the shape of the technology. Later models will fabricate less; they won't stop." (M2, apt101-fluent-is-not-true). Mechanism: next likely word, not next true word.
- "Scope it down far enough and the system is safe and useless. Nobody hands you the number. You pick it, you write down what is left, and you sign for it." (M2, apt101-what-the-agents-may-keep).
- "A team that writes for its own criteria has rebuilt the feature factory with a dashboard." (M3, apt101-a-check-is-a-claim). Goodhart turned on its own instrument.
- "So a proposal to the wider team cannot promise 'everyone gets our agent'." (M3, apt101-from-us-to-the-team). Defended by the mechanism that the work lives in memory and corrections, not in the instructions.
Score 80. These are positions defended by a mechanism, and one is held against commercial interest. But a large share of the stance is borrowed and defended by its author's authority (Torres on who does discovery, Cagan on risks, Fitzpatrick on compliments), not by this training's own scar.
