## Frame

**A (AE101): agentic engineering is engineering, a closed feedback loop where a check from outside the session resets the drift.** Every module reads through it:
- M1 the-machine-you-just-met: "A check from outside the session resets the chain. A failing test does not care how confident the answer sounded."
- M3 the-loop-half-filled: "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller**. ... Cut the feedback signal (a test, a check, a read) and it drifts."
- M5 what-packaging-is: "**A check is a position fix**. At a fix the wedge of possible states collapses to a point".
- M5 the-gate-is-a-claim, where the frame breaks: "Green is a claim about the check, not a fact about the work." and "Today's right procedure, your gates and workflow, yours or the agent's, is superseded too."
The frame turns back on its own instrument (Goodhart, bitter lesson). It is close to 100 and only loses points because M2's rules-placement stretch is mechanics more than lens. **94.**

**B (APT101): building got cheap, deciding didn't. The trio's craft is the decisions agents can't make.** It is stated up front and named where it breaks, which reaches the 100-rung element:
- M1 apt101-work-backwards: "Agents make the slice cheap. The loop can now turn in days, and the hard part moves to the decisions".
- M1 apt101-write-the-bet: "Agents make much of the building cheap, which shrinks the feasibility work. They do not settle the other three."
- M2 apt101-opportunities-before-solutions: "With agents, three rough solutions cost an afternoon. Judging which opportunity matters still costs what it always did: listening to customers."
- M3 apt101-slice-by-learning: "Specifying what to build is now the harder half".
- M3 apt101-what-each-of-us-is-for: "This is where 'building got cheap, deciding didn't' stops being an edge on its own."
Not every module reads through it, though. context-is-king, a-memory-that-compounds, the M2 split/risk/fabrication block (start-with-dont, assess-then-mitigate, why-the-llm-fabricates) and the six-parts slides would read the same in any agent training. Large stretches are A101/AE101 transplants the lens never touches. It sits between the 40 and 80 rungs, lifted by the explicit break. **72.**

## Narrative

**A.** An engineer meets a machine that mirrors them. They learn to steer it in short loops, then send an un-packaged session off alone, and it comes back confident and wrong. They diagnose, package and re-send. Then the author shows the training itself being built and failing the same way. The turn is lived ("Session one goes now, un-packaged", M4 test-and-learn → "The un-packaged run was supposed to underdeliver. What came back is data, not blame", M5 diagnose-and-resend), and it is also the training's own failure: "The agent I was working with had just finished writing those three modules. It still opened with the un-packaged shape." (M6 story-of-module-6). Note that the manifest comment says story-of-module-6 is excluded, but it is in the list. **93.**

**B.** A trio that can now build cheaply wants to stop running a feature factory. They write the bet so it can lose and build an overnight digest agent. The digest comes back wrong, and that is reframed as the discovery. They learn to check, slice and read the signal, then hit the wall of sharing it with the wider team. The thread is real:
- M1 apt101-it-runs-overnight: "Some of what it writes will be wrong, and finding which part is the team's job."
- M2 apt101-when-the-run-went-wrong: "A digest that came back wrong is the discovery". "Expect an agent's first digest of your customer material to be wrong in places. That is the check working early, on something cheap."
- M3 apt101-read-the-signal: "A slice that came back no did its job."
- M3 apt101-from-us-to-the-team: "What three people worked out stays with three people until someone writes it where the team will find it".
The turn is announced in advance ("Expect...") rather than shown happening. The trio is addressed but never followed as a protagonist, and M2/M3 stretch into catalogue order (Nielsen → Krug → Ries). The training's own failure never becomes the plot. The installer and the fumbled vision are the narrator's failures, but they are cited, not plotted. It reaches past "a shape asserted in headings" without fully reaching "a turn the student lives through" on this surface. **58.**

## Point of view

**A.** Antti speaks, signed at the close: a practitioner who built this training with the agent and was caught out by it. The narrator shows in three places:
- M1 compound-and-close aside: "Everyone sees how this will bloat almost immediately."
- M2 how-instructions-grow: "This training stops short of the full system".
- M6 story-of-module-6, in full first person with his own failure: "Five taste reversals from me on Claude's confident recommendations", "I drifted in every one of the ways this story just walked. I fixed what I caught. The loop caught what I missed."
**95.**

**B.** The body voice is an impersonal, sourced second person. The narrator (Antti) arrives through quotation boxes in the third person, credited like Torres or Cagan. He shows in four places:
- M1 apt101-work-backwards: "Antti Tevanlinna, writing from inside an AI transformation ... 'Bad idea, as the need wasn't a daily one.'"
- M2 apt101-when-the-run-went-wrong: "I used to think of being wrong as failure. But being wrong does not diminish you as a person."
- M3 apt101-from-us-to-the-team: "We made an 80% ready vision, but I never pushed enough to put it really on paper and share."
- M3 apt101-what-each-of-us-is-for: the Red Queen quote.
His own failure is on the page, attested and verbatim, which is new and real. But he is a cited witness, not a teller. The surrounding prose never says "I" and never connects the quotes to how this training was made, so it reads as one more source in a well-researched deck. That is above "a voice in asides", short of "a narrator who tells". **60.**

## Stance

**A.** Positions it could lose a customer over, each defended:
- "Your stance is the ceiling" (M1 painting-the-picture), defended by mechanism in M1 the-machine-you-just-met: "Agreeable answers won the second round."
- "what it holds about your next run is a forecast" / "**A prediction is not a measurement.**" (M5 what-packaging-is), defended by local A/B.
- "A rule in context is not a rule in the output" (M6 story-of-module-6), defended by the scar of the banned word leaking four times.
- "Don't make general what you don't practice yourself." (M3 skills-from-the-frontier).
Against its own interest: it concedes its own limits ("This training stops short of the full system") but holds no position that costs the seller. **92.**

**B.** Positions it could lose a customer over:
- "Building got cheap, deciding didn't" (M1 apt101-work-backwards), defended by the installer scar and Cagan/Martin ("costs can be controlled; revenues cannot").
- "'Are you sure?' is another fluent answer" (M2 apt101-fluent-is-not-true), defended by mechanism: "Large language models generate the next likely word. Not the next true word". Also: "Later models will fabricate less; they won't stop."
- "An agent's instructions are not the agent" (M3 apt101-from-us-to-the-team), defended by mechanism: "The page was never where the work lived."
- "A team that writes for its own criteria has rebuilt the feature factory with a dashboard." (M3 apt101-a-check-is-a-claim)
- Against its own commercial interest, on the page: "It costs whoever sells you agents too, this training included: an agent that reaches less is a smaller thing to sell, and it is still the right call." (M1 apt101-before-anything-goes-in)
Many positions are carried by borrowed authority (Torres, Seiden, Cutler, Cagan, Nielsen) rather than held in the training's own voice, and the M2 risk/legal slides hedge ("safe enough, under these conditions, for now"; that hedge is a principled stance, but a soft one). The against-interest line is a 100-rung element, but it is a single sentence in an imported security slide, not a position the training argues. **78.**

## The pair

**A:** both. It doubts its own gates ("The gate is a claim too", "same-window self-charity") and holds its ground ("A rule in context is not a rule in the output"). It reads as someone who has been there.

**B:** both, leaning toward the well-read colleague rather than the veteran. The doubt is strong and well placed ("A pass is a claim about the check", "Run more than one check on your own material", "What would change this training's mind"). The ground it holds is mostly held through citations, with the narrator's scars quoted, not told. Not a hedge and not a pitch: the been-there voice is visible but borrowed from the narrator's essays rather than spoken by the training.
