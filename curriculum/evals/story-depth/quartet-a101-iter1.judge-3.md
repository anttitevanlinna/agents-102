## Frame

**One sentence:** Every capability this training teaches — a genuine voice, a durable memory, a division of labour, a scoped risk, a trustworthy claim, a self-improving loop, a handoff to a colleague, a room of agents arguing — is the same operation, shaping what surrounds the model, run at a different scale and with a different payload; and the training names, mid-arc, the one place that operation has to run backward.

Three modules that only make sense through it:
- M1: "Context is whatever you tell it... every useful thing in this training is built on this one idea" (`context-is-king.md`) — stated as the literal mechanism before any system exists.
- M2: "This is the same mechanism from Module 1: context shapes output, run at system scale" (`compounding.md`) — the frame is not just reused, it is named as reused.
- M6: "A yardstick you rewrite is not a yardstick" (`evaluations.md` Key Concepts) — feedback is context too, and the same fragility (garbage in, garbage compounds) applies at the judge layer.

Where it breaks, stated on the page: "Everything you have built so far gave the agent more. More context. A memory that survives the session. More stances at the table. Now the move is to give it less" (`practice-of-risk.md`). M4 is not an exception the frame ignores; the training stops and names the inversion, then keeps the same vocabulary ("Every door you close is a source you won't read") to describe the opposite move. That is the top anchor's move, not just a wobble.

## Narrative

**Five sentences, with the turn:** A student arrives wanting output that finally reads as theirs instead of statistically generic, and gets it, once, on a site built from their own LinkedIn and their own hated meetings. Encouraged, they build a real system around a real problem — a memory, three agents that search, three that argue, a synthesizer that decides — and at the exact moment it starts producing something they'd stake a meeting on, the training pulls the rug: *"Nothing here checks any of it. Nothing in it reads the briefing and tells you whether it is true"* (`multi-agent-systems.md`). They keep building anyway — scoping the system's access, benchmarking its claims, running a loop that sharpens itself while they're not watching — and each fix rescues one narrow thing while the maintainer's own mood contract insists the earlier doubts stay open ("Do NOT resolve M3's strategic uncertainty or M4's security residual," `output-quality.md`). Then, holding eight modules of exactly the kind of compounding the training sold them on, they try to hand it to a colleague and learn the punchline: *"You cannot really share an agent... The hard part is not access; it is absorption"* (`personal-to-team.md`) — the thing they spent six modules making theirs is, by the same logic, the thing that cannot travel. They close not graduated but mid-loop, told the polished capstone demo "would have been the wrong thing to sell you" (`agents-building-agents.md`).

The turn quoted above ("Nothing here checks any of it") is a real lived reversal — competence built in public, immediately shadowed by an admission the training's own machinery can't verify itself — but it is a limit named about the *system the student built*, not a first-person confession that the training's own pedagogy failed. That keeps it short of the top anchor.

**Is the narrator's own failure on the page?** No, and this iteration knows it isn't. Both candidate homes for a first-person scar are explicitly flagged unfilled in the maintainer layer: `practice-of-risk.md` — *"Scar slot, open for Antti... unfilled, and no placeholder ships in student text... Nobody drafts a stand-in"* — and `access-is-not-absorption.md`, which names the same fallback slot and the same refusal to fake it. The training substitutes a *structural* doubt (the model's self-report can't be trusted, named repeatedly: M1's cold read, M3's "the doubt stays," M6's "which rule did you claim to remove") for a *personal* one. That is real and repeated, but it is doubt about the tool, not the narrator's own lived failure.

## Stance

Positions the training could lose a customer over:

1. **Plain text over tooling, against a training company's own upsell instinct.** "Plain text beats databases here... No special tool. No paid tier. No setup... every fancier setup that promised to fix this added a layer that the model had to work around" (`compounding.md`). Defended by mechanism (what the model is actually strong at), not asserted.
2. **Refuses to let the audit framework grade itself.** STRIDE is named "historical context, NOT... the structuring rubric of the audit prompt. The audit names attack classes directly so the lens cannot collapse into closed-loop self-grading" (maintainer block, `security.md`) — a defensible but maintainer-side statement; the body's matching commitment is "I can't tell is a real answer. Most rows land here" (Key Concepts), which will read as evasive to a buyer who wants a clean pass/fail security story.
3. **You cannot share an agent, so don't buy the pitch that says you can.** "The product being sold as a shared agent is always three of the four parts, with the fourth still sitting with whoever built it. Accountability is the part with a name on it, and a name does not travel inside a folder" (`access-is-not-absorption.md`) — defended by mechanism (accountability requires a person), directly undercuts any platform-rollout sale.
4. **Held against the training's own commercial interest, and flagged by the maintainer as exactly that.** "We could have spent today building you one impressive agent to show on Friday... it would have been the wrong thing to sell you" (`agents-building-agents.md`); maintainer block: "This is the arc's single against-interest instance." The cleanest hit on the top anchor in the whole corpus.
5. **Refuses the "your own report is enough" comfort.** "Agreeable answers won the round the model was tuned on... The self-report is the same kind of output as the thing it is reporting on" (`what-just-happened.md`) — a position that makes every self-audit in the training suspect, including its own, defended by naming the RLHF mechanism rather than just asserting distrust.

## The pair

Stance and self-doubt run together throughout: nearly every strong position (plain text, STRIDE-avoidance, no-agent-sharing, against-interest close) sits beside an explicit refusal to call the matching problem solved (M3's held doubt, M4's named residual, M5's "not full closure," M6's self-audit challenge). It reads closer to **someone who has been there** than a hedge or a pitch — the doubt is specific, mechanism-named, and revisited rather than a single disclaimer, and the stances are defended rather than merely stated. What keeps it from the fullest version of "been there": the one first-person scar the design explicitly wanted (both candidate slots, M4 and M7) is on record as *not written* in this iteration, so the self-doubt is all structural ("the tool can't grade itself") and none of it is personal ("I built this and it went wrong on me").
