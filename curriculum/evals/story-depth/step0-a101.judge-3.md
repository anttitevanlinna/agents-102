### Learning 1 — "Trust the check, not the output" (you cannot inspect your way to safety or quality; you have to build and run a check, then let the check become the thing you trust)

The single most developed idea in the training. One-line claim the student carries out: *the discomfort of not knowing whether an agent's work is right or safe never goes away — you don't wait for certainty, you build a repeatable check and let it stand between you and the output.*

- **Planted:** M1, `getting-going.md` / `personal-site-with-guardrails.md` — the cold-critic move and the warning that self-grading is charitable: "Claude is reading its own work in the chat where it just wrote it... the LLM tends to be lovely about its own prior output," with an explicit forward pointer, "You'll meet this mechanism again in Module 5 when the stakes are higher."
- **Complicated, M2** (`compounding.md`) — citations become the check: "If it's generic, hand-wavy, or leans on uncited knowledge, you know exactly where the memory is still thin."
- **Complicated, M3** (`three-minds-one-synthesis.md`) — the idea is explicitly NOT resolved: "you can't yet say which is which. That feeling is correct. Hold the doubt. Name it to yourself. Don't fix it here. Module 5 builds the tools to name what's off systematically." Also complicated at the system level: multi-agent self-review is diplomatic by default (`multi-agent-systems.md`: "Diplomatic is the failure mode at a handoff seam").
- **Complicated, M4** (`practice-of-risk.md`) — formalized into a named discipline with no terminal state: "You don't get certainty. You get a loop" (assess → mitigate → reassess residual → decide); "'Hope it doesn't happen' is not one [option]."
- **Paid off with mechanism, M5** (`grounded.md`, `hallucination-bakeoff.md`) — an actual empirical method for building a check: "Don't pick a method. Run the candidates," four detectors scored by a scorer, winner saved as a judge file that must "name what it can't catch." Explicitly named as partial: "This module is the rescue. Not full closure."
- **Complicated again, M5** (`self-consistency-after-scoreboard.md`) — a plausible-looking second check is deliberately demoted immediately after being shown: "That is useful, but it is not the yardstick... Drift is a warning signal, not proof of fabrication."
- **Infrastructure payoff, M6** (`eval-loop.md`, `when-the-score-stops-moving.md`) — the check becomes a fixed yardstick a generator improves against without the human watching: "The judge has moved from object to yardstick... A yardstick you rewrite is not a yardstick." Immediately complicated again: "A flat score is real information, but only about the frame the judge can see... The judge told you what it could. The rest is yours to name."
- **Extended to a social register, M7** (`personal-to-team.md`) — the same "don't trust the surface signal, verify" logic reapplied to organizational adoption: "Access is not absorption," `UNASSIGNED` fields the agent is forbidden to invent, so the gap stays visible rather than papered over.
- **Final payoff, M8** (`agents-building-agents.md`, `joint-double-diamond.md`) — the human's role crystallizes as the check-of-last-resort: "The agents produced the options. The human picks... Don't let an agent pick the two or three assumptions to commit to." Citation discipline reapplied one more time to agent-to-agent claims: "agents reading agents smoothly hallucinate each other's memory" without a citation rule.
- **Governor:** varies by module but always concrete — cold-read prompts (M1), citation checks (M2), the four-step risk loop (M4), the candidates-then-benchmark method (M5), the SHA-integrity check on the judge file (M6), "send it back if the only reason is infrastructure fit" (M7), "the human picks, not the agent" (M8).
- **Counter-voice:** present at nearly every beat — this is the training's most self-doubting thread, and the doubt is aimed at the check mechanism itself by the end (M5's "known limit" line, M6's flat-score complication), not only at the raw agent output.
- **Forward-looking:** genuinely open at several points — M3 explicitly defers the answer rather than resolving it; M8 ends on "You do not graduate. You have a flywheel" and "Module 8 doesn't answer it" rather than a prediction.

### Learning 2 — "Context has shelf life, and shaping it is how generic becomes yours" (a single prompt's context → a durable memory → a shared organizational context)

One-line claim: *the difference between generic AI output and output that's actually useful is never the model getting smarter — it's how deliberately you shape and extend the context around it, and that context can be made to persist and travel.*

- **Planted, M1** (`context-is-king.md`, `personal-site-with-guardrails.md`) — same words, different context, different answer: "Change the front, change the back." Payoff within-module: "Generic output comes from generic context. The LLM didn't get better between Phase 1 and Phase 6. You did."
- **Complicated, M2** (`compounding.md`) — context gets shelf life: "Context stops being something trapped in one conversation. It becomes memory the agent can re-read, update, and carry into the next session... This is the same mechanism from Module 1: context shapes output, run at system scale."
- **Complicated, M7** (`personal-to-team.md`) — context becomes a shareable, travelling artifact, one of four sharing shapes ("Share the context — your memory/, sources/, CLAUDE.md... travel. Teammates build on top"), and the module's key tension (access vs. absorption) is really about whether shared context gets used, not just moved.
- **Governor:** M1's "name the framework, tune it, ask Claude to run it"; M2's "could a competitor write this?" specificity test.
- **Forward-looking:** weak on its own — mostly resolved rather than left open; it functions more as connective tissue for Learning 1 and Learning 3 than as its own suspended question.

### Learning 3 — "Systems compound: persistence + automation, and eventually the system builds the next system" (the flywheel)

One-line claim: *once you have something that both remembers and runs on its own, every cycle makes the next cycle better without you doing the improving by hand — and that same logic eventually applies to the builder itself.*

- **Planted, M2** (`compounding.md`) — "Persistence... Automation... Either alone is a toy... Put them together and you get something that keeps getting better the more you feed it." Demonstrated concretely: "The second batch made the first batch better. Chat literally cannot do this."
- **Complicated, M6** (`evals-as-steering.md`, `eval-loop.md`) — compounding gets a fixed reference point so it can be measured, not just felt: "The judge stays fixed. The generator changes... If the generator improved under the same judge, the loop worked."
- **Paid off, M8** (`agents-building-agents.md`, `extend-your-system.md`) — the compounding mechanism turns on the builder itself: "The tool that builds tools compounds"; "You become the describer; the agent becomes the builder."
- **Governor:** M2's "keep the rules small, keep them boring, and let the compounding do the work"; M8's "inherit your agents' existing rules, don't rediscover."
- **Counter-voice:** weaker than Learning 1's — mostly asserted and demonstrated rather than doubted, though M6's "flat score ≠ done" partially complicates the assumption that compounding always continues to matter on the axis you're measuring.
- **Forward-looking:** strong at the very end — M8 refuses to resolve "where is this all going," explicitly deferring rather than predicting.

- **The autonomy ladder as a named six-rung structure** — planted in `supplementary/what-is-an-agent.md` (dated for M5) and referenced again in M5/M6, but the six named rungs themselves are introduced once and never revisited with new complication; the *practice* of propose-then-check-then-act recurs (M3, M4, M6) but the ladder's specific vocabulary does not.
- **When to split an agent (three tests: access / dialect / stance)** — `when-to-split-an-agent.md`, M3. Strong governor and counter-voice within its own module ("A whole module just showed you multi-agent works. Next Monday, you will be tempted to apply it to everything. Don't"), and the underlying pattern is structurally reused later (M6 generator/judge separation, M8's room-scale forum), but the three-test vocabulary itself is never re-named or complicated again — treated here as a one-module idea, not a developing one.
- **Access is not absorption** — `personal-to-team.md`, M7. Fully developed within its own module (planted, tested via three sequential exercises, closed with a Monday action), but it does not recur or get complicated in M8; M8's "humans sit at the decision" is adjacent but is really a beat of Learning 1, not a continuation of this idea.
- **The compound-reliability arithmetic (85%^10 ≈ 20%; the flip-side 10%→1%→0.1%)** — `grounded.md`, M5. A vivid one-off illustration explicitly flagged in the text itself as "an illustrative number... The arithmetic is an illustration, not a measurement." Motivates the M5 benchmark but is not returned to.
- **Jobs-to-be-Done as an interview technique** — `share-your-work.md`, M7. Thoroughly applied within M7 but confined to that module; does not reappear or complicate elsewhere.

## Audience fit

Passes the gate. The challenges named throughout (pricing redesign, EU market entry, AI strategy for the next two quarters, rolling out agentic ways of working to 300 engineers) are explicitly executive-shaped, not IC-shaped, and the "bad" examples in `name-your-challenge.md` explicitly reject IC-scale or self-improvement framings ("Becoming a better leader," "Understanding AI"). M4's security module is framed as a business-judgment exercise, not a technical one ("The skill does the threat modelling. Your job is to read the report with judgment — that's business judgment, not security expertise"). M7's sharing plan requires a people plan with governance/ownership language recognisable to an SVP, not a deployment runbook. M8's capstone explicitly seats a CTO/sponsor as the room's decision-maker. The learnings (trust-the-check, context compounds, access≠absorption, humans-own-the-decision) are all decisions a builder leader owns, not implementation details an IC would own.

## Weakest factor

Forward-looking is the weakest of the five, not because the training lacks genuine open questions (M3's deferred doubt and M8's refusal to resolve "where is this all going" are both strong, real instances) but because at least one passage undercuts the pattern by stating a flat prediction rather than posing a question: `grounded.md`'s "Later models will fabricate less; they won't stop" asserts a specific trajectory for model capability rather than leaving it open, and it sits right next to material (the compound-reliability arithmetic) that is otherwise carefully hedged as illustrative rather than measured. The smallest concrete fix: reframe that one sentence into the same register the rest of the module already uses for its numbers — something like "Nobody knows how much less models will fabricate. Design as if some amount survives every version" — which converts a prediction into the same kind of held-open design stance the training uses everywhere else (M3's "hold the doubt," M6's "the rest is yours to name," M8's "Module 8 doesn't answer it").
