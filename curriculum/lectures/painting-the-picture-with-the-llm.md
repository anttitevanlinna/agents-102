# Painting the picture with the LLM

## The LLM mirrors your stance

The wizard typing in neat Perl syntax is dead. Auto-complete was a thing. Auto-complete is no longer a thing. A part of high-end engineering skill has been taken on by the LLM.

The LLM can do good work and bad work. It will be what fits the session. The LLM mirrors your stance.

The tool is not the thing; the taste behind the tool is the thing. Seth Godin has been making that argument about tools for decades. The LLM is the infinite version of it: your stance as input to the session is mirrored in the output.

It was trained to match you. Your stance is the ceiling.

## You prime, the LLM scales

Stance and approach matter more now, not less. The session bends to what sits in the context window. You prime it. The LLM scales it.

Whatever you put in front of the LLM is what the LLM scales. The rules file. The prompt. What sits on disk for the agent to read.

If you want a shotgun, you have a shotgun. If you want a cannon, you have that too. The sharp engineers stay sharp.

## Two frontiers: how fast, and the right things

How fast can your setup learn? The rules and context you put in front of the LLM: can that stack learn faster than a human practitioner can write things down?

Can it learn the right things? Once the setup can learn fast, how does it learn the right things, and not just any things?

Let's go.

**Stance** `[stance:2026-07-29 level:L2]`
- holds: at organisational and team level the amplifier framing is strong — three independent sources from three methodological starting points, plus the 15-source productivity-paradox convergence behind Pattern 50, plus DORA 2025 in its own words (*"AI's primary role is as an amplifier, magnifying an organization's existing strengths and weaknesses"*). AI reinforces the system it enters rather than transforming it.
- contested: **the level, and the axis — and neither side of the individual-level question is settled.** The org-level evidence is L3; the individual-engineer leg is not. Cui et al. (N=4,867 developers) and Brynjolfsson et al. (N=5,172 support agents) both find juniors gaining more — **but read the axis before reading the result.** Both measure *percentage gain against each worker's own baseline* on *bounded task volume*. That axis mechanically flatters a low baseline: 30% on a junior's throughput can be fewer absolute tasks than 5% on a senior's, and neither paper reports the absolute spread. **The generalisation does not follow from the finding** (`check_research_claims.md §12` — the axis, not only the population).
  The deeper limit is the mechanism the papers name themselves: the tool *disseminates the tacit knowledge of the more able workers* and moves newcomers down the experience curve. That is a **catch-up-to-known-best-practice** effect, and by construction it has the most room where the knowledge is missing. It measures who closes the gap to existing practice — **not who pulls ahead at the frontier**, which is what this lecture's line claims. Distributing known patterns through autocomplete and running agentic work at the edge are different games on different axes.
  On the other side sit Intercom's 6× top-5%-vs-median PR throughput (real, individual-level, but single-org, uncontrolled, measuring orchestration skill) and a broad practitioner convergence that the people who know their stuff pull furthest ahead — L1, not data, but from people who ship. **Neither side settles it.** The body states the koan flat and carries its own hedge by register — a paragraph written as belief, with slide 3's "neither has a settled answer" two lines later: **do not strengthen it, do not import the org-level L3 to prop it up, and do not cite the compression studies as if they refuted it.** Our own KB still conflates the levels: `insights.md` states the strong-teams-thrive form as thesis, `patterns.md` evidences it at org level, `theory-audit.md` flags the individual leg as contested. `theory-audit`'s "demote to hypothesis or split the legs" was right, and the split is org-level-holds / individual-level-open.
- decided: **the koan stands at belief strength and the engineer-population studies do not refute it, maintainer call 2026-07-29, re-affirmed 2026-08-14 when the verbal hedge came off.** Cui et al. (*Management Science*, N=4,867) find Copilot raises task completion for junior and recent hires but not for senior, long-tenure developers, which read narrowly inverts the lecture's line. Read carefully it does not: the axis is percentage-of-own-baseline on bounded task volume, which flatters a low baseline and reports no absolute spread, and the mechanism the authors name is disseminating able workers' tacit knowledge — catch-up to known practice, not who pulls ahead at the frontier. **Do not re-open this as "the evidence says juniors gain more" without carrying the axis with it.**
