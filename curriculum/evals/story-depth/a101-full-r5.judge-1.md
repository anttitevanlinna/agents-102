## Frame

**A.** *Agentic engineering is engineering*: control loops, checks from outside the session, and compounding evidence. Every module reads through that lens. M2 when-a-plan-is-good: "A plan is a check before implementation." M3 the-loop-half-filled: "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller**... Cut the feedback signal... and it drifts." M5 what-packaging-is: "**A check is a position fix**. At a fix the wedge of possible states collapses to a point." The frame also names where it breaks. M5 what-packaging-is: "Whether this field ever settles into a real best practice is an open question. Either way, today's playbooks are **candidates**." M5 the-gate-is-a-claim: "Today's right procedure, your gates and workflow... is superseded too." **94.**

**B.** *What you give the agent, and what you keep back, decides what it does for you.* The frame starts as context, scales into a system, and then inverts into boundary. M1 context-is-king: "That is context. You steer your agent with context." M2 compounding: "Context shapes output, at system scale." M4 practice-of-risk: "You have given the agent more: context, memory, more stances. Now give it less. More context makes a good answer likelier. Less access makes a bad answer smaller." M6 new-human-role-in-the-loop: "Each was one decision about what you give the agent and what you keep back." M7 says where it breaks (access-is-not-absorption): "Accountability is the part with a name on it, and a name does not travel inside a folder." The frame is load-bearing in M1, M2, M4, M6 and M7. It is thinner in M3, where the frame is divide-and-hand-off rather than give/keep, and in M8. Breaking at absorption is implied, not stated as a limit of the lens. **82.**

## Narrative

**A.** You arrive steering short loops. You learn plans are plausible, not good. You send a long task off un-packaged and it underdelivers by design (M4 test-and-learn: "Session one goes now, un-packaged"). You read the return, package it, and re-send. Then the narrator shows that the training's own author drifted while building it. The turn is lived, and the last turn is the training's own failure. M6 story-of-module-6: "The agent I was working with had just finished writing those three modules. It still opened with the un-packaged shape." M4 set-the-markers-send-it-off: "send it off plainly and you find out what you can't steer yet." M5 reading-the-return: "The closing summary is not the artefact." **93.**

**B.** A leader who uses chat makes a site that is "yes, this is me". They build a memory that compounds, then split the work across six agents and end with an answer they can't trust (M3 three-minds-one-synthesis: "You can't yet tell if the answer is right. That is expected. Name the doubt; don't fix it here."). Security deepens the unease (M4 audit-your-agent: "The risk remains. That is expected. Name it, accept it on record, and stop there."). The rescue lands in the exercise, on the very artefact that was doubted (M5 hallucination-bakeoff: "run it once on your `./crux.md` answer, the one Module 3 left you doubting"). Then the loop runs without them, the work is shared, and it turns into a flywheel. The plot is designed and mostly lived. The narrator's own failure appears twice: M6 "I drifted too" and the M7 non-decision. Neither is the *training's* failure turned into the turn the way AE101 M6 is. The M8 close is told by a scripted line, not lived ("You are now agent builders"). **83.**

## Stance

- M6 story-of-module-6: "A rule in context is not a rule in the output." (scar: four leaks of the banned word)
- M5 the-gate-is-a-claim: "Green is a claim about the check, not a fact about the work." (Goodhart)
- M2 how-instructions-grow: "Prohibitions are weak instructions."

Against its own interest: M4 pre-read: "Mollick does not call the winner. Neither does this training." **92.**

**B.** Positions defended:

## Room (B)

The exercises carry most of the turn. The lectures mostly name what was just done: M2 compounding "The thing a chat can't do is **Phase 3**"; M3 "Six agents worked on one question in this module."

**Strongest beats**
1. **M5 hallucination-bakeoff, Phase 5:** "Ask the agent to run it once on your `./crux.md` answer, the one Module 3 left you doubting. Write one line in `./crux.md`: what the judge caught, or what it could not check." The M3 doubt is resolved on the student's own artefact. The rescue is lived, not told.
2. **M1 personal-site-with-guardrails, Phases 1→6:** "Iterate until you say *'yes, this is me.'*" and then "The LLM didn't get better between Phase 1 and Phase 6. The context did." The frame is lived before it is named.
3. (Close third) **M6 eval-loop:** "Mark three claims from the last round yourself before you read its verdicts. Where you disagree, the judge has a blind spot the loop cannot see." The counter-voice is a move, not a caveat.

**Weakest beats**
1. **M8 joint-double-diamond, identity close:** "the sponsor says, in one sentence: *'You are now agent builders...'*". The arc's payoff is a scripted utterance by someone else. It is told, not lived, and the student's own M1→M8 change is never put in front of them.
2. **M3 three-retrievers-one-curator:** mostly prompt routing ("Session 1, wiki... Session 2, docs..."). Its one story beat ("Find one number or phrase that the synthesizer dropped... Should it have survived?") is a question with no consequence. The unease lands in the debrief and the next exercise instead.
3. (Also weak) **M7 share-your-work / design-the-sharing-plan / test-the-sharing-plan:** "The agent compares... and writes your choice to `module-7/branch.md`." The agent writes every artefact. The scar story that gives M7 its weight sits in the lecture after the exercises and is never touched by them.
