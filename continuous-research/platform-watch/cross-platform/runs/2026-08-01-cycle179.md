### [Finding 1] EU AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations — enforcement TOMORROW (August 2, 2026); high-risk delay confirmed but transparency tier is live

**Sources:**
- https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/yes-august-2-still-matters-the-eu-approved-a-high-risk-ai-delay-but-most-trans.html — [practitioner analysis — law firm] (July 2026)
- https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-article-50-transparency-20260729/ — [practitioner analysis — CSA] (July 29, 2026)
- https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/06/24/eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-preparing-for-compliance-by-2-august-2026/ — [practitioner analysis — law firm] (June 24, 2026)
- https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-50 — [regulatory source — EU Commission] (2026)

**Date:** August 1, 2026 (enforcement: August 2, 2026)
**Agent level:** company (enterprise compliance obligation)
**What:** The EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency obligations take effect tomorrow, August 2, 2026. This is NOT delayed by the Digital Omnibus (which pushed high-risk Annex III obligations to December 2, 2027). Article 50 applies to ALL AI systems deployed to EU users regardless of risk tier — an organization with no high-risk AI systems may still have significant Article 50 obligations.

**Five Article 50 obligations (effective August 2, 2026):**

1. **AI-interaction disclosure**: Providers must inform users they are interacting with AI, "unless that fact is already obvious to a reasonably well-informed, observant and circumspect person" — chatbots, AI agents, conversational systems all potentially in scope.

2. **Content marking**: Providers must mark AI-generated audio, image, video, or text in a machine-readable format "to the extent technically feasible." Applies to synthetic-content generators.

3. **Emotion recognition/biometric notice**: Deployers must notify individuals when emotion recognition or biometric categorization systems are operating.

4. **Deepfake disclosure**: Deployers must disclose when visual or audio content is artificially generated or manipulated.

5. **Public interest text disclosure**: Deployers publishing AI-generated text on public matters must disclose it (exception for human-editorially-reviewed content).

**Narrow grace period**: Only providers with systems marketed before August 2, 2026 get until December 2, 2026 for machine-readable marking implementation — all other August 2 obligations apply immediately.

**Jones Walker (Loring/Ramsden/Lee) assessment**: "Companies that treat the extra sixteen months [on high-risk] as cancellation rather than runway will face the same obligations later, with less time to build the controls they require." The law firm treats this as a reprieve, not a repeal.

**CTO implication:** All enterprise AI agent deployments serving EU users face Article 50 requirements starting tomorrow. The compliance question for agent platforms: do they label AI-generated outputs in machine-readable format? Do they disclose agent identity to users? Most enterprise agent platforms (Copilot, Agentforce, etc.) have made disclosure UI updates but machine-readable marking for synthetic content remains unclear.

**Evidence level:** L3 (regulatory source + 3 independent law firm analyses converging on same obligations)
**Key claims:**
- Article 50 takes effect August 2, 2026 — no delay; enforcement is tomorrow
- High-risk Annex III delayed to December 2, 2027 via Digital Omnibus (confirmed, separate)
- AI-interaction disclosure is the most broadly applicable obligation — enterprise agent deployments to EU users are in scope
- Machine-readable marking for AI-generated content is technically complex; narrow grace period for pre-August systems only
- EU Commission published implementation guidelines; law firm analysis (Baker McKenzie, Jones Walker, Sidley) confirms scope
