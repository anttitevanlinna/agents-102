## Frame

**One-sentence frame:** Agents 101 is one agent, assembled one piece at a time in front of the student — context (M1), memory (M2), other agents (M3), boundary (M4), check (M5), loop (M6), interface (M7), the assembler itself (M8) — and every module is a case of "what breaks when this piece is missing, and what you give up by adding it."

The refrain is explicit and repeats by name across modules, not just implied: M1 — "In the full agent picture, this is the first piece: context" (`context-is-king.md`). M2 — "In the full agent picture, Module 2 adds shelf life" (`compounding.md`). M3 — "In the full agent picture, other agents are part of the tool surface" (`when-to-split-an-agent.md`). M4 — "In the full agent picture, this is the boundary piece" (`practice-of-risk.md`). M5 — "In the full agent picture, this is the check" (`grounded.md`). M7 — "In the full agent picture, this is the interface piece" (`access-is-not-absorption.md`). M8 closes it: "At this point the full picture is visible: model, context, tools, goal, loop, checks, boundary, interface" (`new-human-role-in-the-loop.md`), and the backing block for `where-is-this-all-going.md` calls it "the refrain's last turn."

Three modules that only make sense through the frame: M2 (memory is context that gained shelf life — nothing else in M2 is explicable without the M1 mechanism, stated directly: "This is the same mechanism from Module 1: context shapes output, run at system scale"), M4 (the boundary piece deliberately *removes* what M1–M3 added — "Everything you have built so far gave the agent more... Now the move is to give it less"), M8 (the assembler is itself named as one more piece the student now owns: "You described it; something else built it... What changed is who assembles them").

Where the frame breaks, named on the page: `where-is-this-all-going.md` — "Some of what you built here will be obsolete within a year... The judge you wrote, or the thing it judges? The memory, or the work of curating it? Nobody in this room knows yet, and the people running this training do not know either." The backing block goes further, naming the frame's structural mortality: "A model that plans, checks and bounds itself end to end would collapse several parts into one, and the lecture would then be teaching an anatomy the platform had absorbed."

## Narrative

The training in five sentences: a builder leader walks in able to get only generic output from an LLM and leaves with an agent system built entirely from their own live problem. Each module hands the student one more capability — memory that survives the chat window, other agents that disagree with each other, a boundary that gives less instead of more, a judge that measures instead of trusting the model's own confidence — and each capability immediately reveals a new thing the student can no longer take on faith. The training could have ended on the easiest, most demo-friendly beat: one impressive agent built live on the last day. It refuses that beat on the page: **"We could have spent today building you one impressive agent to show on Friday. It would have demoed well and it would have been the wrong thing to sell you, because the agent that matters in six months is one nobody in this room has thought of yet, built by somebody who is not you."** (`agents-building-agents.md`). The training ends not on a solved system but on an admitted one: "Nothing here closed. The memory is still short. The judge is still yours, and still unchecked in the places you have not looked" (`where-is-this-all-going.md`).

The turn is lived, not just asserted, at three separate points the student actually sits inside: M3's debrief closes on "The doubt stays. Hold it," M6's `when-the-score-stops-moving.md` catches the student about to misread a flat score as "done" and corrects it in real time, and M8 refuses its own most sellable ending. That's a turn a student experiences across the arc, not a shape only visible in the headings. It falls short of "the turn is the training's own failure" (100) because the concrete first-person incident of the training's own approach failing is a marked, currently-empty slot (see POV) rather than a shipped scene — the turn is the training declining temptation and naming its own unease, not yet narrating a specific place it broke.

## Stance

Positions the training could lose a customer over, each with the mechanism or consequence it's defended by rather than a bare assertion:

1. **Plain text beats a database or platform.** "Nothing sits between the model and what it's best at. Every fancier setup that promised to 'fix' this added a layer that the model had to work *around*." (`compounding.md`) — undercuts any vendor pitching structured storage as the fix.
2. **Mostly don't split into multiple agents.** "Resist it. Single-agent is the plain default... please, for the sake of your sanity, write the one prompt." (`when-to-split-an-agent.md`) — a training that just spent 90 minutes making multi-agent feel powerful turns and argues most students shouldn't use it. Defended by naming the cost directly: "coordination cost eats the win."
3. **The security check does not replace security.** "Mitigations are layered, not replacements... Concluding that the agent check replaces security ships a breach." (`security.md`) — refuses to let the training's own exercise be oversold as sufficient coverage; defended by naming the concrete consequence.
4. **Hallucination is permanent, not a patch note away.** "This isn't a bug that gets patched in the next release. It's the shape of the technology. Later models will fabricate less; they won't stop." (`grounded.md`) — refuses the "wait for the next model" story a vendor benefits from.
5. **You cannot actually share an agent; accountability doesn't travel.** "The first three copy in an afternoon. The fourth does not copy at all... Accountability is the part with a name on it, and a name does not travel inside a folder." (`access-is-not-absorption.md`) — undercuts a naive "roll it out to everyone" sales narrative for exactly the kind of platform-rollout engagement a training vendor might want to sell next.
6. **The impressive closing demo is the wrong thing to sell.** "It would have demoed well and it would have been the wrong thing to sell you." (`agents-building-agents.md`) — flagged by the maintainer block itself as unique: "This is the arc's single against-interest instance."

Position #6 is explicitly held against the training's own commercial interest, and the training's own production notes confirm it is deliberate and singular rather than a pattern spread thin. That satisfies the 100 anchor on its own; the density and mechanistic defense of #1–#5 back it up rather than diluting it.

## The pair

Self-doubt is not rationed to a caveat paragraph; it runs the same route as the stance, module by module: the self-grading LLM (M1), "the doubt stays, hold it" (M3), "the unease stays, nothing today resolves it, that's the curriculum" (M4), "certainty isn't available" (M5), "you have not checked the judge against yourself" and "you lose the eye that made you worth putting in charge of it" (M6), "nobody in this room knows yet, and the people running this training do not know either" (M8). Every module that lands a strong position also names, in the same file, what that position or its instrument cannot see.

This reads as someone who has been there, not a hedge and not a pitch. A pure hedge would soften the positions themselves ("multi-agent might not always help"); this training states the position bluntly ("write the one prompt," "ships a breach," "it would have been the wrong thing to sell you") and *then* separately names what it still doesn't know about its own tools. A pure pitch would let the stance stand unqualified for sales copy; this training's own maintainer layer is explicit that the load-bearing personal failure story is still unwritten, which is the one piece "been there" would need to close the loop and the training visibly hasn't closed it yet.
