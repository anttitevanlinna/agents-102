## Frame

**A (AE101) — 94.** Frame in one sentence: *agentic work is engineering — a feedback loop that drifts unless something outside it measures, so trust is a check you have verified, and the check is itself a claim.*
- M1 `lectures/the-machine-you-just-met.md`: "A check from outside the session resets the chain. A failing test does not care how confident the answer sounded."
- M3 `lectures/the-loop-half-filled.md`: "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller**... Cut the feedback signal (a test, a check, a read) and it drifts."
- M5 `lectures/what-packaging-is.md`: "**A check is a position fix**. At a fix the wedge of possible states collapses to a point"
- M5 `lectures/the-gate-is-a-claim.md`: "Green is a claim about the check, not a fact about the work."
- Where it breaks, on the page: M5 `lectures/the-gate-is-a-claim.md` "Today's right procedure, your gates and workflow, yours or the agent's, is superseded too."; M5 `lectures/what-packaging-is.md` "Whether this field ever settles into a real best practice is an open question."

Held back from 100: the map (six phases) and the loop compete as the organising picture in M2–M3, and M6 `lectures/composing-the-workflow.md` hands the frame over as four bare figures.

**B (A101) — 88.** Frame in one sentence: *an agent is a handful of nameable parts — context, tools, boundary, checks, loop, interface — and generic AI becomes yours by what you decide to give it and what you keep back.*

- M4 `lectures/practice-of-risk.md`: "Everything you have built so far gave the agent more... Now the move is to give it less. More context makes a good answer likelier. Less access makes a bad answer smaller."
