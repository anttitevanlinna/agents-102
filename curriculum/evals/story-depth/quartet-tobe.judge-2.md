# Quartet estimate — A101 skeleton to be (titles only)

## Frame

One candidate lens: **what you build either compounds or rots — and the training is the discipline of telling which.** Three modules only make sense through it: M2's own name, "**Compounding**," plus "**It gets better by being edited, not by being clever**" (an agent artifact as a compounding asset); M6's "**Module 5 turned judgment into a judge**" (compounding *evaluation* on top of M2's compounding *memory*); M8's "**The tool that builds tools compounds**," explicitly naming the Big Idea as a slide, plus the closing "**A flywheel, not a graduation**." That's a load-bearing thread across three widely separated modules by title alone.

But titles can't show the lens holding for M3 (multi-agent) or M4 (security) — "**Start with don't**," "**Three stances beat one summarizer**," "**Certainty is a fantasy you inherited**," "**No employee reads every document as an instruction**" read as their own topics (splitting judgment, risk discipline) with no title-level bridge back to compounding. No title names where the frame breaks; a break would have to say "this doesn't compound" or similar, and none does.

## Narrative

A builder leader wants to run agents without babysitting them. Early wins go too well — "**Why the retro was kind to you**" — and the training breaks that comfort by making them watch an agent's memory, then a synthesizer, drift and average into "**beige**." By M4 the ground itself is gone: "**Certainty is a fantasy you inherited.**" The turn is "**There is truth out there**" — M5 opens the rescue by asserting a floor exists to measure against, after three modules of unresolved and deepening unease. The training then hands the leader the instrument they were missing all along — "**Module 5 turned judgment into a judge**" — and asks them to hold their own org to the same test the training held itself to: "**Will your organisation learn faster than the model changes underneath it?**"

Titles carry a shape (joy → unease → deepened unease → rescue → leverage → generosity → awe), which is more than a bare topic list, but the turn is asserted through the mood labels' logic more than lived beat-to-beat in the titles themselves — a title reader gets the arc's direction, not the felt drop.

## Point of view

A second-person narrator who has watched the student's own sessions: "**You felt context move**," "**You act on the future to know what's real**," "**You cannot share an agent.**" This is someone close enough to name the student's private experience back to them — "**Why the retro was kind to you**" implies the narrator caught the student being flattered by their own agent and is telling them so. That's a narrator with a stated vantage point, not just a voice in asides.

No title puts the narrator's *own* failure on the page, though — every scar in this list belongs to the student ("you," "your organisation") or to the technology ("the judge names its own limit"). "**The report is a hypothesis, not a result**" comes closest to an admission of method-level fallibility, but titles can't show whether that's the narrator confessing or the narrator instructing.

## Stance

Positions a customer could push back on, each carried by a title:
- Multi-agent is usually the wrong call: "**Start with don't**"
- The safest security control is refusing to build the feature: "**The best mitigation is the door you don't open**"
- Self-evaluating metrics can't be trusted once they're the target: "**A yardstick you rewrite is not a yardstick**"
- Oversight should shrink as the system improves, which is the opposite of comfortable: "**The better it gets, the less you watch**"
- The bottleneck isn't the tool, it's the org's people: "**Access is easy; absorption is scarce**"

Titles show these asserted, not defended — a mechanism only surfaces where the title itself names one ("**Three stances beat one summarizer**" gestures at *why* multi-agent works when it does, which is a mechanism, not just an opinion). "**The best mitigation is the door you don't open**" is a position against the training's own commercial interest in one direct sense: it tells a buyer of agent training not to build the agent.

## The pair

Both: "**Start with don't**" (M3 opener, a stance) sits directly against "**The doubt stays. Hold it.**" (M3 debrief) — someone recommending restraint who has also sat with the doubt long enough to name it as a thing to hold, not resolve.

## Scores

Frame: 55 · Narrative: 55 · Point of view: 50 · Stance: 65

## What titles cannot show

- Whether the compounding frame is *argued* inside M3/M4 or simply absent from their surface — prose could reconnect it, or it could genuinely be a different lens for those two modules.
- Whether the second-person narrator's authority comes from having personally failed with agents, or is a purely instructional "you" with no lived cost behind it.
- Whether "**Three stances beat one summarizer**" and similarly mechanism-shaped titles actually deliver a defended mechanism in the body, or the title is doing more work than the prose underneath it.
