## Frame

**A (AE101).** Frame in one sentence: agentic engineering is engineering — a loop that drifts unless something outside it checks it, and that compounds only through what you write down. Three modules that only make sense through it:
- M1, `lectures/the-machine-you-just-met.md`: "A check from outside the session resets the chain … That is why the failing test came before the fix."
- M3, `lectures/the-loop-half-filled.md`: "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller** … Cut the feedback signal (a test, a check, a read) and it drifts."
- M5, `lectures/what-packaging-is.md`: "**A check is a position fix**. At a fix the wedge of possible states collapses to a point".
- Where it breaks, said on the page: M5, `lectures/the-gate-is-a-claim.md`: "Today's right procedure, your gates and workflow, yours or the agent's, is superseded too. Retire what the next model outgrows"; M2, `lectures/how-instructions-grow.md`: "This training stops short of the full system".
The map (`lectures/the-whole-map.md`, lit half by half at M3 and M4) makes each module a place on one drawing. Break is named, but spread over several lines and never as one "here the frame fails" statement. **95.**

**B (APT101).** Frame in one sentence: building got cheap and deciding didn't, so the trio's craft is choosing the outcome, placing a bet that can lose and reading the no. Three days that only make sense through it:
- Day 1, `lectures/apt101-building-is-cheap.md`: "For years the slice was the slow step. Now an agent can build it. Every other step is a decision … None of those got cheaper."
- Day 2, `lectures/apt101-why-it-agreed.md`: "Agents amplify the way a team already works. They do not transform it … A faster feature factory is still a feature factory."
- Day 3, `lectures/apt101-three-jobs-rewritten.md`: "That is real strategy work, and it does not get cheaper when building does."
- Where it breaks, one slide that says so in so many words: Day 3, `lectures/apt101-where-you-go-from-here.md`: "This is where \"building got cheap, deciding didn't\" stops being an edge on its own … What would change this training's mind: agents that start deciding well".
Every exercise is a decision the agent cannot take (the door, the doubt, the prune, persevere/pivot/stop), so the frame is load-bearing in the room and not just in slides. The break is stated more plainly than A's. What keeps it a point under A: a handful of Day 2 slides (`apt101-write-it-down-or-lose-it.md`: "The agent stops where you stop writing"; tampering; don'ts) read through A's frame (expression, compounding), not through cheap-building. **94.**

## Narrative

**A.** Five sentences: An engineer fixes a trivial bug and writes down what the session taught. They learn to distrust a plausible plan and to let the agent run on a branch. Then they send a real task off with nothing around it, and it comes back short. They read the return, build the check their own failure asked for and re-send. The last module shows the author failing the same way while writing it, and hands the loop over. The turn, M5, `exercises/diagnose-and-resend.md`: "The un-packaged run was supposed to underdeliver. What came back is data, not blame." Set up at M4, `lectures/test-and-learn.md`: "Session one goes now, un-packaged: no plan.md, no verifier, no reference artifact." The training's own failure, M6, `lectures/story-of-module-6.md`: "The agent I was working with had just finished writing those three modules. It still opened with the un-packaged shape." Stakes are thinner in M2–M3 (side quest logistics) than in M4–M6. **93.**

**B.** Five sentences: A trio arrives with its own product and the memory of a feature nobody asked for, and writes a bet with each of their names on a line. They send an agent off overnight with a one-sentence brief in their own words and a guess at what it will say. In the morning the digest agrees with them; turned round, the same material gives a second headline. They build a check that won on their own evidence, choose one bet, imagine it dead, and put a slice in front of five strangers who hold or break their own Day 1 line. Then their own agent goes generic on another trio's laptop, and they leave with a proposal their team has not yet changed and a bet still open. The turn, lived: Day 2, `exercises/apt101-read-the-digest.md`: "Ask Claude to run your digest once more from the same brief, with only your look-for line turned round, and to set the two headlines side by side." Named after: Day 2, `lectures/apt101-why-it-agreed.md`: "The headline came from your question as much as from your customers." Stakes return on Day 3, `exercises/apt101-five-users.md`: "each of you reads your own Day 1 hypothesis … and says out loud the line in it the five users touched, and whether it held or broke." Close loops the opener: Day 3 module, "is anything in `team/monday.md` the next thing your team builds that nobody asked for?"

On the 100 anchor ("the turn is the training's own failure"): B's turn is the student's own sentence failing them, which is the right owner for a student-as-hero shape; I score that as the top of the 80 band and above, not as a miss. Two things hold it under A. The turn is conditional on the page ("If your line already asked for a test…"), so for some trios it will not fire, and the exercise's own Take stock softens it the moment it lands ("Each of you still knew one source well enough to catch where it went past what was there"). And two long stretches run on plumbing with no stakes: `apt101-gather-the-evidence.md` between the lived turn and its naming, and the last forty minutes of `apt101-take-it-to-the-team.md`. The open ending is intended and works: "The bet is still open, and the next slice is yours to place." **90.**

## Stance

**A.** Positions it could lose a customer over, with the defence:
- Best practice from the model is a forecast: M5, `lectures/what-packaging-is.md`: "Asked ahead, the model predicts. **A prediction is not a measurement.**" Defended by mechanism ("a well-read average of what other people published about other repos, frozen at a cutoff").
- A rule in context is not a rule in the output: M6, `lectures/story-of-module-6.md`: "The rule was loaded into context. Claude used it twice in the third turn." Defended by scar.
- Green is not proof: M5, `lectures/the-gate-is-a-claim.md`: "A gate nobody has verified is a gate trusted on vibes." Mechanism: Goodhart, lucky sample, miscalibrated judge.
- Earned trust degrades you: M4, `lectures/ironies-of-automation.md`: "The trust is deserved. The watching still has to be engineered."
- Against its own interest: M5, `lectures/the-gate-is-a-claim.md`: "Retire what the next model outgrows"; M6, `lectures/agents-that-build-agents.md`: "nobody has that part figured out yet." Real, though said as humility more than as a cost to the seller. **92.**

**B.** Positions, with the defence:
- Agents do not fix a team that doesn't check: Day 2, `lectures/apt101-why-it-agreed.md`: "A team that ships without asking whether it worked now ships more of it, faster, and still never finds out." Defended by the digest the trio just read.
- The agent's analysis is your question handed back: same file: "Its headline is your own look-for line handed back, not an insight." Defended by the reversed run.
- Asking the agent to double-check is worthless: Day 2, `lectures/apt101-fluent-is-not-true.md`: "\"Are you sure?\" is another fluent answer." Mechanism: next likely word.
- You cannot roll out an agent: Day 3, `lectures/apt101-from-us-to-the-team.md`: "So a proposal to the wider team cannot promise \"everyone gets our agent\"." Defended by the hand-over the trio just ran and by the guide's scar.
- Your own criteria rot: Day 3, `lectures/apt101-what-good-means.md`: "A team that writes for its own criteria has rebuilt the feature factory with a dashboard."
- Against its own commercial interest, in so many words: Day 2, `lectures/apt101-go-back-to-your-customers.md`: "So a team that does not talk with its customers every week should start there, before agents … That sells less of this training. It is still where to start."
B has the cleanest against-own-interest line of the two trainings. It is held back to A's level by how many positions are borrowed with attribution rather than held (Torres, Fitzpatrick, Nielsen, Ries each get a slide that reports a view), and by one or two that are stated flat ("Future leadership is about strategy, outcomes and customers"). **92.**

## Room

Do the exercises carry the story? Mostly yes, and better than the lectures do. The three turns of the plot (the agreeing digest, the five users, the agent that goes generic) each happen to the trio in an exercise before a slide names them, and in each the trio wrote a prediction first: the expect line on Day 1, the face-down post-its in the bake-off, the watch-for file, the post-it before running the other trio's file. That prediction-then-result pattern is what makes the turn lived. Where it sags, an exercise turns into file-routing and the agent does the thinking while the trio reads.

Strongest two:
1. Day 1 → Day 2, `exercises/apt101-send-it-off.md` into `exercises/apt101-read-the-digest.md`. The trap is the trio's own sentence: "One more sentence before it runs, in your own words: what do you expect the digest to say about your hypothesis?" then "`latest.html` stays unopened. What it says waits for Day 2." then "Is the headline the one you expected? Did it find what your line asked for?" and the reversal. Nothing is named until the lecture two exercises later.
2. Day 3, `exercises/apt101-five-users.md`. "One pattern to watch: after the second session, someone wants to explain the slice to the next user before they start. Don't. The confusion is the finding." and Phase 4: "If none of your lines broke, say what result would have broken it. Your own line, not someone else's." The Day 1 hypothesis with a name on it comes back to its author in front of the other two.
(Close third: `exercises/apt101-take-it-to-the-team.md` Phase 2: "The designer points at the line in the output that went generic and says what their own memory would have said there.")

Weakest two:
1. Day 2, `exercises/apt101-gather-the-evidence.md`. Thirty-five minutes directly after the turn, and most of it is session and file logistics: "When your retriever's second round lands, copy its file to `team/<your-name>/`, and copy the other two into your own `sources/` as they land." The one story beat ("read your retriever's skipped list aloud") is the last paragraph. The trio has just seen two headlines from one brief and the exercise does not let that change how they brief the retriever beyond one prompt.
2. Day 3, `exercises/apt101-take-it-to-the-team.md` Phases 3–5. The last exercise of the training hands the pen to the agent: "Ask Claude to draft the job your wider team is trying to get done", "Ask Claude to find the one obstacle … then draft the technical plan and the people plan together", "Ask Claude what would have to be true for your wider team to switch". The trio reads `module-7/` files for half an hour at the point where the story should be most theirs. The one line with teeth is "A polished technical plan beside a people plan full of `UNASSIGNED` is a finding."
(Also thin: `exercises/apt101-keep-and-run-tonight.md`, whose lesson is told by the lecture before it; and a thread left hanging from `exercises/apt101-paint-the-product-box.md`: "Keep the generic line the fresh read found; it is the first thing to fix" is never picked up again on the page.)
