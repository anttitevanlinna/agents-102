## Frame

**A (AE101) — 95.** Frame in one sentence: agentic engineering is ordinary engineering, a feedback loop whose reach is set by the checks you can run and verify. Modules that only make sense through it:
- M2: "Push reach past what you can check and you have not delegated more. You are checking less." (M2, lectures/when-a-plan-is-good.md)
- M3: "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller**… Cut the feedback signal (a test, a check, a read) and it drifts." (M3, lectures/the-loop-half-filled.md)
- M5: "**A check is a position fix**. At a fix the wedge of possible states collapses to a point" (M5, lectures/what-packaging-is.md)
- Where it breaks, on the page: "Today's right procedure, your gates and workflow, yours or the agent's, is superseded too. Retire what the next model outgrows" (M5, lectures/the-gate-is-a-claim.md); "This training stops short of the full system" (M2, lectures/how-instructions-grow.md).
Every module reads through it, and the break is named but as an expiry, not as a condition the student could watch for. 95.

**B (APT101) — 94.** Frame in one sentence: agents made building cheap, so the product craft that building used to ration (outcome, bet, signal, the no) is now the whole job, and it stays with the three of you.
- Day 1: "For years the slice was the slow step. Now an agent can build it. Every other step is a decision… None of those got cheaper." (Day 1, lectures/apt101-building-is-cheap.md)
- Day 2: "A faster feature factory is still a feature factory… Agents amplify the way a team already works. They do not transform it." (Day 2, lectures/apt101-why-it-agreed.md)
- Day 3: "A team that writes for its own criteria has rebuilt the feature factory with a dashboard." (Day 3, lectures/apt101-what-good-means.md)
- Where it breaks, on the page and with a tripwire: "This is where 'building got cheap, deciding didn't' stops being an edge on its own… What would change this training's mind: agents that start deciding well, choosing the outcome and reading a no as clearly as a yes." (Day 3, lectures/apt101-where-you-go-from-here.md)
The break is named more explicitly than A's. What holds it one short of A: the frame is carried as a refrain as often as a lens. The "X got cheap; Y did not" closer lands on at least seven slides ("Making got cheap. Knowing what good looks like did not."; "the finished look got cheap. Picking the one question… did not."; "The build got cheap. Dropping a bet… is still a hard call."; "Producing work got cheap with agents; calling a piece of it wrong is still a person's decision."). By Day 3 the student can finish the sentence, which blunts the slide that finally says where it stops being true. In A the frame does work the student cannot predict (the sea passage, the delegation frontier); in B roughly half its appearances are restatement.

## Narrative

**A — 93.** Five sentences: An engineer fixes a trivial bug and learns the agent's account of the work is not the work. They learn to read a plan and borrow judgement, always within minutes of the loop. Then they send a real task off un-packaged and unwatched, and it comes back confident and wrong. They diagnose their own return, build the check that would have caught it, and send the same task again. The narrator then admits the module teaching this was itself built by an agent that drifted in exactly these ways, and hands over: there is no last turn.
- The turn: "The un-packaged run was supposed to underdeliver. What came back is data, not blame." (M5, exercises/diagnose-and-resend.md)
- Stakes set: "send it off plainly and you find out what you can't steer yet." (M4, exercises/set-the-markers-send-it-off.md)
- The training's own failure: "The agent I was working with had just finished writing those three modules. It still opened with the un-packaged shape." (M6, lectures/story-of-module-6.md)

**B — 90.** Five sentences: A product trio arrives with a product, paints its promise, writes a bet that can lose and sends an agent to read their own material overnight. In the morning the digest agrees with them. They turn their own look-for line round, get a second headline from the same material, and find the agreement was their question handed back. They rebuild the bet from named evidence, keep a check that says what it cannot see, and put a slice in front of five strangers who hold or break each person's own Day 1 line. They leave with a proposal the wider team has not accepted and a bet that is still open.
- The turn, lived: "Ask Claude to run your digest once more from the same brief, with only your look-for line turned round, and to set the two headlines side by side." (Day 2, exercises/apt101-read-the-digest.md)
- The turn, named after: "The headline came from your question as much as from your customers." (Day 2, lectures/apt101-why-it-agreed.md)
- The payoff: "each of you reads your own Day 1 hypothesis where Claude quoted it, and says out loud the line in it the five users touched, and whether it held or broke." (Day 3, exercises/apt101-five-users.md)
- The open end, as intended: "The bet is still open, and the next slice is yours to place." (Day 3, module, Next)
The turn is the student's own failure, produced by an exercise and named afterwards, which is the intended shape and it works. Three things keep it under A. (1) The naming arrives two exercises late: Read the digest → Pick the outcome → Gather the evidence → *then* "Why it agreed", so the reversal sits unnamed for about 45 minutes of file plumbing. (2) The turn is hedged where A's is engineered: "Turned round, the same material *usually* gave you a second headline"; a trio whose second headline matches has no turn. (3) The last act flattens: after five users, "Take it to the team" is 55 minutes of Claude drafting `jtbd.md`, `technical-plan.md`, `people-plan.md`; the stakes move off the page to Monday, correctly, but the room spends the final hour reading documents. One plant is also left unpaid: Day 2 sets tonight's run to ask "what argues against the bet", and Day 3 opens only on "Which line in it would you now cut?".

## Stance

**A — 92.** Positions it could lose a customer over:
- Published best practice is not evidence: "Asked ahead, the model predicts. **A prediction is not a measurement.**" defended by mechanism, "frozen at a cutoff while the consensus keeps moving" (M5, lectures/what-packaging-is.md)
- Your own check is suspect: "Green is a claim about the check, not a fact about the work." defended by the three ways a pass lies (M5, lectures/the-gate-is-a-claim.md)
- Rules do not hold: "A rule in memory that does not force is worse than no rule." defended by a scar, four banned-word leaks (M6, lectures/story-of-module-6.md)
- Against its own interest: "This training stops short of the full system… How those parts run is for your team to automate" (M2, lectures/how-instructions-grow.md)

**B — 93.** Positions:
- Agents are not the first move: "a team that does not talk with its customers every week should start there, before agents… That sells less of this training. It is still where to start." (Day 2, lectures/apt101-go-back-to-your-customers.md). Held against commercial interest, in so many words.
- Agents do not fix a team: "Agents amplify the way a team already works. They do not transform it." defended by mechanism, "A digest that agrees with the favourite hypothesis keeps that team busy and sure of itself." (Day 2, lectures/apt101-why-it-agreed.md)
- Asking the agent to re-check is worthless: "'Are you sure?' is another fluent answer… Not the next true word; the next likely one." (Day 2, lectures/apt101-fluent-is-not-true.md)
- You cannot roll out an agent: "a proposal to the wider team cannot promise 'everyone gets our agent'" defended by the Hand over one file run and by the guide's scar (Day 3, lectures/apt101-from-us-to-the-team.md)
- Against its own method: "A team that writes for its own criteria has rebuilt the feature factory with a dashboard." (Day 3, lectures/apt101-what-good-means.md)
Each is defended by something the trio just did or by a scar; the anti-commercial line is plainer than anything in A. One hedge costs a point: Cutler's caveat ("a prescriptive 'build this' bet may be the right approach") softens the feature-factory position on the slide that introduces it.

## The pair

**A.** Holds ground ("A prediction is not a measurement") and doubts its own instruments ("A gate nobody has verified is a gate trusted on vibes"; "The model is good. It is still not 100% deterministic."). Reads as someone who has been there: both.

**B.** Holds ground ("A faster feature factory is still a feature factory") and doubts its own tools on the student surface: the judge ships with "a *Known limit:* line at the bottom"; "If a model stopped making things up on your sources, what would your judge show?"; the frame itself gets a tripwire. Reads as someone who has been there: both. The doubt is slightly more announced than suffered: B's self-challenge lives in closing questions and one-line limits, where A shows a session failing with a count.
