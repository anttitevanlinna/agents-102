# Teaching authority for learning agents OODA Cycle — 2026-09-08 13:43

**Focus area:** Who may teach which durable lesson in a learning-agent system spanning a central DevSecOps team, security, service owners, and requesters.
**Research mode:** People-first practitioner synthesis plus current governance-capability inspection.
**Why this focus:** Cycle 220 established that learning surfaces carry different force. This round tests the user's stronger claim—that the human gate grounds the agent—by asking which human's judgment is authoritative at each scope.

## Findings

### One deployed rule-compounding loop delegates local generalization to the engineer, but does not solve multi-team authority

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13091v1 — [academic/research]
**Date:** 2026-07-13
**Agent level:** Team
**Who:** Aditya Aggarwal and Nahid Farhady Ghalaty
**What:** In their 35-plus-service deployment, the engineer receiving review feedback decides whether a comment describes a recurring class of mistake. The instruction file is shared, changes require pull requests, and the team arbitrates conflicts through normal review.
**Evidence level:** L2 single-team field report.
**Boundary:** The authors explicitly say their model works for one team and name hierarchical or namespaced rules, cross-team conflict resolution, and provenance during organization-wide promotion as future work. They also warn that poor reviewer judgment can poison the rule set.

### Security memory products separate the lesson's source from its approver

**Source:** https://semgrep.dev/blog/2026/inside-the-ai-memories-security-teams-are-writing/ — [vendor press release]
**Date:** 2026-05-06
**Agent level:** Team / company
**Who:** Braden Riggs, describing Semgrep's product and aggregate customer-memory analysis
**What:** An AppSec engineer may author a security memory directly. A developer's finding dismissal and rationale may instead become a suggested memory that requires admin approval before it affects future scans. Both eventually feed the same organizational memory system.
**Evidence level:** L0 product capability with aggregate vendor analysis.
**Boundary:** This is the clearest current product shape for bottom-up evidence plus higher-authority promotion, but it is not an independently documented customer operating model or proof that the promoted memories are correct.

### Fin makes skill ownership and expert evaluation explicit, but leaves promotion rights unpublished

**Sources:** https://ideas.fin.ai/p/claude-code-good-skills-bad-skills; https://github.com/intercom/2x-skills/tree/main/plugins/skill-tools — [practitioner direct]
**Date:** 2026-07-15; repository checked 2026-09-08
**Agent level:** Company
**Who:** Brian Scanlan
**What:** Fin distributes skills through a private auto-updating marketplace created by its dedicated team-2x. Scanlan says good skills have owners, are created through feedback loops, have maintained evals, and may be refreshed from session analysis; he calls output judged by non-experts a bad-skill failure mode. Fin's public `skill-review` checks seven quality categories and emits structured results.
**Evidence level:** L2 single-company practice; public tooling is directly inspectable.
**Boundary:** Fin reports 153 contributors and 267 skills in the marketplace's first three months, but does not publish who may approve a skill, how domain expertise is encoded in review routing, or how regressions across teams block an auto-update.

### GitHub's governance model supports central baselines plus additive local ownership

**Source:** https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/tutorials/roll-out-at-scale/govern-at-scale/maintain-codebase-standards — [vendor documentation]
**Date:** Checked 2026-09-08
**Agent level:** Company
**What:** GitHub recommends enterprise owners establish baseline rulesets while organization owners and repository administrators add more specific rules. Local rules add to the baseline rather than override it. Repository-specific CODEOWNERS can route changes to people with local knowledge; organization and repository instructions can likewise layer broad standards with project detail.
**Evidence level:** L0 shipped governance capability and vendor guidance.
**Boundary:** The primitives can encode an authority graph, but the documentation does not demonstrate a learning-agent deployment or specify who should approve each learned artifact.

### Production teams divide context authorship, local validation, and outcome accountability

**Sources:** https://www.intercom.com/blog/ai-is-approving-our-pull-requests-heres-how-we-made-it-safe/; https://engineering.atspotify.com/2026/4/background-coding-agents-dataset-migrations-honk-part-4 — [practitioner direct]
**Dates:** Kesha Mykhailov and Niamh Young, 2026-04-21; Devon Edwards Joseph, 2026-04-22
**Agent level:** Company
**What:** Intercom's engineers label review comments as helpful or unhelpful and continually refine company-specific guidance, while the engineer shipping a change remains responsible for production monitoring and rollback. Spotify's migration team wrote the constrained context and declared where the agent must abstain; downstream owning teams performed manual testing before merging when shared repositories lacked automated tests.
**Evidence level:** L2 single-company production accounts.
**Boundary:** Both cases separate central machinery from local semantic ownership. Neither publishes the exact approval policy for changing shared guidance, skills, or rules.

## Orientation: The Teacher Is an Authority Graph, Not a Universal Human Gate

A human correction is grounding evidence, but it is not automatically policy. The system needs to preserve three distinct roles:

1. **Source:** who observed the case and supplied intent or rationale.
2. **Semantic owner:** who may decide that the lesson is valid and generalizable in that domain.
3. **Effect owner:** who may authorize the artifact's operational scope and blast radius.

The same person may hold all three roles for a private or repository-local rule. They separate as soon as a lesson affects other teams, security posture, production infrastructure, or permissions.

## Authority Matrix for a 1,000-Person Software Unit

| Candidate learning | Valid source | Promotion owner | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Request intent, missing facts, satisfaction with one case | Requester | Ticket/process owner | Evidence for this case; no standing control change |
| Repository convention, service behavior, local test or rule | Developer/reviewer | Service or repository owner through normal PR/CODEOWNERS review | Repository or owned service scope |
| Security false-positive pattern, compensating control, detector interpretation | Developer or scanner operator | AppSec/control owner; affected service owner when local semantics are material | Explicit paths, services, rule versions, and expiry |
| Shared instruction schema, retrieval, compounding workflow, eval harness | Any user may propose | Central platform team | Mechanism only; platform cannot declare service truth |
| Organization-wide engineering standard | One or more teams | Enterprise standard owner plus affected-domain representation | Baseline may constrain local rules; local evidence cannot silently become global policy |
| Cloud runbook, IAM role, credential, approval or bypass authority | Operator may report a need | IAM/cloud control owner under external change control | Non-learnable; never granted by a memory update |
| Candidate capture, clustering, routing, replay proposal | Agent or automation | No approval needed until activation; named owner at promotion | Agent may propose and route, never approve its own authority |

**Design inference:** when a promoted artifact crosses a semantic boundary and an effect boundary, require both owners. The semantic owner answers “is this lesson true here?” The effect owner answers “may it change future behavior at this scope?” Human approval without this routing is merely a generic click.

## What I Looked For But Did Not Find

I found no published large-company system that assigns promotion rights across requester, repository, platform, security, and IAM roles and reports measured learning outcomes. Fin, Intercom, and Spotify expose the division of work but not their shared-guidance change policy. Aggarwal and Ghalaty explicitly leave multi-team governance open. GitHub and Semgrep expose useful primitives, but capability is not practice.

## Decision and Training Implication

Module 9 should teach: **preserve every human input, but promote it only through the authority appropriate to its artifact and scope.** A compact exercise can give learners six candidate lessons from ticket traces and ask them to identify the source, semantic owner, effect owner, permitted scope, verifier, and whether the candidate is learnable at all. The trap case is a reasonable operational suggestion that would silently widen IAM or approval authority.

**Next OODA question:** What should happen when two legitimate teachers disagree—how are conflicts represented, tested, scoped, expired, and resolved without flattening one authority into the other?
