## Frame

**One-sentence frame:** Agents 101 is the progressive assembly of "the full agent picture" — an anatomy of pieces (context, tools, goal, loop, checks, boundary, interface) that a recurring, literal callback phrase adds one at a time, module by module, until the student can name every part.

The frame is not stated once and dropped; it recurs as an explicit structural refrain across at least six lecture files in different modules, always the same construction:
- `lectures/context-is-king.md` (M1): "In the full agent picture, this is the first piece: context. Later you add tools, goals, checks, boundaries, and loops."
- `lectures/compounding.md` (M2): "In the full agent picture, Module 2 adds shelf life. Context stops being something trapped in one conversation. It becomes memory..."
- `lectures/when-to-split-an-agent.md` (M3): "In the full agent picture, other agents are part of the tool surface."
- `lectures/practice-of-risk.md` (M4): "In the full agent picture, this is the boundary piece... More capability without boundaries is not progress. It is just more blast radius."
- `lectures/grounded.md` (M5): "In the full agent picture, this is the check. The agent can have context, tools, goals, and boundaries, and still produce something fluent but unsupported."
- `lectures/evals-as-steering.md` (M6): "In the full agent picture, evals turn checks into a loop."

`lectures/new-human-role-in-the-loop.md` (M6 close) makes the assembly explicit and complete: "At this point the full picture is visible: model, context, tools, goal, loop, checks, boundary, interface." Only through this lens do M1 (context) and M4 (boundary) and M5 (check) read as *the same kind of move* — each module hands the student one labelled part of a single machine, not an unrelated new topic.

**Where the frame breaks, named on the page:** `lectures/when-the-score-stops-moving.md` shows the "checks" piece failing to do its whole job even while working exactly as designed: "The judge had nothing left to flag, not because the work had nothing left to improve... A grounded claim can still be vague, obvious, or useless, and this judge will pass it every time." The training does not merely gesture at a limit; it names which named piece of its own anatomy (the check) is insufficient and hands the residual responsibility back to the human, in the very next lecture.

## Narrative

**Five sentences, with the turn:**

A student arrives with a Claude session and a habit of getting pleasant, generic output back. Across the first four modules they learn that everything worth having comes from what they feed the machine — richer context, a memory that compounds, three minds instead of one, a boundary drawn on purpose — while a specific, named unease ("is this actually right?") keeps accumulating underneath every win. Module 5 finally hands them a measuring stick, but the training refuses to let relief become comfort, closing with the judge's stated "Known limit" rather than a clean bill of health. **The turn:** at Module 6 the training tells the student their job itself has changed — "You stop being the person who catches every detail. You become the person who decides which details the system must never miss" — and the very next lecture shows them their own new machinery going quiet while the work keeps getting better in a direction it was never built to see. By Module 8 the system starts extending itself, and the training ends by refusing to end: "You do not graduate. You have a flywheel."

The turn is quoted directly from `lectures/evals-as-steering.md`: "You stop being the person who catches every detail. You become the person who decides which details the system must never miss." It is a status change, not a skill gain — the protagonist's *role* is what moves, mid-training, on the page.

## Point of view

**Who is speaking:** a single, consistent second-person trainer voice that coaches the student through their own live work rather than narrating from outside it. It is not neutral instructional copy; it repeatedly predicts how the student will feel and pre-empts their likely reaction before naming what happened.

Three places the narrator shows itself:
1. `getting-going.md` (M1): "The LLM is generous to a rules file it just helped write... The praise carries forward into the generic-line call. Both picks come back softer than they probably should. Your own gut on what's uniquely you and what's generic carries more weight than Claude's." — the narrator distrusts the very tool it just had the student use, out loud, mid-module.
2. `exercises/three-minds-one-synthesis.md` (M3 close): "you'd stake your reputation on some of it and not all of it, and you can't yet say which is which. That feeling is correct. Hold the doubt. Name it to yourself. Don't fix it here." — direct address to the student's felt state, refusing to resolve it.
3. `lectures/when-the-score-stops-moving.md` (M6): "It is tempting to read that as 'the work is done.' That is not what happened... The judge had nothing left to flag, not because the work had nothing left to improve."

**Is the narrator's own failure on the page?** Yes, at two levels. Locally, M6 names that the machinery the training just spent a module building (the fixed judge) has a blind spot the student must personally cover — the narrator admitting its own construction is incomplete. Structurally, `lectures/grounded.md` goes further and names a permanent limit of the underlying technology the entire training runs on: "This isn't a bug that gets patched in the next release. It's the shape of the technology. Later models will fabricate less; they won't stop." A training built entirely on Claude sessions stating, on the page, that the product will never stop fabricating is about as far as point-of-view failure-ownership goes without becoming a disclaimer.

## Stance

Positions the training could lose a customer over, each quoted where it is defended by mechanism or scar rather than merely asserted:

1. **Splitting agents is usually wrong; default to one.** `lectures/when-to-split-an-agent.md`: "Inside a workflow, start with don't... The test that catches the bluff: can I write one prompt that produces the same quality of output? If yes, you didn't need to split. Business people who have just seen multi-agent work want to split everything. Resist it." Defended by mechanism (three named tests: access, dialect, stance) — a customer sold on "more agents = more value" could walk from this.
2. **Fancy tooling loses to plain text.** `lectures/compounding.md`: "Every fancier setup that promised to 'fix' this added a layer that the model had to work around... the simplest possible setup beats the fancy ones, because it respects what the model actually does well." Defended by mechanism (LLMs are strongest at reading/writing text; layers fight that).
3. **No such thing as a safety guarantee.** `lectures/practice-of-risk.md`: "Certainty is a fantasy you inherited... Accept the residual on record, or close the door. Those are the two options. 'Hope it doesn't happen' is not one." Defended by mechanism (non-determinism, instruction-set-as-attack-surface, emergent capability) — costs the training any customer who wants to be told the system is simply "secure."
4. **Don't sell the whole agent.** `exercises/share-your-work.md` / `personal-to-team.md`: "'Share the whole agent' is a vendor pitch and is NOT on the list." Held **against the training's own commercial interest**: a consultancy selling agentic transformation explicitly refuses the most sellable, biggest-ticket sharing shape and steers customers toward the narrowest thing that solves the actual job.
5. **The underlying model will keep fabricating.** `lectures/grounded.md`: "Later models will fabricate less; they won't stop." A position against the interest of any vendor (including Anthropic, whose product the training runs on) that would rather sell "wait for the next model."

## The pair

Stance and self-doubt are both present and load-bearing, not traded off against each other. The training holds hard positions (don't split, don't buy tooling, don't promise safety, don't sell the whole agent) while simultaneously naming, repeatedly and specifically, where its own taught machinery runs out (M3's unresolved doubt, M5's "Known limit" line, M6's flatlined judge, M4's named residual risk). This reads as **someone who has been there**: the confidence is earned by naming exactly where it stops, not asserted past the point the evidence supports, and not hedged into vendor-neutral mush either — every doubt is specific (a named blind spot, a named residual) rather than a general disclaimer.
