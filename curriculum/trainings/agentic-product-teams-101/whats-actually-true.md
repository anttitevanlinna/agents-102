# What's actually true?

## Big Idea

Agents keep working between sessions and overnight; you come back, read what they did and decide what to trust.

## What you arrive with

A product box, a bet written as hypotheses, a memory of your own material, and an agent that ran overnight on what your team agreed could go in.

## What You'll Learn

After this day, you will be able to:
- **Check** an overnight digest against the material you know best, and pick the doubt that matters most
- **Build** an opportunity solution tree as a team, with every branch keeping the name of who found it
- **Test** four ways of catching made-up claims on your own evidence, and keep the winner as a judge
- **Choose** one bet with evidence behind it, and make your own first piece of it in your craft
- **Write** the day's lessons into rules your agents follow, and set tonight's run against the bet

## Talk it through, laptops shut

Twenty minutes, no screens. Start from the cause your board ranked most likely.

- Does the bet survive it? If not, what would you change before Day 3 builds the first slice?
- Which part of the bet rests on the agents' work: a quote, a count, a branch Claude merged? Has your judge checked that part yet?
- What would each of you need to see on Monday to keep going?

If the bet does not survive, that is good work. Go back to the tree and choose again before you leave. Better now, with three pieces in hand, than after a sprint.

## Close

Laptops shut, one round, each of you in turn:

- Where did the agent get it wrong today?
- What do you now trust it with, and what not?
- What did you make that you could not have made on Day 1?

## Key Concepts

- Read the digest where you know the most; the line you trust least is where checking starts.
- The digest found what its brief asked it to look for. Ask the other way round too.
- Sketch alone before anything gets merged, and keep the name on every branch.
- The outcome at the root is what lets a merge choose instead of average.
- A judge is kept because it won on your own evidence, and it says what it can't see.
- "The agent got this wrong" costs nobody face, so say it early.
- Each of you made a piece of the bet in your own craft; together they test one thing.
- A mistake becomes a rule when it comes back, and examples of good sit beside the don'ts.

## Bring to Day 3

**Leave the next digest unopened until the room.** You read it together against the chosen bet when Day 3 opens, and a digest read alone is a digest read where you know least.

## Next

Tonight the agent reads your chosen bet and looks for what argues against it. On Day 3 you slice the bet by what you need to learn, write what good means in your own words, and put the first slice in front of five people who never saw your tree.
