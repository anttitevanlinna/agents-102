## Frame

**A — AE101.** Frame in one sentence: *agentic engineering is engineering — a closed-loop controller you steer with context, hold with checks, and compound on disk.* Three modules that only make sense through it:
- M3 the-loop-half-filled: "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller**... Cut the feedback signal (a test, a check, a read) and it drifts. Signal quality is part of the law: a flaky test is a closed loop that still drifts."
- M4 ironies-of-automation: "This is a 1983 argument, not an AI take. Lisanne Bainbridge, studying industrial automation..." — the lens makes the agent an automation problem with a literature.
- M5 what-packaging-is: "**A check is a position fix**. At a fix the wedge of possible states collapses to a point."
- Where it breaks, on the page: M5 the-gate-is-a-claim: "Sutton's **bitter lesson**... Today's right procedure, your gates and workflow, yours or the agent's, is superseded too." M5 what-packaging-is: "Whether this field ever settles into a real best practice is an open question. Either way, today's playbooks are **candidates**."
Every module reads through the lens and the lens names its own expiry → 95.

**B — A101.** Frame in one sentence: *an agent is a set of parts you decide one at a time — context first — and as the system takes over the passes, the human moves one level up to design the loop.* It is stated, and it recurs in nearly every module as "the full agent picture":
- M1 context-is-king: "In the full agent picture, this is the first piece: context. Later you add tools, goals, checks, boundaries, and loops."
- M4 practice-of-risk: "In the full agent picture, this is the boundary piece... More capability without boundaries is not progress. It is just more blast radius."
- M7 access-is-not-absorption: "In the full agent picture, this is the interface piece. It is the first one that has another person on the other side of it."
- M8 where-is-this-all-going: "The parts did not change. Context, a goal, tools, checks, a boundary, a loop... What changed is who assembles them."
- Where it breaks: M4 "Now the move is to give it less... Both moves are right" reverses the training's own accumulation; M8 "Which half of this is obsolete next year?... the people running this training do not know either."
Load-bearing in most modules, but it is a parts inventory more than a lens: M3's multi-agent material reaches it only through "other agents are part of the tool surface", and the "full agent picture" line often reads as a label stapled to a slide rather than the thing that generates the module. The frame is also announced before it is earned ("every useful thing in this training is built on this one idea", M1; "That's the point the whole training turns on", M2 compounding) → 76.

## Narrative

**A — AE101.** In five sentences: An engineer meets a machine that mirrors their stance, and learns to steer it on the near half of the map, where feedback is quick. Then the feedback goes quiet: they send a real task off un-packaged and it comes back wrong. **Turn:** "The un-packaged run was supposed to underdeliver. What came back is data, not blame." (M5 diagnose-and-resend). They package against the failure they actually read, re-send, and learn the gate itself is a claim. Then the author confesses the training's own making drifted the same way, and the kit compounds while the model rotates.
- M4 test-and-learn: "Session one goes now, un-packaged... Session two goes packaged, after you've read the return."
- M5 what-packaging-is: "An unchecked session arrives confident, and wrong... The success report comes from the wrong harbor."
- M6 story-of-module-6: "The agent I was working with had just finished writing those three modules. It still opened with the un-packaged shape."
The turn is lived, and the training's own failure is on the page as a coda; it is not the training's spine → 92.

**B — A101.** In five sentences: A leader feels context move and builds a site that is "yes, this is me" (joy). They build a memory that compounds, then a multi-agent system that does everything right. **Turn:** "Everything you just did is the move this training teaches, run properly. It still handed you something you cannot vouch for. Hold the doubt." (M3 three-minds-one-synthesis). Risk deepens the unease ("Would you bet your job on it being safe? Probably not." M4 practice-of-risk), and a benchmark on that same M3 briefing builds the rescue (M5 hallucination-bakeoff: "Your target is the ungrounded briefing from Module 3"; "Phase 5: Close, name the rescue"). The judge runs in a loop while they walk away, the human moves up, and the close turns the doubt on the room's own kernel: "nothing in the room checked it. Hold that doubt the way you held it then." (M8 where-is-this-all-going).
The mood arc is designed and lived, and the turn implicates the training's own method — close to the 100 anchor. Held back by recaps that tell the arc instead of letting it land (new-human-role: "It would need to sound like it came from your world. That was Module 1... That was Module 5") and by the M8 benediction that resolves what the lecture leaves open → 84.

## Stance

**A — AE101.** Positions it could lose a customer over, each defended:
- "A rule in context is not a rule in the output" — defended with a scar: "The rule was loaded into context. Claude used it twice in the third turn... four separate violations across four independent LLM instances. The grep pass caught each one." (M6 story-of-module-6)
- "The model has read the field... what it holds about your next run is a forecast." / "**A prediction is not a measurement.**" — defended by mechanism (weights frozen at a cutoff, the local run is undocumented until it happens) (M5 what-packaging-is).
- "Green is a claim about the check, not a fact about the work" — Goodhart mechanism: "The agent is an optimizer aimed straight at your gate... No malice needed." (M5 the-gate-is-a-claim)
- "A rule in memory that does not force is worse than no rule." (M6 story-of-module-6) and "Don't make general what you don't practice yourself." (M3 skills-from-the-frontier)
Against its own commercial interest: "This training stops short of the full system" (M2 how-instructions-grow) and "today's playbooks are candidates", which covers its own playbook. Not a stake in the sale, but a limit on the product → 92.

**B — A101.** Positions it could lose a customer over, each defended:
- "*Share the whole agent* is a vendor pitch" (M7 share-your-work) — defended by mechanism: "The agent file is a page of instructions. It is not where the work lives... They run it, get a generic answer, and quietly stop." and "the product being sold as a shared agent is always three of the four parts" (M7 access-is-not-absorption).
- "Certainty is a fantasy you inherited" — defended with three mechanisms (non-determinism, instruction-set attack surface, emergent capability) (M4 practice-of-risk).
- Multi-agent restraint: "Inside a workflow, start with don't... Splitting adds coordination cost" and "A whole module just showed you multi-agent works. Next Monday, you will be tempted to apply it to everything. Don't." (M3 when-to-split-an-agent); the training even admits "Four sessions for one question is more than the work strictly needs... The four is for the feeling." (M3 three-retrievers-one-curator)
- "the simplest possible setup beats the fancy ones... Every fancier setup that promised to 'fix' this added a layer that the model had to work around." (M2 compounding)
- Against its own commercial interest, on the page: "Expect it to cost you a feature somebody in your company wanted. It costs whoever sells you agents too, this training included: an agent that reaches less is a smaller thing to sell, and it is still the right call." (M4 practice-of-risk)
That last line meets the 100 anchor. Held back because several positions are asserted, not argued: "Nobody knows where agents are going. Not Anthropic, not your board" (M1 what-just-happened) has no mechanism or scar beside it; "Chat literally cannot do this" (M2 build-your-challenge-memory) is a claim of victory. And the M8 sponsor line "You are now agent builders... That's what you carry out of this room" reads as a pitch inside a lecture that refuses graduation → 86.
