## Let the agent say what it intends to do
<!--slide:i-intend-to-->

In David Marquet's intent-based leadership, the person closest to the work says "I intend to…" instead of asking permission. The default flips: "with intent, the default is action absent a veto whereas with permission the default is stasis absent approval." His site sums it up as giving control, not taking control. "I intend to" is level 5 of his seven-rung ladder, not the top.

The same move works with agents. Before a piece of work that matters, the agent states what it intends to do and why. A person keeps the call: let it go, change it, or stop it.

The team lead's part is the same with people and with agents: make clear what the team is trying to achieve, then listen to intentions instead of handing out tasks.
