## Frame

**A (AE101).** One sentence: agentic engineering is engineering, a control loop you close with checks and compound into a kit.
- M1 the-machine-you-just-met: "A check from outside the session resets the chain. A failing test does not care how confident the answer sounded."
- M3 the-loop-half-filled: "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller**... Cut the feedback signal (a test, a check, a read) and it drifts."
- M5 what-packaging-is: "**A check is a position fix**."
- Where the frame breaks, M5 the-gate-is-a-claim: "Green is a claim about the check, not a fact about the work." and "Today's right procedure, your gates and workflow... is superseded too."
- **Score 95.**

**B (A101).** One sentence: an agent is a set of decisions about what you give it and what you keep back, assembled piece by piece.
- M1 context-is-king: "Context is the first piece of the agent. Tools, goals, checks, boundaries, and loops come later and depend on it."
- M4 practice-of-risk: "You have given the agent more: context, memory, more stances. Now give it less."
- M6 new-human-role-in-the-loop: "Each was one decision about what you give the agent and what you keep back."
- Where it breaks, M8 where-is-this-all-going: "Which half of this is obsolete next year?"
- What holds it down: the module headlines do not all speak the frame.
  - The M1 Big Idea is about guardrails ("With the right guardrails...").
  - The M5 Big Idea is a method ("keep the winner").
  - The M3 headline ("Hire three agents to search...") is contradicted by its own lecture ("Start with don't... Single-agent is the default.", when-to-split-an-agent).
- **Score 81.**
