## Frame

**A (AE101): agentic engineering is engineering — a closed-loop controller you steer with context and checks, and a system that compounds.**
- M1 the-machine-you-just-met: "A check from outside the session resets the chain. A failing test does not care how confident the answer sounded."
- M3 the-loop-half-filled: "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller**. (Work) It acts, observes the result, and corrects. Cut the feedback signal ... and it drifts."
- M5 what-packaging-is: "**A check is a position fix**. At a fix the wedge of possible states collapses to a point."
- Breaks named: M5 the-gate-is-a-claim "Today's right procedure, your gates and workflow, yours or the agent's, is superseded too."; M6 agents-that-build-agents "The goal is giving the system the right information, and nobody has that part figured out yet."
Every module reads through it, and the frame says where it breaks. **95.**

**B (A101): an agent is a set of parts, each one a decision about what you give it and what you keep back, and you put them together one module at a time.**
- M1 context-is-king: "Context is the first piece of the agent. Tools, goals, checks, boundaries, and loops come later and depend on it."
- M4 practice-of-risk: "You have given the agent more: context, memory, more stances. Now give it less. More context makes a good answer likelier. Less access makes a bad answer smaller."
- M6 new-human-role-in-the-loop: "The full picture: model, context, tools, goal, loop, checks, boundary, interface. Each was one decision about what you give the agent and what you keep back."
- M8 where-is-this-all-going: "The parts hold; the model rotates ... The assembly is the durable half."
- Strain: M3's module Big Idea "Hire three agents to search. Three more to decide." vs its own lecture when-to-split-an-agent "Start with don't ... Single-agent is the default." M5's page Big Idea ("Run several quality checks ... keep the winner") is a method, not a part. M7 names where the frame breaks: access-is-not-absorption "An agent is context, a boundary, a set of checks and somebody who answers for what it does ... The fourth does not copy at all."
Load-bearing in M1/M2/M4/M6/M7/M8. The M3 and M5 headlines pull against it. **78.**

## Narrative

**A.** An engineer fixes a trivial bug in their own repo and learns that the machine mirrors them. They plan and author skills, then deliberately send a long task off un-packaged (M4 test-and-learn: "Session one goes now, un-packaged ... Session two goes packaged, after you've read the return"). It comes back wrong, and they read why (M5 reading-the-return: "The closing summary is not the artefact. The machine would rather produce something than admit nothing"). They package against their own failure and re-send. Then the training turns on itself (M6 story-of-module-6: "The agent I was working with had just finished writing those three modules. It still opened with the un-packaged shape."). The turn is lived, and it is the training's own failure. **93.**

**B.** A chat-using leader makes a generic site and adds context until it reads as theirs (M1 personal-site-with-guardrails: "The LLM didn't get better between Phase 1 and Phase 6. The context did."). They build a memory that compounds and a scheduled agent, then split the work across agents and are left unsure (M3 three-minds-one-synthesis: "You can't yet tell if the answer is right. That is expected. Name the doubt"; M3 debrief "The doubt stays. Hold it."). The unease deepens (M4 practice-of-risk: "You built something that works. Would you bet your job on it being safe?"). The rescue comes when they measure their own briefing against seeded fabrications and keep a judge (M5 hallucination-bakeoff: "Point at a row: *this is why I'm keeping this one*"). They loop it, then learn that sharing is not access. At the close the doubt turns back on the room (M8 where-is-this-all-going: "The kernel came out of the same move as your first briefing: agents read, agents argued, something chose, and nothing in the room checked it. Hold that doubt."). Three things weaken it: the M3 doubt sits in an optional line, M5's lecture announces the detectors before the student meets them, and M8's identity close is spoken by the sponsor. **80.**
