# From Personal to Team

## Big Idea

You cannot really share an agent. You can share its context, a skill, its output or an interface, and the one you pick is a strategy decision.

## Prework

Bring one example from your own organisation where people got access to something and did not use it. A licence rollout, a shared drive, a portal, an internal tool. Write two or three sentences on what was switched on and what never followed.

The example gives the closing lecture something real to land on. If you don't bring one, the module's main idea stays general when it could be about your own company.

## What You'll Learn

After this module, you will be able to:

- **Interview** a teammate for the job they are trying to get done, before you decide what to share
- **Choose** a sharing strategy based on what the teammate needs to get done, not on what your infrastructure happens to make easy
- **Identify** the absorption bottleneck that decides whether the teammate takes it in
- **Draft** a technical plan and a people plan side by side
- **Test** the switch assumptions and leave with one named conversation and one test

## Start here

Pick one teammate who could use what your agent produces. What would you want them to have: your folder, one of your skills, the morning output, or a way to ask the agent a question in Slack? Why that one?

Start a fresh session at `~/Documents/agents-101/`. The agent writes the sharing plan into `module-7/`, and `module-7/monday.md` holds the next move: one person, one question, one test.

The four sharing strategies:

1. **Share the context.** Your `memory/`, `sources/`, root `CLAUDE.md` and `style.md` travel. Teammates build their own agent on top of your curation.
2. **Share a skill.** One scoped capability, extracted as a skill file. Teammates plug it into their own agents.
3. **Share the output (push).** The agent runs on a schedule. Its output lands where the team already looks.
4. **Share an interface (pull).** The agent sits behind a bot, a mention, a form or an endpoint. Teammates ask in their own words when they need it.

## Key Concepts

- Start from the teammate's job. Your candidate competes with the way they get that job done today.
- Pick one of the four: context, skill, output or interface. Push delivers on a schedule. Pull waits until someone asks.
- The bottleneck worth solving is the one that explains why the other obstacles stay hard.
- Write the people plan next to the technical plan. A role with no name on it tells you who to talk to next.
- Test the switch, not the solution.
- Access is a switch someone flips. Absorption happens in other people's weeks, at their pace, for their reasons.
