## Frame

**A (AE101): 94.** Frame: *agentic engineering is engineering*, meaning feedback control, verification and compounding applied to a stochastic machine.
- M2 when-a-plan-is-good: "Push reach past what you can check and you have not delegated more. You are checking less." Plan review only makes sense as a calibration move.
- M3 the-loop-half-filled: "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller**… Cut the feedback signal (a test, a check, a read) and it drifts." The laws are named as the lens.
- M5 what-packaging-is: "**A check is a position fix**. At a fix the wedge of possible states collapses to a point." Packaging is control theory drawn as a sea passage.
- Where it breaks, partly: M5 the-gate-is-a-claim: "Sutton's **bitter lesson**: built-in human knowledge wins today and loses to the next model… Retire what the next model outgrows". M5 what-packaging-is: "Whether this field ever settles into a real best practice is an open question." The frame doubts its own playbook. It never says where *engineering* stops being the right lens.

**B (APT101): 88.** Frame: *building got cheap, deciding didn't* (Day 1 apt101-building-is-cheap: "Agents make the slow step cheap. The hard steps stay where they were.").
- Day 2 apt101-why-it-agreed: "A team that tests its bets gets to test more of them, sooner. A team that ships without asking whether it worked now ships more of it, faster". The digest turn only matters because of the frame.
- Day 3 apt101-slice-by-learning: "Specifying well is the harder half". Day 3 apt101-three-jobs-rewritten: "Your insight is the choice it leaves you with. Which outcome… Which customer… Which bet… Which good idea you say no to". All three days read through the frame.
- It names where it breaks, which is the 100 rung: Day 3 apt101-where-you-go-from-here: "This is where 'building got cheap, deciding didn't' stops being an edge on its own… What would change this training's mind: agents that start deciding well".
- What holds it below 90: the Day 2 framework slides attach the frame as a closing suffix instead of being generated by it. apt101-each-of-you-makes-something (Houde & Hill): "With agents, the finished look got cheap. Picking the one question a prototype should answer did not." apt101-widen-before-you-choose (1-2-4-All): "Merging the pile now takes an agent seconds. Choosing which idea survives it takes the same people it always did." Both slides would stand without the suffix, so the frame decorates them. The Klein slide is the counter-example where the frame does work: "a thing that works makes doubts harder to say. The build got cheap."

## Room

The exercises carry the story. The turn happens at the student's own laptop, then gets named.

**Strongest beats**
1. **apt101-read-the-digest, Phases 1 and 3.** The Day 1 look-for line is preserved ("writes the look-for line I wrote on Day 1… word for word, at the head of `team/<your-name>/doubts.md`") and read aloud beside the headline: "Does the headline say what your bet says? / Which sentence of yours asked for it?" The failure is produced by the student's own sentence and recognised before any lecture names it.
2. **apt101-catch-it-making-things-up, Phases 1 to 5.** Planted claims, four methods, a scorer, and a judge kept with "a *Known limit:* line". Then: "Now pick a summary nobody built to be caught… It found something in a summary nobody planted anything in." The check is earned on the student's own evidence, then turned on real work. Honourable mention: apt101-five-users Phase 2, "The confusion is the finding."

**Weakest beats**
1. **apt101-five-users, Phase 4.** "each of you reads your own Day 1 hypothesis… and says out loud the line in it the five users proved wrong. Your own line, not someone else's." This scripts a confession the five sessions may not support. If the users confirmed a hypothesis, the room is asked to invent a falsification. That is the reverse of the training's own rule: "Decide on what you agreed, not on how the result feels."
2. **apt101-write-what-good-means, Phase 2.** Ported A101 machinery (`module-6/eval-notes.md`, `module-6/runs/`, `generation-tactic.md`) where the trio stops acting: "Now step away from the screen." Twenty-five minutes of loop with no role beat. Borrowed-prompt seams also show elsewhere: apt101-what-goes-in has "Claude's prompt calls it your challenge. Here the challenge is the bet", and apt101-pick-the-outcome has "Claude's prompt treats `challenge.md` as your challenge".
