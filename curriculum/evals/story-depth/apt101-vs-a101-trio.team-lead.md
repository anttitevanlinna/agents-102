# Team lead: A vs B

## A, as I read it

This is the one that would finally show me what an agent *does*. It reads files, runs while I'm away, gets checked by a judge, writes memory it re-reads next time. I've only seen that in demos, and I'd leave knowing how it works. But it's built around me alone: my challenge, my memory, my morning agent, my security skill. My team only turns up in Module 7, as "a teammate" I interview, and by then I'm the builder handing things down. Eight modules of me getting good at it, and one of how anyone else takes it up.

- **Slides I'd remember on Monday:** (1) The Saimaa dinner: same question, the first exchange changes the answer. Simple, and it sticks. (2) Mata v. Avianca: "Is Varghese a real case?" "Yes." The missing check was organisational, not technical. I'd tell that one at lunch. (3) "Three parts copy, one does not": accountability has a name on it, and a name doesn't travel inside a folder. That's my job in one line.
- **Where I'd tune out:** The LinkedIn/StoryBrand personal site ("I'm the greatest colleague ever") makes me physically uncomfortable; nobody here writes about themselves like that. The four-windows choreography, which the training itself admits is "for the feeling". The proposal forum ending with the sponsor declaring "You are now agent builders". And the precision/recall scoreboard went on longer than I needed.

## B, as I read it

This one is about my actual team: me, my PO and our designer, deciding together what agents do in our work. It says plainly what I've privately worried about: when building is cheap, coordinating the build stops being my job, and what's left is keeping the team's choices clear and keeping it safe to say the agent got it wrong. The working agreement, "what agents do here, agreed together", is something I could bring to the team meeting as a draft and let them change. That's how change works here. I'd come away knowing less about how agents run under the hood than with A, and a lot of the middle is my PO's craft: Torres, Cagan, O'Reilly, the Mom Test, Nielsen's five users. Good material, but not mine, and I've sat through most of it in workshops already.

- **Slides I'd remember on Monday:** (1) "Agents get checked, people don't get watched." That's the sentence I need before anyone on my team hears "AI" and thinks "monitoring". (2) "The team lead's piece: what agents do here, agreed together": a working agreement, with Marquet pushing decisions to the people with the information. (3) "A faster feature factory is still a feature factory." Agents amplify how we already work; they don't fix it.
- **Where I'd tune out:** The product-discovery canon I've heard before (four risks, opportunity trees, the milkshake). The Saimaa dinner again, which is fine but basic. And the author quoting his own blog posts five times started to feel like a pitch. The honest ones landed ("used just a bit", "I make them faster now"), but five is a lot.

## Which one I'd go to

**B.** My job isn't to build agents. I left building on purpose. My job is to make sure my team changes how it works without anyone feeling watched, overruled or replaced, and B is the only one of the two that treats that as the main problem rather than the last module. It also gets the three of us into the same room working on the same bet, which matters more to me than any one of us getting individually fluent. "On Monday the team decides, not the three of you" is exactly right where I work; we'd lose people otherwise. And I trust it a little more for saying, against its own sale, that a team not talking to customers weekly should start there, not with agents.

**What A does better:** it actually teaches the machine. After A I could answer my engineers' "but how does it really work?" with something I'd done, not something I'd heard. B gives me a list of six parts; A makes me assemble them, break them, audit them (the GDPR/policy lens and "give it less" are more useful to me than anything on risk in B) and watch an eval loop run without me. If my problem were "I don't understand agents", A would win. My problem is "what is my team for now", and B answers it.
