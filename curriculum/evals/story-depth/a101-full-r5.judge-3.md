## Frame

**A — 93.** Frame: *agentic engineering is engineering: a closed loop you steer with context and checks, and compound from evidence.* Load-bearing in every module:
- M3 the-loop-half-filled: "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller**. ... Cut the feedback signal (a test, a check, a read) and it drifts."
- M5 what-packaging-is: "**A check is a position fix**. At a fix the wedge of possible states collapses to a point".
- M6 composing-the-workflow: "An **eval** is a measurement. ... One run tells you what happened. The measurement tells you what the system does."
- Where it breaks: M5 the-gate-is-a-claim: "Sutton's **bitter lesson**: built-in human knowledge wins today and loses to the next model. ... Retire what the next model outgrows". The frame names its own expiry.

**B — 84.** Frame: *what you give the agent, and what you keep back, decides what it does: at prompt, system, team and organisation scale.* Stated late and explicitly:
- M6 new-human-role-in-the-loop: "model, context, tools, goal, loop, checks, boundary, interface. Each was one decision about what you give the agent and what you keep back."
- M2 compounding: "Context shapes output, at system scale."
- M4 practice-of-risk: "You have given the agent more: context, memory, more stances. Now give it less." (the frame turns on itself)
- Where it breaks, M7 access-is-not-absorption: "Accountability is the part with a name on it, and a name does not travel inside a folder." and "Companies, even when they go agentic, work first and foremost on the human principle."
M3 (split/handoffs) and M8 (builder compounds) read as adjacent lenses more than cases of this one; the frame is assembled in M6 rather than carried from M1.
