# DevSecOps ticket automation at 1,000-person scale: operating topology OODA cycle — 2026-09-06 10:34

**Focus area:** Who should own context, policy, approval, and execution when a central DevSecOps team administers GitHub, AWS, and adjacent systems for a roughly 1,000-person software unit.
**Research mode:** Cross-platform pattern, starting from named builders and live product documentation.
**Why this focus:** The previous cycle established an `auto / ask / deny` frontier for coding actions. This round tests whether that idea survives a large, federated operating environment where the platform team cannot know every service's intended behaviour.

## Findings

### Current products separate central security coordination from repository-local remediation

**Sources:** https://docs.github.com/en/enterprise-cloud@latest/organizations/managing-peoples-access-to-your-organization-with-roles/managing-security-managers-in-your-organization — [vendor documentation]; https://docs.github.com/en/code-security/tutorials/manage-security-alerts/best-practices-for-participating-in-a-security-campaign — [vendor documentation]
**Dates:** live documentation checked 2026-09-06
**Agent level:** Company → team
**What:** GitHub's security-manager role gives a central team read access across repositories and write access to security alerts without making it the owner of every application change. Security campaigns turn the central concern into repository-specific work, while issues coordinate stakeholders and repository teams receive fix and test guidance.
**Evidence level:** L0 capability evidence.
**Key claims:** The available control surface already supports a federated split: central visibility, policy, campaign management, and routing; local ownership of code intent, tests, review, and deployment.

### Semgrep's current product design encodes repeated triage decisions as reviewable organizational memory

**Sources:** https://semgrep.dev/blog/2026/inside-the-ai-memories-security-teams-are-writing/ — [vendor press release]; https://semgrep.dev/blog/2026/introducing-semgrep-custom-workflows/ — [vendor press release]; https://github.com/semgrep/semgrep-policies-gitops/blob/main/README.md — [vendor documentation]
**Dates:** Braden Riggs, 2026-05-06; Vivek Khimani and Braden Riggs, 2026-03-18; repository checked 2026-09-06
**Agent level:** Team → company
**What:** Semgrep reports that a developer's dismissal context can become a suggested memory for administrator approval, after which it affects later scans. Its workflow pattern combines deterministic scanners and policy checks with AI reasoning, emits traceable intermediate outputs, and delivers outcomes into GitHub, Jira, Slack, or an API. Its GitOps reference keeps policies in YAML, dry-runs the semantic diff, applies only from `main`, detects concurrent change, and checks nightly for drift.
**Evidence level:** L0 vendor capability and aggregate product telemetry; no independent customer outcome.
**Key claims:** The important M9 pattern is not unrestricted self-learning. Local feedback becomes a candidate; a central owner promotes a scoped, versioned policy after review and backtest.

### The patch, not the ticket, is the current autonomy boundary

**Sources:** https://snyk.io/blog/remediation-agents-demystified/ — [vendor press release]; https://docs.github.com/en/code-security/concepts/code-scanning/autofix-for-code-scanning — [vendor documentation]
**Dates:** Snyk Team on Gérald Crescione, Ryan McMorrow, and Brendan Hann, 2026-08-19; GitHub live documentation checked 2026-09-06
**Agent level:** Team
**What:** Snyk says its naive “LLM against the backlog” attempt produced changes developers rejected most of the time; its current design adds security intelligence, rescanning, tests, and stops at a pull request. GitHub's agentic autofix likewise explores, edits, validates best-effort, and opens a pull request. Neither source makes the ticket's status a grant of merge or deployment authority.
**Evidence level:** L0 vendor-described method and capability. Vendor-reported outcome numbers are not independent evidence.
**Key claims:** The useful automated unit is a testable candidate change with provenance. Ticket updates are coordination; they are not proof that a finding is fixed or that a change is safe to execute.

## Operating Shape for a 1,000-Person Software Unit

1. **Central DevSecOps owns the doctrine:** finding schema, risk classes, routing rules, identity and role templates, reusable workflows/runbooks, evidence requirements, audit, and promotion of shared rules.
2. **Service teams own intent:** repository instructions, tests, CODEOWNERS, business correctness, deployment windows, and approval for changes whose consequences are service-specific.
3. **The agent owns preparation:** deduplicate, enrich, retrieve history, propose a classification, draft a patch or runbook invocation, assemble evidence, and escalate uncertainty.
4. **The control plane owns effect:** GitHub rules and AWS IAM/workflow policy determine whether a prepared action may merge or execute.

## What I Looked For But Did Not Find

No independently documented 1,000-person software unit publishes the complete operating model from cross-system finding through ticket, PR or runbook, verification, and closure. I found no common case schema shared by GitHub and AWS, no measured false-negative rate for agent triage at this scale, and no independent evidence that bottom-up “memories” are safely promoted across hundreds of repositories without rule drift or stale exceptions.

## Orientation

**The ticket is a case file, not a permission slip.** At this scale the platform team should automate evidence collection and routing centrally, while keeping semantic approval with the service owner and executable authority in separately administered controls.
