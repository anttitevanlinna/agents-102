## Frame

**A (AE101) — 94.** One sentence: *agentic engineering is engineering — feedback control, verification and compounding, with the human's judgement as the signal.* Three modules that only make sense through it:
- M2 · `lectures/when-a-plan-is-good.md`: "Push reach past what you can check and you have not delegated more. You are checking less." Key Concepts M2: "You don't have to execute a plan to know it's good. The skill is old; only the surface is new."
- M3 · `lectures/the-loop-half-filled.md`: "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller**... Cut the feedback signal (a test, a check, a read) and it drifts."
- M5 · `lectures/what-packaging-is.md`: "**A check is a position fix**. At a fix the wedge of possible states collapses to a point."
- Where it breaks, on the page: M5 · `lectures/the-gate-is-a-claim.md`: "Green is a claim about the check, not a fact about the work." and "Sutton's **bitter lesson**... Today's right procedure, your gates and workflow... is superseded too."
Short of 100 only because the frame is never said in one line the student can carry; the map does the carrying.

**B (APT101) — 90.** One sentence: *building got cheap, deciding didn't — so the outcome craft the post-it rationed finally gets its experiments, and the trio's job becomes the choice.*
- Day 1 · `lectures/apt101-building-is-cheap.md`: "Agents make the slow step cheap. The hard steps stay where they were." and "That is the vindication: the outcome craft finally gets the experiments it always asked for."
- Day 2 · `lectures/apt101-why-it-agreed.md`: "Agents amplify the way a team already works. They do not transform it... A faster feature factory is still a feature factory" — only makes sense through Day 1's feature-factory slide.
- Day 3 · `lectures/apt101-three-jobs-rewritten.md`: "When building took most of a team's weeks, leading meant rationing that capacity... When building is cheap, that rationing matters less."
- Breaks named: Day 3 · `lectures/apt101-where-you-go-from-here.md`: "This is where 'building got cheap, deciding didn't' stops being an edge on its own... What would change this training's mind: agents that start deciding well."
It reaches the 100 rung's content (the frame names where it breaks, and what would change its mind). It loses points for being announced in the first ten minutes (`building-is-cheap` comes before any exercise) and for Day 2's middle (tree, 1-2-4-All, Edmondson), which reads as good product practice more than as cases of the frame.

## Narrative

**A — 93.** In five sentences: An engineer learns that the agent mirrors them and runs a short loop that compounds into a rules file. They learn to read a plan and to own the delta on curated skills. They send a long task off un-packaged, and it comes back confident and wrong. Diagnosing that failure builds the packaging, and the re-send reads differently. Then the narrator shows his own module going wrong in the same ways, and the loop has no last turn. The turn is M4 · `exercises/set-the-markers-send-it-off.md`: "send it off plainly and you find out what you can't steer yet". It is named in M5 · `lectures/what-packaging-is.md`: "An unchecked session arrives confident, and wrong. ...The success report comes from the wrong harbor." The training's own failure is the turn's echo: M6 · `lectures/story-of-module-6.md`: "The agent I was working with had just finished writing those three modules. It still opened with the un-packaged shape."

**B — 86.** In five sentences: A trio arrives with its own product and the feature nobody asked for. They paint the box, write a bet that can lose, build memories through a door they agreed, and send an overnight agent off to read their material *against the bet*. The digest comes back agreeing with them, and the turn is finding out why: their own brief asked for agreement. They prune the tree with a judge they measured, make their pieces, and kill the bet in a pre-mortem before building. Five users then do things no digest could have said, and the bet is handed to the wider team still open. The turn is planted in an exercise. Day 1 · `exercises/apt101-send-it-off.md` asks for "what your material says about the bet, with the line it came from". It is named in Day 2 · `lectures/apt101-why-it-agreed.md`: "Open the brief you wrote for the digest agent... If it does, the agent did as asked." It pays off in Day 2 · `exercises/apt101-keep-and-run-tonight.md`: "tonight's brief asks the question the first digest never did: what argues against the bet." The second turn lands in Day 3 · `exercises/apt101-five-users.md`: "for each moment, say whether the digest said it, could have said it, or could not have".
The turn is the training's own design producing the failure, which is the 100 shape. It is held to the high 80s by pre-announcement: Day 1 · `lectures/apt101-it-runs-overnight.md` says "It looks for what your brief tells it to look for" and "Some of those lines will be wrong" before the run. The recognition also happens in a lecture, not in the read-the-digest exercise.

## Point of view

**A — 95.** The narrator is Antti, a practitioner who built this module with Claude and lists the session's misses. He shows in three places:
- M6 · `lectures/story-of-module-6.md`: "Five taste reversals from me on Claude's confident recommendations."
- Same file: "I drifted in every one of the ways this story just walked. I fixed what I caught. The loop caught what I missed."
- Same file: "The paraphrase I shipped as a quote."
Before M6 the voice is second person with scars in asides: M1 · `exercises/orient-and-introspect.md`: "Assume about 10% of what it says or does is made up." His own failure is on the page.

**B — 85.** The guide is Antti, a product/transformation leader who spent a year unsure which bets were right. He appears as guide, never hero, which matches the intended shape:
- Day 1 · `lectures/apt101-building-is-cheap.md`: "One of the first things we tried building was a local installer for developer tooling. It got working and it was used just a bit."
- Day 2 · `lectures/apt101-why-it-agreed.md`: "For most of my working life I wanted to be right... It gave me anxiety, and a feeling of insufficiency... Being wrong does not diminish you. Lack of effort to understand does."
- Day 3 · `lectures/apt101-from-us-to-the-team.md`: "We built good things. We failed to share them well." and `apt101-where-you-go-from-here.md`: "I still make mistakes. More than I'd like."
His own failure is on the page, which is the 100 rung's content, but each appearance is a quote card of two to four lines with no scene: no bet named, no moment when he found out. Between the cards the voice is a well-sourced second person that hands most of its authority to cited names (Cutler, Seiden, Torres, Bland, Fitzpatrick, Klein, Nielsen, Ries, Kniberg). The "I wanted to be right" passage is the only one with lived texture.

## Stance

**A — 92.** These are positions it could lose a customer over, each defended:
- "A prediction is not a measurement." The defence is the mechanism of the frozen, read-the-field model. M5 · `lectures/what-packaging-is.md`: "Ask for best practice and that is what answers: a well-read average... today's playbooks are **candidates**." This position works against selling a playbook.
- "A rule in context is not a rule in the output." It is defended with a scar, M6 · `story-of-module-6.md`: "Same rule, same rules file, same task, four separate violations across four independent LLM instances."
- The agent's agreement settles nothing. It is defended with the sycophancy mechanism. M1 · `exercises/fix-tests-first.md`: "The agent yields if you push hard enough, so its agreement settles nothing." M1 · `lectures/the-machine-you-just-met.md`: "Agreeable answers won the second round."
- Trust degrades the watcher. M4 · `lectures/ironies-of-automation.md`: "The more autonomy the agent earns, the worse a watcher you quietly become."

**B — 89.** These are positions it could lose a customer over:
- It argues against its own commercial interest, explicitly. Day 2 · `lectures/apt101-go-back-to-your-customers.md`: "So a team that does not talk with its customers every week should start there, before agents... That sells less of this training. It is still where to start." This meets the 100 anchor, on the page.
- "A faster feature factory is still a feature factory." The mechanism, "Agents amplify the way a team already works. They do not transform it.", is lived through the digest that agreed (Day 2 · `apt101-why-it-agreed.md`).
- Fabrication is permanent. Day 2 · `lectures/apt101-fluent-is-not-true.md`: "This isn't a bug that gets patched in the next release. It's the shape of the technology. Later models will fabricate less; they won't stop."
- "Share the agent" does not work. Day 3 · `lectures/apt101-from-us-to-the-team.md`: "The page was never where the work lived." and "Access is a switch somebody flips... Absorption happens in other people's weeks."
- The door costs something. Day 1 · `exercises/apt101-what-goes-in.md`: "It will cost a source somebody wanted in. That is the door working. Anyone may say no to a source, and a no needs no defending."
Several positions are defended by citation rather than mechanism or scar: the five-user rule, pre-mortem +30%, 1-2-4-All. That keeps it below A's 92 on defence, despite the higher against-interest moment.

## Room

The exercises mostly carry the story, so the turn is lived. The trio's own Day 1 brief causes the Day 2 failure, and the Day 2 rule changes are run again that night. Ordering mostly holds for the main turns. Where it slips, the lecture names the move before the room does it: three lectures open Day 1 before the box; `apt101-each-of-you-makes-something.md` gives "Imagine it already failed" before `apt101-imagine-it-failed.md`; `apt101-safe-to-say-its-wrong.md` comes before the bake-off; `apt101-slice-by-learning.md` comes before map-the-story.

**Strongest beats**
1. **Day 1 · `exercises/apt101-paint-the-product-box.md`.** "Same model, five boxes. The first came from your public page. The last came from three people who know the product... Each of you can point at the line on the final box that came from you." The trio lives the context-steers lesson before `apt101-only-what-you-tell-it.md` names it ("Same words. Different answer."). The cold read ("quote the one line only this product could print and the most generic line") hands the training's doubt to the room.
2. **Day 3 · `exercises/apt101-five-users.md`.** "One pattern to watch: after the second session, someone wants to explain the slice to the next user before they start. Don't. The confusion is the finding." Then: "for each moment, say whether the digest said it, could have said it, or could not have". It is the payoff of the Day 1 digest, kept on purpose ("digest-day1.html"), and the decision is read "against the signal you agreed before building, not against what would feel better now." (A close runner-up is Day 2 · `apt101-catch-it-making-things-up.md`: "a scorer measures which one caught what, and the winner becomes the check you keep, with a note of what it can't see.")

**Weakest beats**
1. **Day 2 · `exercises/apt101-read-the-digest.md`.** This is the hinge of the narrative, but the exercise has the student mark "the one line you trust least... If your doubt lands on a typo, look again." It never has them open `morning.md` and find the sentence that asked for agreement. That recognition is left to a lecture ("Open the brief you wrote for the digest agent", `apt101-why-it-agreed.md`), so the turn is told rather than lived at its sharpest point.
2. **Day 3 · `exercises/apt101-take-it-to-the-team.md`.** It runs on borrowed prompts with a translation layer: "Where Claude says *teammate*, read *your wider team*. Where it says *candidate*, read *the way of working*." It reads files from `module-7/`. The persevere/pivot/stop call just made with five users enters only as post-its ("the designer and the product owner add what the five users showed you"), so the bet's story goes quiet in the last exercise. (Day 2 · `apt101-pick-the-outcome.md` has the same translation smell: "Claude's prompt treats `challenge.md` as your challenge; yours points at the bet".)
