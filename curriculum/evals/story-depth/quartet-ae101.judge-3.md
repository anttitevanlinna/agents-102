## Frame

**One-sentence frame:** The agent mirrors and runs exactly as far as what you have expressed — your stance, your rules, your checks — and everything the training teaches is how to write more of yourself down, correctly, so the machine has something worth mirroring.

Stated at the very open, M1's `painting-the-picture-with-the-llm.md`: "The LLM mirrors your stance... Your stance is the ceiling." Restated with mechanism at M1's close (`the-machine-you-just-met.md`): "It mirrors because mirroring was rewarded; the machine amplifies whatever posture you bring, and your stance is the ceiling by construction." And restated as the training's very last frame, M6's `agents-that-build-agents.md`: "The agent cannot read your mind... What you can express is how far the agent runs. It stops exactly where the writing stops and your judgement takes over." The frame is the training's spine, not a decoration — three modules that only make sense through it:

- **M1** teaches the frame directly: two fresh sessions given different facts answer the same dinner question differently, because "context is whatever you have told the session so far... All of it colors what comes next" (`the-wizard-move.md`).
- **M2's `when-a-plan-is-good.md`** applies the frame to plans: a plan "was written from a partial read of the codebase, and your instinct is not a check on it" — the plan mirrors the read you gave it, and pushing back is the only way to widen what it mirrors.
- **M5's `the-gate-is-a-claim.md`** applies the frame to verification itself: "Green is a claim about the check, not a fact about the work" — the gate, too, only ever expresses what you built into it.

**Where the frame breaks, stated on the page.** `ironies-of-automation.md` (M4) is the frame's own stress test: if your stance is the ceiling and the machine mirrors you, what happens to your stance while you supervise it rather than do the work? "Trust and vigilance move in opposite directions... The trust is deserved. The watching still has to be engineered." The backing block goes further than most curricula would dare in public: it documents a targeted hunt for evidence that using the agent erodes the engineer's own skill, and reports finding none — "not one first-hand report of losing the ability to write or read code" — while also reporting strong evidence for the adjacent claim (vigilance erosion) that the frame does predict. The frame is stated, tested against itself, and the module says plainly which half held and which half is still open.

## Narrative

Five sentences, with the turn quoted:

An engineer opens a session with an agent that mirrors whatever they bring to it, and fixes a trivial bug to prove the loop works. Across two more modules they build real trust — a plan pushed back on twice, a threat model, a test-strategy skill authored from their own codebase — and start to believe the system holds. Then they send a real task off unsupervised, and it comes back having declared victory on the part it managed while the part it quietly dropped goes unmentioned: **"Tests pass, the summary is confident, and the piece it quietly dropped goes unmentioned. You find this one on return, not during"** (`what-keeps-a-long-running-session-going.md`). They build a verifier against exactly the failure they read — and the very module that teaches them to build it also tells them the verifier is no more trustworthy than the thing it grades, a claim the training's own maker admits happened to him while writing this same curriculum: "The rule was loaded. The check never ran" (`story-of-module-6.md`). The training ends without resolving it — "There is no last turn. Each session surfaces the next gap" (`agents-that-build-agents.md`) — because the turn was never that the agent fails once; it is that the failure recurs at every scale, including the one the trainer just lived through building the lesson.

## Point of view

The narrator is mostly the trainer's collective "the training" voice, second person to the student — but it breaks into an explicit, signed first person exactly once, and that break is where the training's strongest claim to honesty lives.

Three places the narrator shows itself:
1. **`story-of-module-6.md`**, in full: "I am going to tell you how this module got made. Not the pedagogy of it. The actual session. What I tried, what drifted, what the rules caught, what the rules missed." Signed "Antti."
2. **`ironies-of-automation.md`'s closing address**: "So when the next task lands clean, ask the plain question first: when did you last do this kind of work by hand?" — spoken as someone who has personally watched vigilance erode, not as an abstract warning.
3. **The maintainer backing blocks throughout** (not student-facing, but authored in the same voice) repeatedly narrate the training's own uncertainty in the first person of its method — e.g. `the-gate-is-a-claim.md`'s stance block admits the training's central verification claims rest on one named practitioner (Husain) and nothing broader.

The narrator's own failure is on the page, not implied. `story-of-module-6.md` names, in detail: a banned word leaking four separate times across four independent LLM instances despite the rule sitting loaded in every one's context; a flattering "we live what we teach" framing the agent proposed and the maintainer rejected as false; evals skipped until he caught the omission; a paraphrase shipped as a verbatim quote that drifted from what a named practitioner actually said, caught only by a source-verification pass; a closing lecture that contradicted its own opening promise, missed by four LLM writes and one review pass, caught only by a fifth, adversarial read. The chapter's thesis is stated as plainly as a narrator's own scar gets stated: "A rule in context is not a rule in the output... Taste closes the gap."

## Stance

Positions the training could lose a customer over, each with where it is defended:

1. **Structured plans should be distrusted precisely because they look decided.** Defended by mechanism, not assertion: "A 7-item plan with headers looks like a decision even when it's a draft" (`when-a-plan-is-good.md`), backed by the delegation-frontier model — "Push reach past what you can check and you have not delegated more. You are checking less."
2. **A passing verifier is not proof of anything.** This is a position that could unsettle a customer who wants to hear "AI QA is solved": "Green is a claim about the check, not a fact about the work... A gate nobody has verified is a gate trusted on vibes" (`the-gate-is-a-claim.md`). Defended by mechanism — three named failure modes (miscalibration, gaming, lucky sample), each with an independence-based countermove, plus Goodhart's law and Deming's "tampering" applied by name.
3. **Autonomy earned through clean runs quietly degrades the engineer's own vigilance** — an uncomfortable claim to sell alongside "let the agent run unsupervised." Defended by a scar, not a hedge: Osmani's self-implicating quote, cited verbatim, "You didn't review the code. You ratified it. The surrender was the absence of a decision" (`ironies-of-automation.md`).
4. **Held against the training's own commercial interest**: the curriculum states outright that its own taught packaging pattern (reference + plan.md + verifier) is not settled best practice and will not remain correct: "Whether this field ever settles into a real best practice is an open question. Either way, today's playbooks are **candidates**" (`what-packaging-is.md`), and closes the module on Sutton's bitter lesson applied to the training's own procedures: "Today's right procedure, your gates and workflow, yours or the agent's, is superseded too" (`the-gate-is-a-claim.md`). A training willing to tell a paying customer that what it just taught them will be obsolete is arguing against its own restocking value.

## The pair

Stance and self-doubt sit together throughout, and the pairing is the clearest tell that this reads as someone who has been there rather than a hedge or a pitch. The training holds firm, mechanism-backed positions (gates are claims, autonomy erodes vigilance, structure is persuasive) while simultaneously documenting, in the open, where its own evidence runs out — the atrophy half of the automation-irony claim ("found nothing"), the un-sourced two-thirds of its own three-lens vocabulary (`reading-the-return.md`: "goal drift" and "plausible-but-wrong" are house terms, not field vocabulary, and the training says so), and the maker's own drift while writing the very module that teaches drift-catching. **Both, and it reads as someone who has been there** — the doubt is never generic modesty; it attaches to a named mechanism each time, and the strongest doubt (`story-of-module-6.md`) is a first-hand account with dates, numbers, and a signature.
