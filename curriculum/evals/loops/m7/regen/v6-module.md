# From Personal to Team

## Big Idea

You can't really share an agent. You share its context, a skill, its output or an interface, and the choice between them is a strategy decision.

## Prework

Bring one example from your own organisation of a tool people were given access to and then did not use. Two or three sentences are enough: what was rolled out, who could reach it, and what people kept doing instead.

You know your own case well enough to test this module's central claim against it.

## What You'll Learn

After this module, you will be able to:

- **Interview** for the job a teammate is trying to get done before you choose what to share
- **Choose** one of four sharing strategies against that teammate's outcome
- **Identify** the absorption bottleneck that decides whether the teammate takes the candidate up
- **Draft** a technical plan and a people plan side by side
- **Test** the assumptions behind the switch and name the first person to talk to

## Key Concepts

- Start from the teammate's job, not from what you built. A candidate gets hired only if it does that job better than what they use now.
- Four sharing strategies: share the context, share a skill, share the output (push), share an interface (pull). Each has its own coordination cost, trust requirement and engineering lift.
- Push and pull deliver a running agent's value without the teammate running one. Most teams want one or the other.
- The absorption bottleneck is the obstacle that keeps several others hard, and it is rarely the first technical limit you see.
- The people plan carries as much weight as the technical plan. An unassigned role is the next question to ask, not a blank to fill in.
- Test what would have to be true for the teammate to switch, not whether the solution works.
- Access is a switch someone flips. Absorption happens in other people's weeks, at their pace, for their reasons.
