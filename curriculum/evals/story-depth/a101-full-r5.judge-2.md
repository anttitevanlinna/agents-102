## Frame

**A (AE101): agentic engineering is engineering: control comes from checks and feedback, not from reading, and the system compounds.**
- M1 the-machine-you-just-met: "A check from outside the session resets the chain."
- M3 the-loop-half-filled: "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller** ... Cut the feedback signal ... and it drifts."; "Reading was never the control."
- M5 the-gate-is-a-claim: "Green is a claim about the check, not a fact about the work." M5 only makes sense through the control lens: the gate itself becomes the next thing under control.
- Where it breaks: M5 what-packaging-is: "Whether this field ever settles into a real best practice is an open question. Either way, today's playbooks are **candidates**."; M6 agents-that-build-agents: "nobody has that part figured out yet." → **93**

**B (A101): every module is one decision about what you give the agent and what you keep back.**
- M1 context-is-king: "That is context. You steer your agent with context."
- M4 practice-of-risk: "You have given the agent more: context, memory, more stances. Now give it less. ... More context makes a good answer likelier. Less access makes a bad answer smaller." Module 4 only reads this way through the frame.
- M6 new-human-role-in-the-loop: "Each was one decision about what you give the agent and what you keep back."
- Where it breaks: M7 access-is-not-absorption: "Companies, even when they go agentic, work first and foremost on the human principle." The frame is load-bearing in M1, M2, M4, M5 and M6, but it is stated whole only in M6. M3 (splitting) and M7 (sharing) read through it only loosely. → **80**

## Narrative

**A.** An engineer learns to steer one short loop, then sends a long task off unpackaged and it fails. They read the failure as data, package the task against it and send it again. Then they learn that even the gate is a claim. The turn comes when the author's own module-building session repeats every failure the training warned about. Turn: M4 set-the-markers-send-it-off "send it off plainly and you find out what you can't steer yet" → M5 diagnose-and-resend "The un-packaged run was supposed to underdeliver. What came back is data, not blame." → M6 story-of-module-6 "The agent I was working with had just finished writing those three modules. It still opened with the un-packaged shape." The turn is the training's own failure. → **94**

**B.** A chat user makes a site that is clearly theirs and feels the joy of it (M1). They build a memory and a team of agents, and get an answer they can't trust: "You can't yet tell if the answer is right. That is expected. Name the doubt; don't fix it here." (M3 three-minds-one-synthesis). The unease deepens: "The risk remains. That is expected. Name it, accept it on record, and stop there." (M4 audit-your-agent). The rescue: "run it once on your `./crux.md` answer, the one Module 3 left you doubting" (M5 hallucination-bakeoff, "Close, name the rescue"). Then comes the leverage: "Your agent just improved your agent." (M8 extend-your-system). The doubt is lived in M3 and rescued in M5, and that arc is the story's spine. The training's own failure appears twice, "I drifted too" (M6) and "The decision never came" (M7), but both arrive in lectures, not in the student's run. M8's identity line is scripted. → **82**
