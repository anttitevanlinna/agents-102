## getting-going.md (Module 1) + its lectures/exercises

- Big Idea plants the compounding idea directly: "Your first session should leave something behind that the next one can use. Otherwise you have a clever assistant and no compounding." (getting-going.md)

### painting-the-picture-with-the-llm.md (lecture, opener)

- Two frontiers posed as open questions, explicitly not answered: "can that setup learn faster than you can write things down?" and "does it learn the right things?" — genuine forward-looking questions, unresolved by design (maintainer notes: "The training answers by accretion through M1–M6").

### the-wizard-move.md (lecture)

Complicates nothing yet but is the MECHANISM underlying "mirrors your stance" — connects to painting-the-picture's stance claim. "The move is loading the right context before the question."

### orient-and-introspect.md (Ex1)
- Plants: "the context window is not your codebase" and "The account is a reconstruction, not ground truth... Assume about 10% of what it says or does is made up." This is the FIRST COMPLICATION of the self-report idea from getting-going.md's Key Concepts — turns a stated claim into a lived, quantified-ish (hedged) working prior. Quote: "The agent can introspect on what it did and why, including what it chose not to read" immediately followed by the caution to spot-check it.
- Governor handed over here: "spot-check it. Quote a specific file or function back and ask Claude to confirm it read what it claims."

### fix-tests-first.md (Ex2)
- Plants/complicates the self-report idea again: "The agent runs the loop; you read the result... The agent yields if you push hard enough, so its agreement settles nothing." — complicates trust-the-agent's-account further: even pushback-agreement is suspect.
- Plants a second idea: "There is always a next plausible answer, because the LLM will always try to find what you asked for... when to stop digging is your call." Candidate seed: the model will always produce a plausible-sounding answer regardless of truth — this pairs with self-report-is-hypothesis as the same underlying machine-nature, different surface (diffs/root-cause digging vs. self-reports of reading).

### close-the-ticket.md (Ex3)
- Extends compounding thread sideways: "Anything can be inspected and reverse-engineered with an LLM... Ask Claude to study [it]. Extract a `.md` file. Use that file as context in the next agent." This generalizes the compounding idea from "your session's rules" to "any undocumented system."

### compound-and-close.md (Ex4)
- Payoff of the CLAUDE.local.md compounding thread: "let Claude review the whole session and write your `./CLAUDE.local.md` from session evidence... You are not drafting from a blank page." Governor: "Quote the specific session moment back at Claude" when pushing back on the summary — same self-report-verification governor as Ex1, now applied to the rules-file-writing moment itself (retro confabulation risk named explicitly).
- Escape hatch reinforces self-report skepticism once more: "If a line it wrote reads generic, ask which session moment earned it; with no moment to point at, have Claude take the line back out of the file."

### the-machine-you-just-met.md (closer lecture)
- PAYOFF moment for multiple threads at once:
  - Self-report/mirroring: "The LLM is trained twice... Agreeable answers won the second round... The field calls the result **sycophancy**... The agent's report of its own success is the same kind of output. 'Fixed, and the tests pass' comes from the same machine that learned agreeable answers win. The report is a hypothesis to check, not ground truth." This is the MECHANISM payoff for the self-report-is-hypothesis thread planted in getting-going.md and complicated through Ex1/Ex2/Ex4.
  - Scrollback-is-abstraction: "The agent's chat is an abstraction of the work, necessarily... It is the session's architecture diagram: what was meant, not what runs... A state question... is answered from a fresh read, not from recall." — ties back to orient-and-introspect's "context window is not your codebase."
  - Error cascade: "Enough mostly-right steps stacked without a check, and the end of the chain is mostly wrong... A check from outside the session resets the chain... That is why the failing test came before the fix." — this explicitly re-narrates Ex2's tests-first move as an instance of a general principle, a genuine "payoff earned after the move," per the module's own maintainer note ("names the machine tendencies the exercises already surfaced").
  - Compounding payoff: "Kieran Klaassen calls this **compound engineering**: work produces evidence; evidence improves the system that does the next work." Then complicates/broadens: "Nothing on that list is a rules file... Features teach the system new capabilities... Bug fixes eliminate entire categories of future bugs... The file you wrote today is the smallest unit that qualifies... The loop is the shape. The bug today was the excuse." This is a genuine complication: the rules file the student just wrote is reframed as the SMALLEST instance of a much bigger idea, not the idea itself.
  - Governor for mirroring: "a stated doubt, a standard the answer has to clear, a question that asks for what is wrong before what is right."
  - Governor for output shape: "Tell the LLM what output you want: a ranked list first, detail on request."

M1 close: rich, multi-threaded module. Three candidate big learnings clearly seeded+complicated+paid off already: (1) self-report/agent-account is a hypothesis not ground truth (mirroring/sycophancy mechanism), (2) compounding — session evidence becomes disk artifact that changes the next session, generalizing past the rules file, (3) error/chain stacking needs an outside check (tests-first as instance of a general principle). Candidate headline-only (not yet developed further): "stance is the ceiling" / mirror-your-posture framing from painting-the-picture — overlaps heavily with sycophancy thread, watch if it develops independently or just gets folded in.

## plan-mode-done-right.md (Module 2) + lectures/exercises

- Big Idea: "The first plan is plausible. Plausible isn't good enough, and you are the one who can tell the difference." Candidate big learning #4 seed: PLAUSIBLE ≠ GOOD — structure/fluency of agent output is persuasive independent of correctness. This directly echoes/extends "structure is persuasive" and connects to M1's self-report thread (an agent's account SOUNDS right regardless of whether it IS right) — likely the SAME underlying idea wearing a new face (plan quality vs. self-report trustworthiness), worth tracking as either a sub-thread of learning #1 or its own learning.

### the-whole-map.md (lecture)
- Container/map lecture: six phases (Intent, Context, Work, Verification, Absorption, Outcome). Mostly furniture-setting, not itself a complicating beat — "The phases are places, not a pipeline." Plants vocabulary reused later (M4's "far half", M6). Not a big-learning payoff moment itself; establishes scaffolding.

### when-a-plan-is-good.md (lecture)
- Complicates the "plausible ≠ good" thread directly: "Structure is persuasive. A 7-item plan with section headers and bold text looks like a decision. It's often just a draft formatted like a decision." + "Reasonableness passes for rightness... Judge what the steps add up to, not how each one reads." + "You already agree with it... your instinct is not a check on it."
- Plants governor: five criteria for a good plan (specific file list, early runnable slice, verification step that could fail, named assumptions, list of non-goals).
- New idea: "What the plan doesn't decide, the agent decides mid-run... A wrong call propagates across files... you will not notice." — complicates the earlier "review the plan" idea with a cost-of-not-reviewing mechanism.
- Plants delegation-frontier model: "Reach is how much you delegated... Calibration is whether your trust in what came back was earned by a check you have verified... Push reach past what you can check and you have not delegated more. You are checking less." Candidate big learning #5 seed: reach vs. calibration — trust must be EARNED by a check, not assumed. Governor: "The ceiling is not fixed... building a better check buys you more than approving a bigger plan."

### push-back-on-the-plan.md (Ex1, M2)
- Payoff/governor for "plausible ≠ good": two-read structure (human push-back + agent walk-down) as the concrete governor. "The agent finds; you judge." (bolded law) — this is a genuine NEW governor, not just a restatement: splits labor between agent (breadth) and human (judgment).
- Names the pattern AFTER the student does it: "Plan-mode approval inflation is the thing this pairing defeats: structured plans get rubber-stamped because they look like decisions." — textbook earned-not-announced structure (per exercise's own maintainer note: "Attribution at P5 is terse... the label the exercise hands them after they've already defeated it").
- Complication: re-softening on regeneration — even after pushback, the agent may revert to vague language: "if the vagueness survived, push back again." Extends the self-report-is-hypothesis skepticism to PLAN REVISIONS, not just initial claims.

### extract-the-task-shaping-rule.md (Ex2, M2)
- Extends compounding thread: rules can live in many places (table of CLAUDE.md/rules/memory/skills/roll-your-own), generalizing M1's CLAUDE.local.md-only view. Governor: "the location is reversible... Pick by the mechanism you want."
- Reinforces generic-rule skepticism (same shape as M1's Ex4 retro-confabulation watch): "The rule that arrives generic... is the one to rewrite in your words, or reject."
- New sub-idea (Boy Scout rule): compounding isn't only about rules-files — "Anything the plan tripped on is another [way to compound], and it fixes things for everyone with nothing loaded." This broadens "compounding" beyond artifacts-that-load to direct fixes of the environment (docs, ADRs, tests) — a genuine complication of the M1 compounding thread: not every compounding action requires a rule file at all.

### where-the-rule-could-live.md (lecture, M2)
- Extends compounding/rules thread further outward: "The file is steady; the agent moves... Agents build agents. An agent can help author the file. A later agent reads those rules... The first agent's output becomes the second agent's context." This is a genuine widening: from "you write a rule for your own next session" (M1) to "files become durable artifacts multiple different agents/runtimes consume" (M2 close) — sets up M2's payoff for compounding as an idea bigger than personal habit.

### how-instructions-grow.md (lecture, M2 close)
- Payoff for "rules have a ceiling" — new complication of the compounding thread: "The ceiling comes sooner than you'd guess: every always-loaded rule rides into every session, billed against the same attention that does the work. Past a point the context bloats... The work starts passing your rules and missing your point." This complicates getting-going.md's early "everyone sees how it will bloat almost immediately" hint — now given a mechanism and a cure: "Keep `./CLAUDE.md` and `./CLAUDE.local.md` lean: principles, with the why attached. Move the rest into skills, loaded only when the work calls for them."
- Second-loop governor named: Argyris's double-loop learning — "The first loop corrects the action. The second questions the governing rule or assumption that shaped the action." Ties explicitly back to Key Concepts bullet in plan-mode-done-right.md.
- New idea, NOT yet clearly tied to a thread: "Prohibitions stop; taste steers" — "Prohibitions are weak instructions: each one stops a single behaviour and puts nothing in its place... The positive form is also shorter... it generalizes... Capture the good example beside the corrections." Interesting but appears headline/local — watch whether this returns later or stays a one-off.

M2 close: continues and complicates M1's threads (self-report skepticism → plan plausibility skepticism; compounding → rules ceiling + multi-agent file consumption) and adds a clean new one (reach vs. calibration / delegation frontier) with its own governor. "Find is easier than judge" and "plausible ≠ good" look like a single throughline connecting M1 and M2 — tentatively naming it: MACHINE OUTPUT IS FLUENT BY DEFAULT AND MUST BE INDEPENDENTLY CHECKED, first taught on self-reports (M1), then on plans (M2). Will keep tracking through M3-M6 for further complication/payoff.

## run-the-first-experiment.md (Module 4) + lectures/exercises

- Big Idea: "Prep your first long session. The session is how you find out what's missing." Sets up the shift from "watch it happen" (M1-M3) to "let it run unwatched" — a genuine new phase, complicating trust/calibration (M2) and control-is-interrogation (M3) by removing the option of moment-to-moment interrogation.

### the-far-half.md (lecture, opener)
- Names new phases: Verification, Absorption, Outcome — explicitly frames the shift: "So far you sat inside every loop... When the agent runs for an hour without you watching, that quick feedback goes quiet." Direct complication of M3's "control is interrogation" — interrogation assumed availability; now availability is gone.
- Forward-looking, genuinely open question, not a prediction: "One question to carry into the session: when the agent takes a hundred steps alone, what makes you trust the result at the end?" Explicitly left unanswered (maintainer notes confirm this is deliberate, payoff comes M5/M6).

### the-agent-loop.md (lecture)

### test-and-learn.md (lecture)
- Names the experimental stance explicitly: "Every send-off from here on is an experiment... the result is data." Governor-shaped: "You don't need to get it right first time. Read the result and decide what to change next." ### walk-and-send-off.md + set-the-markers-send-it-off.md (Ex1-2, M4)
- Extends compounding/gap thread: "you find the thin spots before the agent does" — gap analysis of CLAUDE.md/rules/ADRs/skills against a real task. ### what-keeps-a-long-running-session-going.md (closer lecture)
- Names backpressure: "Flow engineering calls the push from a slower downstream stage backpressure." NEW named concept, direct complication of "control is interrogation" — when generation outruns judgment, the human becomes the bottleneck/gate.
- Names four concrete failure modes of unattended sessions (stops-and-waits, declares-done-on-the-part-it-managed, blocks-on-something-that-never-returns, spends-the-whole-run-on-one-obstacle) — this is a genuine NEW complication of M1's self-report skepticism: not just "the agent's account may be wrong" but specific, nameable shapes of HOW an unwatched agent goes wrong. Governor: "check whether there was a way for it to uncover that. Usually there was" (for the stops-and-waits case) — a new, specific governor.

### ironies-of-automation.md (closer lecture, M4 close)
- Major new complication, arguably the sharpest twist in the whole training so far: "Trust and vigilance move in opposite directions... The more tasks the agent lands cleanly, the worse you get at catching the one that doesn't." This directly INVERTS and complicates the trust-building arc of M1-M3 (earn trust → build skills → compound). It says: the more successfully you compound trust, the WORSE your own vigilance becomes — a genuine counter-voice against the training's own throughline, stated ON the student surface (self-challenging, not just theoretical). Bainbridge 1983 (skill atrophy from automation) applied via analogy (explicitly flagged in backing as an inference, not evidenced for agents specifically, but the lecture doesn't overclaim — stays scoped to industrial automation on slide 1, extends via named practitioner reports on slide 2).
- Explicit governor: "when did you last do this kind of work by hand?" — closing question, the module's last word.
- This is the training's clearest instance of "self-challenging" (per the judging rubric): the training explicitly doubts its own core selling point (agent delegation/trust-building) on the student surface.

M4 close: continues and sharpens "control is interrogation" (M3) into "traces are data" / experimental stance (test-and-learn), and adds backpressure as a named mechanism for when generation outruns judgment. Most importantly, ironies-of-automation introduces a genuine COUNTER-VOICE to the entire trust-building arc — trust and vigilance move in opposite directions, meaning increasing competence in the training's own terms increases risk of missed failure. This is the closest thing found so far to explicit self-doubt about the training's core method. Tracking into M5/M6 to see if this counter-voice gets a governor/resolution or stays as an open tension.

- Extends this into "The optimum is local, and it moves" — ties the whole module's method (A/B test on your own repo) into a broader claim: published practices are hypotheses, only local experiments promote them to "tested-here." Governor: "The engineer decides: what counts, what the evidence means, what earns promotion into durable practice."

### the-gate-is-a-claim.md (lecture, M5 closer)
- MAJOR complication/self-challenging moment: "The gate is a claim too... Green is a claim about the check, not a fact about the work." This turns the training's own newly-built solution (verifiers/gates) into ANOTHER thing that needs skepticism — direct recursive extension of "same-window self-charity" (M3) and self-report skepticism (M1) onto the VERIFIER ITSELF. This is a clean example of self-challenging: the training doubts the very tool it just taught the student to build, on the student surface, with named countermoves (independence: judge calibration, hold-out checks, repeated sessions).
- New law: "Goodhart's law: when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure... optimization pressure finds the cheapest path to green." Complicates the verifier-building payoff from Ex2: even a well-built gate decays.
- New law: "One session is a sample... passing once shows the task is reachable, passing again and again shows it is dependable." Complicates the entire M4-M5 experimental method (test once, learn, encode) — now says ONE test isn't enough either.
- New law (Deming's tampering): "React on recurrence... one stochastic miss is not a process failure." — governor against over-reacting to noise.
- Payoff/consolidation: "The delegation frontier" — reach (how much delegated) x calibration (whether trust was earned by a verified check). This ties together M2's reach/calibration seed, M3's control-is-interrogation, M4's trust-building, and M5's gate-skepticism into ONE named 2x2 model. Governor: "The frontier moves outward only as fast as the gates behind it. Push reach past your calibration and you are not delegating more. You are checking less." — this is essentially the M2 delegation-frontier idea, now fully realized with the gate-skepticism layered in.
- Forward-looking, explicitly a question not a prediction, tied to Sutton's bitter lesson: "Today's right procedure... is superseded too. Retire what the next model outgrows, add what it needs." This gives the entire packaging/verifier apparatus a built-in expiration — genuinely self-challenging and forward-looking at once.

M5 close: this is the training's richest module for self-challenging and payoff. Two major threads reach clear articulation: (1) self-report/plausible-output skepticism, now named as three failure-mode lenses AND extended recursively onto the verifier itself ("the gate is a claim too" — Goodhart, judge miscalibration, sampling); (2) the M2 delegation-frontier/reach-calibration idea, now fully realized as a named 2x2 model with a governor and an explicit forward-looking expiration (bitter lesson). The "ironies of automation" counter-voice from M4 is not directly resolved here but the gate-skepticism material functions as a parallel counter-voice: don't over-trust your own checks either. Tracking into M6 for final payoff/resolution.

## Learning-set

### Learning 1 — The agent's account of its own work is fluent by default and must be independently checked, not trusted
One-line claim the student carries out: *what the agent says it did, and how confident it sounds, tells you nothing about whether it's true — check the artefact, not the narration.*

- **Planted:** M1, `getting-going.md` Key Concepts — "The agent's self-report is a hypothesis, not ground truth. Read it as the agent's account of the repo and the session, not the things themselves."
- **Complicated (meaning-changing beats, ~8):**
  1. M1 `exercises/orient-and-introspect.md` — "The account is a reconstruction, not ground truth... Assume about 10% of what it says or does is made up." (turns a stated claim into a quantified-ish working prior + a concrete spot-check move)
  2. M1 `exercises/fix-tests-first.md` — "There will be a next plausible answer, because the LLM will always try to find what you asked for." (extends the doubt from *what it read* to *what it argues for*)
  3. M2 `lectures/when-a-plan-is-good.md` — "Structure is persuasive. A 7-item plan with headers looks like a decision even when it's a draft." (the same fluent-but-unverified problem, now in a structured artefact rather than a sentence)
  4. M2 `exercises/push-back-on-the-plan.md` — "if the vagueness survived, push back again" (the agent's *revision* after being caught is itself suspect — re-softening on regeneration)
  5. M3 `exercises/author-test-strategy-skill.md` — "The grade is biased by design. The agent invoked the skill it just helped author, then graded the result in the same context window: same-window self-charity." (names the mechanism precisely for the first time)
  6. M5 `lectures/reading-the-return.md` — "The closing summary is not the artefact. The machine would rather produce something than admit nothing." (applied to a whole unsupervised session, not a single turn)
  7. M5 `exercises/diagnose-and-resend.md` — "Expect partial failures framed as partial successes... RLHF is a big part of why: agreeable answers won the preference round." (names the *cause*, closing the loop back to M1's mechanism lecture)
  8. M5 `lectures/the-gate-is-a-claim.md` — "Green is a claim about the check, not a fact about the work... the judge is miscalibrated, the gate got gamed, or the session was a lucky sample." (the doubt turns recursive — even the tool built to check the agent is itself unverified)
- **Paid off:** M1 `lectures/the-machine-you-just-met.md` — "Agreeable answers won the preference round... The agent's report of its own success is the same kind of output... The report is a hypothesis to check, not ground truth." (names the mechanism — sycophancy from preference tuning); M6 `lectures/story-of-module-6.md` — a first-person account of the exact failure happening to the trainer while building this very module: "The rules were loaded every time... Claude typed the banned word anyway... A rule in context is not a rule in the output."
- **Governor:** "Quote a specific file or function back and ask Claude to confirm it read what it claims" (M1); generalizes across the arc to "ask which session moment earned it" (M2) and "name one thing the verifier missed, concretely" (M6).
- **Counter-voice:** `story-of-module-6.md` is the training turning this exact claim on itself — the maintainer shows the rule failing to hold even when loaded in context, live, in the session that built the module the student is reading. Closing line: "Taste closes the gap."
- **Future:** Genuinely open, not resolved: `the-gate-is-a-claim.md` ends on "Today's right procedure... is superseded too" — a question about how long any given check keeps working, not a claim about what will happen.

### Learning 2 — Session evidence, not intention, is what should get written down and reused — and what gets written down must be pruned as hard as it's added
One-line claim: *don't draft rules from a blank page or intuition — extract them from what a real session actually showed, and cut the ones later evidence kills.*

- **Planted:** M1, `getting-going.md` Big Idea — "Your first session should leave something behind that the next one can use. Otherwise you have a clever assistant and no compounding," paired immediately with its own future complication: "It is the simplest personal store, and a starter. Everyone sees how it will bloat almost immediately."
- **Complicated (meaning-changing beats, ~7):**
  1. M2 `lectures/how-instructions-grow.md` — "The ceiling comes sooner than you'd guess: every always-loaded rule rides into every session... The work starts passing your rules and missing your point." (gives the bloat warning a mechanism and a cure: move to skills)
  2. M2 `lectures/where-the-rule-could-live.md` — "Agents build agents. An agent can help author the file. A later agent reads those rules... The first agent's output becomes the second agent's context." (widens compounding from *your next session* to *any agent, any runtime, that reads the file*)
  3. M3 `lectures/skills-from-the-frontier.md` — "A skill outlives the thread it came from... A STRIDE skill tuned to your stack lives as long as the repo." (a rule becomes a reusable, invocable capability, not just a loaded instruction)
  4. M4 `exercises/walk-and-send-off.md` — "Anything the plan tripped on is another [way to compound], and it fixes things for everyone with nothing loaded." (compounding no longer requires a file at all — direct fixes to the environment count too)
  5. M3 `lectures/the-loop-half-filled.md` — "The compound ladder: fix → memory → skill → system." (names the whole progression explicitly for the first time)
  6. M6 `exercises/spot-gaps-build-the-loop.md` — "Rules-files have a half-life. Adding rules is only half of it; subtracting the dead ones is the other half." (the first exercise that actually deletes a rule, rather than warning about deletion)
  7. M6 `lectures/composing-the-workflow.md` — "A workflow is skills composed around one task... Your job moves from reading every intermediate output to choosing the skills, their order, and the instructions each one carries." (the ladder's final rung: system-level composition)
- **Paid off:** M6 `lectures/the-handoff-prompt.md` / `agents-that-build-agents.md` — "Ask the agent to write you a handoff prompt to run later: one skill, with its checks... What comes back is a prompt, not a plan." (the compounding loop now writes its own next iteration)
- **Governor:** "Integrate, don't append" (M1 compound-and-close); "the location is reversible... pick by the mechanism you want" (M2); "cut one stale rule the diagnosis killed" (M6).
- **Counter-voice:** `story-of-module-6.md` — "A rule in memory that does not force is worse than no rule" — a sharper doubt than the bloat warning: passive, unenforced rules may be actively misleading, not merely inert.
- **Future:** `agents-that-build-agents.md` closes the whole training on this thread, explicitly unresolved: "There is no last turn... The kit compounds; the model rotates." A stated ongoing condition, not a prediction of an end-state.

### Learning 3 — Delegate only as far as what you have actually verified, and that boundary keeps moving
One-line claim: *"reach" (how much you hand off) is safe only up to "calibration" (checks you've actually earned) — and both the checks and the model keep changing under you, so the boundary is never settled.*

- **Planted:** M2, `lectures/when-a-plan-is-good.md` — "Reach is how much you delegated: the size of the task, the distance between checks. Calibration is whether your trust in what came back was earned by a check you have verified... Push reach past what you can check and you have not delegated more. You are checking less."
- **Complicated (meaning-changing beats, ~4):**
  1. M4 `lectures/the-far-half.md` / `lectures/test-and-learn.md` — the un-packaged, un-watched first send-off is deliberately built as reach-without-calibration: "no plan.md, no verifier, no reference artefact... let it run." (the student is made to feel the ungoverned end of the frontier before being taught to fix it)
  2. M4 `lectures/ironies-of-automation.md` — "Trust and vigilance move in opposite directions... The more tasks the agent lands cleanly, the worse you get at catching the one that doesn't." (a direct challenge to the idea that earned trust is simply good — earning it can itself erode the very calibration the frontier depends on)
  3. M5 `lectures/the-gate-is-a-claim.md` — "A gate only means what the gate can see... A gate nobody has verified is a gate trusted on vibes." (the *check itself* — the thing that was supposed to certify calibration — needs its own calibration, recursively)
- **Paid off:** M5 `the-gate-is-a-claim.md` § *The delegation frontier* — "Every task you hand off sits on two axes... Calibrated agency when the gates behind the green are ones you have measured, reckless autonomy when they are not. From outside, the two look identical." (the M2 model, deliberately left unnamed there, is fully named and completed here, three modules later)
- **Governor:** "Building a better check buys you more than approving a bigger plan" (M2); "compare a handful of your own judgements with the judge's verdicts, and teach the judge until you converge... keep a check the agent never sees... repeat the task across several sessions" (M5, three independent countermoves).
- **Counter-voice:** `ironies-of-automation.md` is the training's most direct self-doubt on this exact learning — it argues that the training's own success (earned trust, compounding competence) actively degrades the student's ability to catch the failure that matters most. It is stated on the student surface, not hedged into a footnote: "The trust is deserved. The watching still has to be engineered."
- **Future:** Explicit and forward-looking, tied to Sutton's bitter lesson: "Today's right procedure, your gates and workflow, yours or the agent's, is superseded too. Retire what the next model outgrows, add what it needs." A live open question (how long does any calibration hold?), not a dated prediction.

## Audience fit

Pass. The learnings and their governors are stated in the register and concerns of a software-engineer IC throughout: git worktrees, TDD, ADRs in the repo's own convention, STRIDE and least-privilege, CI gates, shell-hooks and stop-hooks, `CLAUDE.local.md` vs `CLAUDE.md` scoping, PR review. ## Weakest factor

Forward-looking is the factor with the thinnest and least-distributed evidence, even though it is genuinely present and well-protected where it exists. The training's open questions all collapse into essentially one thread — the two-frontiers bookend (M1 → M6, explicitly never answered) and its close cousin, the bitter-lesson/no-last-turn ending attached to Learning 3 (reach/calibration). The smallest concrete fix: the material already exists to pose a dedicated open question for Learning 1 — `reading-the-return.md`'s own backing block states plainly that "practitioners hit the same three [failure modes]... only *context rot* is field vocabulary" and that "no hour-marker is supported by anything anyone measured" — but this doubt about whether the vocabulary itself will hold up is never surfaced to the student as a question. One sentence at the close of `reading-the-return.md` or `the-gate-is-a-claim.md` — something like "these three names are today's best guess at what breaks; expect the field, or your own repo, to teach you better ones" — would give Learning 1 its own forward-looking edge instead of borrowing Learning 3's.
