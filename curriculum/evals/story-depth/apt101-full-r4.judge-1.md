## Frame

**A (AE101).** Frame: agentic engineering is engineering, meaning feedback control, verification and compounding, with the agent as an unreliable component inside a loop you own.
- M1 the-machine-you-just-met: "A check from outside the session resets the chain. A failing test does not care how confident the answer sounded."
- M3 the-loop-half-filled: "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller**... Cut the feedback signal (a test, a check, a read) and it drifts."
- M5 what-packaging-is: "**A check is a position fix**. At a fix the wedge of possible states collapses to a point."
- M5 the-gate-is-a-claim (where it breaks): "Sutton's **bitter lesson**: built-in human knowledge wins today and loses to the next model... Retire what the next model outgrows."
M2 (plan read as calibration), M4 (un-packaged send-off as experiment) and M6 (eval as measurement) only make sense through the control lens. Where the frame breaks is named, though as a general law rather than as this training's own limit. **95.**

**B (APT101).** Frame: building got cheap and deciding didn't, so the trio's job moves to choosing the outcome, the bet and the no, and to reading the signal.
- D1 apt101-building-is-cheap: "Agents make the slow step cheap. The hard steps stay where they were... Every other step is a decision."
- D2 apt101-why-it-agreed: "None of it is a choice. It cannot tell you which of its findings matters... Its headline is your own look-for line handed back, not an insight."
- D3 apt101-three-jobs-rewritten: "Your insight is the choice it leaves you with... Which good idea you say no to, out loud."
- D3 apt101-where-you-go-from-here (where it breaks): "This is where 'building got cheap, deciding didn't' stops being an edge on its own... What would change this training's mind: agents that start deciding well."
Each day is a case of the frame: D1 decides the promise, the bet and the door; D2 decides what's true; D3 decides persevere, pivot or stop and who does what. The break is named against this training's own thesis, which clears the 100 clause. Two leaks cost it: the D2 bakeoff (catch-it-making-things-up) reads as verification craft carried over from engineering more than as a case of deciding, and the `module-2/…module-7/` folder names show another training's skeleton. **94.**

## Narrative

**A.** Five sentences: An engineer brings a real repo and learns the agent's account isn't the repo. They learn to plan, check and compound on short loops. Then they send a long task off un-packaged, and it comes back confidently partial. Reading the return through three failure lenses turns each failure into a piece of packaging, and the same task goes out again. The close shows the author's own module-making session failing the same way, so the loop never ends. The turn: "The un-packaged run was supposed to underdeliver. What came back is data, not blame." (M5 diagnose-and-resend). The training's own failure: "It still opened with the un-packaged shape." and "I drifted in every one of the ways this story just walked." (M6 story-of-module-6). The un-packaged and packaged pair is lived across M4 to M6 ("send it off plainly and you find out what you can't steer yet", M4 set-the-markers-send-it-off). **94.**

**B.** Five sentences: A trio arrives with its own product and the memory of a feature nobody used. They paint the box, write a bet that can lose, agree the door, and before sending a digest overnight each writes a look-for line and an expectation. On Day 2 the digest agrees with them, and run the other way round the same material gives a second headline: the agent found what they asked for. They check, build a judge, choose a bet with evidence behind it and imagine it failing. On Day 3 five users show what no digest could have said, each person says which line of their own Day 1 hypothesis broke, and the close asks whether Monday's plan is the next thing nobody asked for. The turn is quoted at D2 apt101-read-the-digest: "Did it find what your line asked for?" It is then the reversed rerun "a line that looked for support becomes 'find what in my material argues against it'", and it is named only afterwards: "The headline came from your question as much as from your customers." (D2 apt101-why-it-agreed). The ring closes at D3 close: "is anything in `team/monday.md` the next thing your team builds that nobody asked for?" Every seat lives the turn, and the turn is the student's own failure. The guide's own failure sits beside it ("I used to think of being wrong as failure.", D2 why-it-agreed) but is not the plot's turn. The 60-minute bakeoff in mid-Day 2 is a manufactured failure ("Claude writes a briefing... with made-up claims planted in it") that stalls the plot for two of three seats. **91.**
