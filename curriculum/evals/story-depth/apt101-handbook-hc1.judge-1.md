## Frame

**A · AE101: agentic engineering is engineering. The agent is a closed-loop controller, and trust comes only from checks outside the session.**
- M1 · the-machine-you-just-met: "A check from outside the session resets the chain. A failing test does not care how confident the answer sounded."
- M3 · the-loop-half-filled: "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller**… Cut the feedback signal (a test, a check, a read) and it drifts. Signal quality is part of the law: a flaky test is a closed loop that still drifts."
- M5 · what-packaging-is: "**A check is a position fix**. At a fix the wedge of possible states collapses to a point."
- Where it breaks, on the page. M5 · the-gate-is-a-claim: "The check you built is itself a claim that wants verifying… A gate nobody has verified is a gate trusted on vibes." Also "Sutton's **bitter lesson**… Today's right procedure, your gates and workflow, yours or the agent's, is superseded too." M5 · what-packaging-is: "today's playbooks are **candidates**."
- M2 (plan review as a calibration move), M4 (Bainbridge: "The watching still has to be engineered") and M6 ("You drew a control loop") all make sense only through the lens. The frame is load-bearing in every module and names its own limit, at the 100 rung. Small deductions: it is never stated in one sentence the student can carry, and M6 · composing-the-workflow has figure-only slides where the lens goes quiet.

**B · APT101: building got cheap, deciding didn't. The outcome craft you always wanted is now affordable, and the scarce part is your team's insight and choice.**
- M1 · apt101-building-is-cheap: "For years the slice was the slow step. Now an agent can build it. Every other step is a decision… None of those got cheaper."
- M2 · apt101-why-it-agreed: "A faster feature factory is still a feature factory… A team that ships without asking whether it worked now ships more of it, faster, and still never finds out."
- M3 · apt101-three-jobs-rewritten: "Your insight is the choice it leaves you with… That is real strategy work, and it does not get cheaper when building does."
- Where it breaks. M3 · apt101-where-you-go-from-here: "This is where 'building got cheap, deciding didn't' stops being an edge on its own. When rivals build as cheaply, speed is the entry price… What would change this training's mind: agents that start deciding well."
- The frame is stated, carries each module (M1 rationed craft → vindication; M2 analysis cheap, choice not; M3 jobs re-cut around deciding), and names where it breaks. That is the 100-rung shape. It scores lower than A because the break is argued in one closing slide. Rival parity is seeded ("Your competitors can use the same models you do", M1 · apt101-your-material-is-the-moat), but the break's own consequence, speed as the entry price and feature bloat, arrives fresh at the close, and because many bodies are a named authority's framework retold (Patton, Nielsen, Kniberg, the double diamond) with the frame attached only in the last line ("With agents, three rough solutions cost an afternoon" in M2 · apt101-widen-before-you-choose).

## Narrative

**A.** (1) An engineer meets a steerable mirror and learns that a check outside the session is what resets a chain of mostly-right steps. (2) They build rules, plans and skills on the near half, where feedback is quick. (3) The far half goes quiet: they send a real task off un-packaged, and it comes back confident and wrong. (4) They diagnose it, package it, re-send it, and then learn that the gate itself is a claim. (5) The guide then shows the training's own making failing in exactly these ways, and hands the loop over: "There is no last turn."
- The turn, lived. M4 · test-and-learn: "Session one goes now, un-packaged: no plan.md, no verifier, no reference artifact." M5 · diagnose-and-resend: "The un-packaged run was supposed to underdeliver. What came back is data, not blame."
- M5 · what-packaging-is: "An unchecked session arrives confident, and wrong… The success report comes from the wrong harbor."
- The training's own failure is the echo of the turn. M6 · story-of-module-6: "The training teaches this pattern across three modules. The agent I was working with had just finished writing those three modules. It still opened with the un-packaged shape."
- This is at the 100 rung: the student lives the turn, and the training's own failure is on the page.

**B.** (1) A product trio who always knew the outcome craft had to ration it, because building was expensive. (2) Agents make building cheap, so the trio writes a bet, builds a product box and sends off an overnight digest. (3) The turn: the digest comes back agreeing with their favourite hypothesis, because it found what the brief asked it to find. (4) They widen, go back to customers, write down what good means, and let five users and a "no" signal correct the bet. (5) They re-cut their three jobs around the choice no analysis makes, and take a proposal, not a decision, to their team.
- The turn. M2 · apt101-the-digest-is-back: "its headline is the hunch your team liked most… It feels like good news. It is also the moment you are least likely to check."
- M2 · apt101-why-it-agreed: "So the agreement may come from your question, not from your customers."
- M3 · apt101-what-good-means: "Hold the overnight digest that agreed with your favourite hypothesis against those lines, and you can see where it agreed on thin evidence." This calls the turn back, which is good braiding.
- The turn is narrated as a scenario ("Three quotes sit under it, and a ticket count beside it") rather than read off the student's own artefact. The handbook carries no exercise text, so on the page the student is told about the turn rather than shown living through it. The five-user beat is hypothetical too: "Say one hesitates at a step the digest called simple." The training's own failure is not the turn. The guide's installer is a side anecdote in M1. This sits between 40 and 80, nearer 80, because the digest is the student's own earlier artefact by construction.

## Point of view

**A.** Second person throughout, with a guide who surfaces in lines and owns one passage: Antti, an engineer who built this module with Claude and drifted.
- M6 · story-of-module-6: "I am going to tell you how this module got made… What I tried, what drifted, what the rules caught, what the rules missed."
- M6 · story-of-module-6: "Claude had written a paraphrase and presented it as attribution." Also "The loop caught what the subagents missed. I caught what the loop missed. You will catch what I missed."
- M6 · story-of-module-6: "I drifted in every one of the ways this story just walked… Your turn. Antti"
- Earlier the voice shows in asides that carry experience. M1 · orient-and-introspect: "Assume about 10% of what it says or does is made up." M2 · extract-the-task-shaping-rule: "Precise prompting is harder than it looks."
- The guide's own failure is on the page, in detail and with the training's own tools: the 100 rung. The guide stays a guide and the student stays the hero ("Your turn"). The only deduction is that the guide is absent as an "I" for M1–M5.

**B.** An impersonal, second-person handbook voice. The guide appears as four cited first-person essay excerpts, attributed by byline.
- M1 · apt101-building-is-cheap: "One of the first things we tried building was a local installer for developer tooling. It got working and it was used just a bit. Bad idea, as the need wasn't a daily one… (Lucky it did not take that long to build it.)"
- M2 · apt101-why-it-agreed: "I used to think of being wrong as failure… My creations are not me and not everything has to be perfect."
- M3 · apt101-from-us-to-the-team: "We built good things. We failed to share them well." M3 · apt101-where-you-go-from-here: "I still make mistakes. I make them faster now."
- The guide's own failure is on the page, and it is on-thesis. The installer that worked and that nobody needed is "building got cheap, deciding didn't" lived by the guide, and the sharing failure is M3's thesis. Under the dispatch's "wherever that guide appears", the citation form is not a discount. Two things keep it below A. The sharing failure is abstract ("Cross-team knowledge transfer remains the unsolved problem"). And there is no failure with the agent work the training teaches: no digest that fooled the guide, no invented quote that nearly reached a decision. The scars are short lines, not a walked passage.

## Stance

**A.** Positions it could lose a customer over:
- *The model's best practice is a forecast, not a measurement.* Defended by mechanism. M5 · what-packaging-is: "a well-read average of what other people published about other repos, frozen at a cutoff… **A prediction is not a measurement.**"
- *A rule in context is not a rule in the output.* Defended by a scar. M6 · story-of-module-6: "Same rule, same rules file, same task, four separate violations across four independent LLM instances. The grep pass caught each one. The LLM self-check did not."
- *Your gate decays, and green is not proof.* M5 · the-gate-is-a-claim: "The agent is an optimizer aimed straight at your gate… No malice needed: optimization pressure finds the cheapest path to green."
- *Reach without calibration is checking less.* M2 · when-a-plan-is-good: "Push reach past what you can check and you have not delegated more. You are checking less."
- Against its own commercial interest: "This training stops short of the full system" (M2 · how-instructions-grow), and "Today's right procedure, your gates and workflow… is superseded too" (M5 · the-gate-is-a-claim). Also "Expect over-credit on the packaging" (M6 · spot-gaps-build-the-loop). These are present, though stated in passing rather than argued as a position. That puts it between 80 and 100.

**B.** Positions it could lose a customer over:
- *Agents amplify, they do not transform.* Defended by mechanism. M2 · apt101-why-it-agreed: "Agents amplify the way a team already works. They do not transform it… That check is product craft, and agents do not bring it with them."
- *"Everyone gets our agent" is a false promise.* M3 · apt101-from-us-to-the-team: "The page was never where the work lived. The work lives in the memory you built up… a proposal to the wider team cannot promise 'everyone gets our agent'."
- *Fabrication is the shape of the technology.* M2 · apt101-fluent-is-not-true: "This isn't a bug that gets patched in the next release. It's the shape of the technology. Later models will fabricate less; they won't stop."
- *Leave sources out even when someone wants them.* M1 · apt101-it-runs-overnight: "It will cost you a source somebody wanted in. Leaving it out is still the right call."
- Against its own interest: M3 · apt101-where-you-go-from-here: "Cheap building helps your rivals too… What would change this training's mind." The training names the condition under which its own premise expires.
- Positions are defended with mechanism, rarely with a scar. The guide's essays are about being wrong in general, not about these positions. Many slides hand their authority to a cited author (Torres, Cagan, Fitzpatrick, Nielsen), so the training's own ground shows mainly in each slide's last line. That places it at about 80.

## The pair

- **A:** both. It doubts its own tools ("The grade is biased by design… same-window self-charity", M3 · author-test-strategy-skill; "A gate nobody has verified is a gate trusted on vibes", M5) and still holds ground ("A prediction is not a measurement"). It reads as someone who has been there, and story-of-module-6 makes the doubt autobiographical.
- **B:** both, tilted toward stance. It doubts its checks ("A pass is a claim about a check nobody has tested", "Asking the same agent twice in the same conversation is not a second opinion", M3 · apt101-what-good-means) and its own premise (where-the-frame-breaks). It holds ground ("A faster feature factory is still a feature factory"). The doubt is borrowed from AE101's machinery and from cited authors more than lived by the guide. It reads as someone who has been there, at one remove: a well-read guide more than a scarred one.

## Smallest moves for B

1. Replace one cited essay excerpt with a first-person passage at the M2 turn, in which the guide's own agent-written digest or summary agreed with the guide's hypothesis, or put a quote nobody said into a decision, and say how it was caught.
2. Swap the hypothetical in apt101-bet-meets-five-users ("Say one hesitates at a step the digest called simple") for a prompt that has the trio put their own five users' observed moments beside their own digest, so the second turn is lived, not narrated.
3. Cut the restated authority bodies on the slides where the training's own line only closes them (Nielsen's five users, Patton's story map, the double diamond), down to the one fact used, so the training's position leads each slide.
