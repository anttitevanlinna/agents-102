# Quartet estimate — a101-skeleton-to-be (titles only)

Scope note: titles only, in student order, as listed in `a101-skeleton-to-be.md`. `(keep)/(rename)/(new)` markers and `mood:` labels ignored per instructions — this is an estimate of what a training whose titles read like this would score, not a read of the actual prose.

## Frame

No single sentence anywhere in the title list functions as a stated lens ("everything here is a case of X"). What's visible instead is a *spine of nouns* that escalates module to module — Context → Compounding → Splitting → Security → Grounding → Evals → Access → Tool-building — each module title naming its own topic rather than restating a shared frame. The closest candidate a reader could reconstruct is "control keeps slipping and you keep re-earning it," visible in "**Context is whatever you tell it**" (M1), "**The doubt stays. Hold it.**" (M3), "**The gate is a claim too**" (M6), and "**Will your organisation learn faster than the model changes underneath it?**" (M8) — four different modules where the title says the same underlying thing (nothing here is settled, you have to keep checking). But titles alone can't confirm this is *load-bearing* rather than a pattern a rater is imposing after the fact; nothing states it as a frame, and no title names where it breaks. Three modules that would only make sense through it: M3 ("**Splitting earns its keep when they can't be one**" — control fractures), M4 ("**Certainty is a fantasy you inherited**" — control was always false), M6 ("**A yardstick you rewrite is not a yardstick**" — even the measuring stick isn't stable). That's a plausible reading, not a demonstrated one from titles alone.

## Narrative

Titles-only story, five sentences: A builder leader starts the room believing a good demo proves the tool works ("**Same question, two answers**"). They learn the tool has no memory of its own and is only as good as what they hand it ("**A file the agent reads every time**"), then watch that same mechanism scale into something that compounds against them or for them ("**It gets better by being edited, not by being clever**"). Multi-agent and security modules turn the mood from capability to exposure — the turn is a debrief title, not a plot beat: "**The doubt stays. Hold it.**" Grounding and evals hand back a measuring tool, but even that tool admits its own limit ("**The judge names its own limit**"), and the training ends not with mastery but with a question aimed at the room's own future ("**Will your organisation learn faster than the model changes underneath it?**"). This reads as a shape asserted through the sequence of headings — each module's titles have an internal arc — but a title-only pass can't confirm the student *lives through* the turn rather than being told about it in slide form.

## Point of view

A "you" narrator speaks throughout and never lets up: "**You felt context move**," "**You act on the future to know what's real**," "**You cannot share an agent**." That's a stated, consistent address, not just asides — most modules carry at least one direct "you" title. Whether that voice has *been through* what it's describing, titles can partly show: "**Why the retro was kind to you**" and "**The report is a hypothesis, not a result**" (M1) imply a narrator who has watched this exact self-flattery happen before, which reads as more than a neutral instructor. No title puts the narrator's *own* failure on the page the way a memoir beat would — the closest is institutional rather than personal: "**The gate is a claim too**" and "**The judge names its own limit**" admit the training's own evaluation machinery is fallible, but that's the training doubting its tool, not a narrator doubting themself.

## Stance

Positions a training could lose a customer over, each carried by a title:
- **Autonomy has a ceiling, and it's not generosity to hide that** — "**The better it gets, the less you watch**" (names the exact failure mode of the thing being sold).
- **Groups are worse at judgment than one committed stance** — "**Left alone, the synthesizer averages to beige**" (an argument against consensus-by-default, which is how most orgs actually run meetings).
- **Access doesn't equal adoption** — "**Access is easy; absorption is scarce**" (undercuts any pitch that ships-the-tool = done).
- **The measurement layer you're building is not neutral** — "**The gate is a claim too**."
- **Naming people, not process, is where change plans actually die** — "**The people plan stalls on names. That is the finding.**" (an uncomfortable, specific claim a client could take personally).

Titles alone show these positions *asserted*, not defended — a title can carry a claim but not a mechanism or a scar; whether "beige" or "the gate is a claim too" is backed by an argument lives in body text a title-only pass can't see. One position is held against commercial interest in a way titles make legible on their own: "**The better it gets, the less you watch**" argues the vendor's own product gets more dangerous as it improves, which is not a sentence a tool-seller says for free.

## The pair

Someone who has been there: the "you"-address plus "**Why the retro was kind to you**" and "**The doubt stays. Hold it.**" read as stance-with-self-doubt rather than pure hedge or pure pitch — the titles assert positions (splitting earns its keep, absorption is scarce, the gate is a claim) while also repeatedly admitting the training's own tools and judges have limits ("**the judge names its own limit**," "**a flat score is information about the judge**"). That's the pair, not a hedge alone or a pitch alone — though titles can't confirm the *same* voice carries both halves in the actual prose.

## Scores

Frame: 35 · Narrative: 45 · Point of view: 45 · Stance: 55

## What titles cannot show

- Whether the reconstructed frame ("control keeps slipping, you keep re-earning it") is actually stated once and made load-bearing in body prose, or whether a rater is retrofitting a pattern onto independently-written module titles.
- Whether the turn (M3's "the doubt stays") is something the student is walked through experientially (an exercise, a live failure) or only told about across consecutive slides — titles can't distinguish enacted from asserted.
- Whether the defended-vs-asserted stance positions ("beige," "the gate is a claim too") carry an actual mechanism or scar in the body, or stop at the assertion the title already makes.
