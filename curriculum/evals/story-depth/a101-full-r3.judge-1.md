## Frame

**A — 95.** Frame: *agentic engineering is engineering* — a control loop around a machine that mirrors you; checks, not watching, carry trust; every session should compound.
- M1 `lectures/the-machine-you-just-met.md`: "That is the machine. The rest is steering."
- M3 `lectures/the-loop-half-filled.md`: "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller**... Cut the feedback signal (a test, a check, a read) and it drifts."
- M5 `lectures/what-packaging-is.md`: "**A check is a position fix**. At a fix the wedge of possible states collapses to a point."
- Where it breaks, named: M5 `lectures/the-gate-is-a-claim.md`: "Green is a claim about the check, not a fact about the work." and "Sutton's **bitter lesson**... Today's right procedure, your gates and workflow... is superseded too."
M4 (un-packaged send-off) and M6 (eval as measurement) only make sense through the control lens. Frame names its own breaking point → near 100.

**B — 80.** Frame: *an agent is a set of parts you assemble — context first, then tools, checks, boundary, loop, interface — and each part is a decision about what you give it and what you keep back.*
- M1 `lectures/context-is-king.md`: "Context is the first piece of the agent. Tools, goals, checks, boundaries, and loops come later and depend on it."
- M4 `lectures/practice-of-risk.md`: "More context makes a good answer likelier. Less access makes a bad answer smaller." (only makes sense if the student holds "give/keep back")
- M6 `lectures/new-human-role-in-the-loop.md`: "model, context, tools, goal, loop, checks, boundary, interface. Each was one decision about what you give the agent and what you keep back."
- M7 `lectures/access-is-not-absorption.md`: "An agent is context, a boundary, a set of checks and somebody who answers for what it does. The first three copy in an afternoon."
- Where it breaks: M8 `lectures/where-is-this-all-going.md`: "Which half goes first? The judge you wrote, or the thing it judges?"
Load-bearing in M1, M4, M6, M7, M8. M3 and M5 sit loosely: M5's module Big Idea ("Run several quality checks... keep the winner") and its lecture opener ("There is truth out there") pull two frames on one page; M3's lecture argues against its own exercise without tying back to the parts frame. Every-module reading not yet achieved → 80.

## Narrative

**A — 93.** An engineer fixes a bug and learns the machine mirrors them. They learn to read plans and build skills, always with quick feedback. Then they send a long task off un-packaged, and it comes back wrong. They diagnose their own failure, package, re-send, and measure the difference. The turn is the training's own: the author's M6 session failed the same way.
- Turn set up, M4 `exercises/set-the-markers-send-it-off.md`: "send it off plainly and you find out what you can't steer yet."
- Turn lived, M5 `exercises/diagnose-and-resend.md`: "The un-packaged run was supposed to underdeliver. What came back is data, not blame."
- Turn as the training's own failure, M6 `lectures/story-of-module-6.md`: "The agent I was working with had just finished writing those three modules. It still opened with the un-packaged shape."

**B — 80.** A leader who uses chat makes a site that is theirs, then a memory that compounds and a team of agents that argue. They are asked if they would bet their job on its safety, and find they can't tell. They learn they can't trust the output either, build a judge, and put it in a loop that runs without them. Sharing it meets the human principle, and they leave with a flywheel and an open doubt.
- Joy, M1 `exercises/personal-site-with-guardrails.md`: "Iterate until you say *\"yes, this is me.\"*"
- Turn, M4 `lectures/practice-of-risk.md`: "You built something that works. Would you bet your job on it being safe?" lived in `exercises/audit-your-agent.md`: "The uncomfortable feeling is the evidence."
- Rescue, M6 `exercises/eval-loop.md`: "If the judge file moved, the exercise failed; if only `./generation-tactic.md` moved, the loop worked."
- Training's own doubt at close, M8 `lectures/where-is-this-all-going.md`: "nothing in the room checked it. Hold that doubt."
The unease is lived. The rescue beat is thin where it should land: M5's `hallucination-bakeoff.md` heads "Phase 5: Close, name the rescue" and then gives one line about production claim counts. The M3 doubt ("You can't yet tell if the answer is right") is never brought back and answered with the student's own judge. The training shows the narrator's project failing (M7), but not the training failing → 80.
