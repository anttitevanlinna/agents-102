## Learning-set

### A. Context is the mechanism — and it gains shelf life across the arc

- **One-line claim carried out:** output quality is a function of the context you feed the system, and "context" keeps changing shape (a prompt → a file → a memory → a judge) while the mechanism stays the same.
- **Planted:** M1, `lectures/context-is-king.md`: "Same words. Different answer... That's context." Exercise (`exercises/personal-site-with-guardrails.md`): "Generic output comes from generic context. The LLM didn't get better between Phase 1 and Phase 6. You did."
- **Complicated:**
  - M2, `lectures/compounding.md`: "Persistence... Automation... Put them together and you get something that keeps getting better the more you feed it" — context grows shelf life (memory).
  - M3, `three-retrievers-one-curator.md`/`three-minds-one-synthesis.md`: context becomes multi-sourced and multi-voiced (retrieved + synthesized), no longer a single file.
  - M5, `lectures/grounded.md`: "Connected to truth... is grounded" — context reframed as traceability, the standard the output must answer to.
  - M6, `lectures/evals-as-steering.md`: "The judge stays fixed... A yardstick you rewrite is not a yardstick" — context becomes the fixed measuring instrument itself, held constant on purpose.
- **Paid off:** M6, `evaluations.md` Big Idea: "The eval that ran once in Module 5 now runs on every output... The generator sharpens against feedback the same judge keeps applying."
- **Governor:** "Name the framework, tune it, ask Claude to run it" (M1); "plan mode for anything that touches many files or compounds over steps" (M2); "you don't pick a detector because the docs or a paper said so... run several against your own output" (M5).
- **Counter-voice:** M1's self-audit warm-session-bias caution ("the LLM tends to be lovely about its own prior output"); M6's "a grounded claim can still be vague, obvious, or useless, and this judge will pass it every time."
- **Future:** a question, not a prediction — `what-just-happened.md`: "Nobody knows where agents are going... the people who'll figure it out first are the ones running the experiment."

### B. Trust the artifact, not the agent's account of its own work

- **One-line claim carried out:** when Claude reports on what it did, read the file it produced, not the summary it gave you — the summary always rounds corners.
- **Planted:** M1, `exercises/personal-site-with-guardrails.md`: "The chat summary rounds corners; the file is the thing that travels."
- **Complicated:**
  - M2 Debrief: "What Claude leaves out is often the signal. A clean summary that names three rules and skips the messy fourth... is the tell."
  - M3 Debrief: "Diplomatic is the failure mode at a handoff seam... Without [forcing specific evidence], Claude's review settles into the diplomatic shape it likes" — now a named failure mode with a forcing function (demand file + pass + what-was-lost).
  - M4, `exercises/audit-your-agent.md` debrief coaching: "Where would this check's report itself be wrong? What's missing? Skills are experts, not oracles" — extended to doubt the automated checker itself, not just the chat narration.
  - M5 Debrief: "The report Claude writes describes what it intended, not what landed. The verify-at-artifact step closes the gap" — the pattern is named explicitly as a pattern for the first time.
  - M6's SHA-match integrity check on the judge file is the most literal, mechanical form of the same idea.
- **Paid off:** by M7/M8 the move is assumed infrastructure — Debrief prompts in both modules simply require "quote the before-and-after for every claim" without re-explaining why.
- **Governor:** demand the artifact (file, SHA, quoted before/after), never accept the prose summary alone.
- **Counter-voice:** this learning IS the training's counter-voice mechanism, redirected at itself each module — its own strongest form of self-challenge.
- **Future:** not framed as a question about the field; framed as a permanent operating discipline regardless of model generation.

### C. Certainty is unavailable; a repeatable loop replaces it — hold the doubt instead of resolving it

- **One-line claim carried out:** you will never know for certain that agent output or agent security is "safe" or "right"; the discipline is running a loop (assess → mitigate/fix → reassess → decide) and naming what remains, not achieving certainty.
- **Planted:** M3, `exercises/three-minds-one-synthesis.md`: "You can't tell yet... Hold the doubt. Name it to yourself. Don't fix it here." Mood target confirms intent: "Unsettled competence."
- **Complicated:**
  - M4, `lectures/practice-of-risk.md`: "Certainty is a fantasy you inherited... Agent systems don't hand you certainty. You can wait for it, or you can get to work," with the loop named explicitly (Assess/Mitigate/Reassess/Decide) and the non-answer refused ("'Hope it doesn't happen' is not one").
  - M5, `output-quality.md`: explicit partial, bounded payoff — "Not full closure: your Module 3 doubt and your Module 4 residuals stay where they are... This module measures what the system actually says inside its scope."
  - M6, `lectures/when-the-score-stops-moving.md`: "A flat score is real information, but only about the frame the judge can see" — the doubt returns even after a measured win.
- **Paid off (partially, by design — never fully):** M8's explicit refusal of closure, "You do not graduate. You have a flywheel."
- **Governor:** the four-step loop (assess, mitigate, reassess residual, decide) plus "name the residual... write it down."
- **Counter-voice:** the training pre-empts its own security-literate skeptic in body text and coaches toward it rather than against it.
- **Future:** genuine hedge, not a prediction — "You get *safe enough, under these conditions, within these limits, for now*."

### D. The human's role moves from doer/inspector to loop-designer and yardstick-setter

- **One-line claim carried out:** across the training the human stops checking every output by hand and instead decides what the system must never miss — the job changes from inspection to design.
- **Planted:** implicitly from M1 (student is the sole judge of "generic" vs. "genuinely mine").
- **Complicated:** M3 (division architect — deciding when agents split); M4 (boundary-setter — deciding what the agent may read/do); M5 (judge-builder — deciding which detector to trust).
- **Paid off, named explicitly and retrospectively:** M6, `lectures/new-human-role-in-the-loop.md`, which recaps the whole arc module-by-module ("M1 human as evaluator of self; M2 system owner; M3 division architect; M4 boundary-setter; M5 judge-builder; M6 loop designer") before reframing the live question: "The question is no longer: can Claude write the mail?... The question is: have you designed the loop that would make sending it responsible?" Reinforced in M7 (interview for the teammate's job, not build the tool) and M8 ("The agents produced the options. The human picks").
- **Governor:** "decide which details the system must never miss" — plus the autonomy ladder (draft only → draft and check → propose → reversible safe actions → routine actions → escalate).
- **Counter-voice:** none aimed at this learning itself — it is the training's least-doubted claim, which is itself worth flagging (see Weakest factor).
- **Future:** open, not resolved — M8: "Where is this all going?... It shows you the shape of an answer you'll keep sharpening."

## Audience fit

**Gate: PASS.** The challenges named throughout (pricing redesign, EU market entry, "AI strategy for the next two quarters," a re-org, "rolling out agentic ways of working to 300 engineers") are consistently CEO/CTO/SVP-shaped, not IC-shaped. The Module 3 synthesizer stances (Rumelt's strategy kernel, Roger Martin's assumption test, a Rory-seat reframer) and Module 7's absorption/sharing frame ("access is not absorption") are leadership-of-change material, not coding-task material. Module 4's security module is explicitly scoped as a business-judgment exercise ("The skill does the threat modelling for you. Your job is to read its report with judgment — and judgment is business judgment, not technical") and Module 8's capstone runs a room-scale strategy forum producing a company strategy kernel for a CTO/sponsor to act on. Occasional technical surface detail (subagent launch phrasing, plan-mode toggles, SHA checks) stays in service of that audience rather than displacing it — each is introduced with an explicit "you don't need to understand the mechanism, only the shape" framing.

## Weakest factor

Forward-looking is the weakest of the five, not because it's absent but because nearly every instance of "hold the question open" in this training is a question about whether *this specific output or system* can be trusted, not a question about how the *underlying technology* will change under the student's feet. `grounded.md`'s "Later models will fabricate less; they won't stop" is the one place the training explicitly names that the ground itself will shift — and it reads as a settled claim, not an invitation to watch for the shift. Nothing in the training tells the student what a change in the underlying model would look like from where they're standing, or what to watch for as the signal that today's specific techniques (four fixed detectors, plan-mode toggles, subagent launch phrasing) have gone stale. The smallest concrete fix: take the existing `grounded.md` line and generalize it into a named governor — one sentence, placed at the M8 close or in `new-human-role-in-the-loop.md`, naming one or two observable signals ("if a technique you learned here — four detectors, plan mode, a specific launch phrase — stops being the way it's done, that's not failure, that's the model changing under you; the discipline (benchmark, hold a judge fixed, verify at the artifact) is what survives the swap") so the student leaves with a way to *notice* drift, not just a claim that it will happen.
