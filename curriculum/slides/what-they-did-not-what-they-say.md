## What customers did beats what they say they will do
<!--slide:what-they-did-not-what-they-say-->

Rob Fitzpatrick's *The Mom Test* (2013) gives three rules for a customer conversation: talk about their life instead of your idea; ask about specifics in the past instead of generics or opinions about the future; talk less and listen more.

The rules keep out three kinds of bad data. Compliments, which he calls worthless. Fluff, like "I would definitely buy that": "folks are wildly optimistic about what they would do in the future." And opinions. What you want instead are facts about what people do and why, and, once there is something to sell, a commitment of time, reputation or money, or a step closer to buying.

An agent summarising your interviews can count "I would definitely buy that" as demand. Ask it to keep facts apart from compliments, fluff and opinions.
