## Frame

**A (AE101).** Frame in one sentence: *agentic engineering is engineering*. The agent is a closed-loop controller, so control comes from checks outside the session, and the kit compounds. Three modules only make sense through it. M1 `the-machine-you-just-met`: "A check from outside the session resets the chain. A failing test does not care how confident the answer sounded." M3 `the-loop-half-filled`: "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller**. ... Cut the feedback signal (a test, a check, a read) and it drifts." M5 `what-packaging-is`: "**A check is a position fix**. At a fix the wedge of possible states collapses to a point." The training also says where the frame breaks. M5 `the-gate-is-a-claim`: "Today's right procedure, your gates and workflow, yours or the agent's, is superseded too." M5 `what-packaging-is`: "Whether this field ever settles into a real best practice is an open question." M2 `how-instructions-grow`: "This training stops short of the full system." Every module reads through the lens, and the lens names its own limit, but the limit is stated as a field-level doubt rather than as a place where *the engineering frame itself* fails. 93.

**B (APT101).** Frame in one sentence: *building got cheap and deciding didn't, so the outcome craft finally gets its experiments, and what the trio is for is the insight analysis cannot give*. M1 `apt101-building-rationed-it`: "The post-it was not naive. It was what the craft shrank to when building was expensive." M1 `apt101-building-is-cheap`: "Every other step is a decision ... None of those got cheaper." M2 `apt101-why-it-agreed`: "Agents amplify the way a team already works. They do not transform it." M3 `apt101-three-jobs-rewritten`: "When building took most of a team's weeks, leading meant rationing that capacity". The frame names where it breaks, explicitly and on a slide titled for it. M3 `apt101-where-you-go-from-here`: "This is where 'building got cheap, deciding didn't' stops being an edge on its own. When rivals build as cheaply, speed is the entry price". That clears the 100 rung's letter. It loses points because a stretch of M2 reads as a method tour that does not need the frame. M2 `apt101-widen-before-you-choose` ("1-2-4-All, from Liberating Structures, builds that in"), M2 `apt101-safe-to-say-its-wrong` (Edmondson's definition) and M2 `apt101-each-of-you-makes-something` (Houde and Hill's prototype taxonomy) are good craft slides, but they would read the same in a 2015 workshop. 90.

## Narrative

**A.** You arrive able to steer one session and trust what it says. You learn the session mirrors you, so you plan, push back, and write rules. Then you send a big task off un-packaged and it underdelivers. M5 `diagnose-and-resend`: "The un-packaged run was supposed to underdeliver. What came back is data, not blame." You package the task against the failure you actually read, re-send it, and diff the two runs. The close then shows the training failing at its own method. M6 `story-of-module-6`: "The agent I was working with had just finished writing those three modules. It still opened with the un-packaged shape." The ending is open on purpose. M6 `agents-that-build-agents`: "There is no last turn." The turn is lived by the student, and the training's own failure sits on the page as the hinge of the close. 95.

**B.** The trio arrives knowing the outcome craft, which building had rationed into post-its. Building gets cheap, so they write a bet that could lose and send an agent overnight to make the digest. The digest comes back agreeing with their favourite hypothesis, and the turn is that the agreement came from their own brief. M2 `apt101-why-it-agreed`: "So the agreement may come from your question, not from your customers." They go back to customers, widen the options, and put the bet in front of five users. That pays off the turn. M3 `apt101-bet-meets-five-users`: "Which of them would you have found by reading the digest alone?" M3 `apt101-bet-meets-five-users`: "A slice that comes back no has done its job." They hand a proposal to the team, and the ending is open. M3 `apt101-where-you-go-from-here`: "put it to the test on Monday." The turn is lived and it is the student's own failure, which is strong. It is not the training's own failure. The guide's failures (the installer, Red Queen) sit beside the plot rather than being its hinge. 85.

## Point of view

**A.** The guide is Antti: an engineer-author who built this module with Claude and drifted. For most of the training the guide speaks through second-person instruction and named practitioners (Klaassen, Horthy, Bainbridge). He then surfaces in one full first-person passage, with dates, counts and his own misses. M6 `story-of-module-6`: "Five taste reversals from me on Claude's confident recommendations." M6 `story-of-module-6`: "The closer's Ramp paragraph ... Claude had written a paraphrase and presented it as attribution." M6 `story-of-module-6`: "I drifted in every one of the ways this story just walked. I fixed what I caught. The loop caught what I missed." M6 `story-of-module-6`: "Your turn." The student stays the hero. The guide's own failure is on the page, but it lives in one passage rather than recurring across the six modules. 94.

**B.** The guide is again Antti, a product builder who has shipped the wrong thing. He shows up in four places, all as attributed essay quotes. M1 `apt101-building-is-cheap`: "One of the first things we tried building was a local installer ... It got working and it was used just a bit. Bad idea". M2 `apt101-why-it-agreed`: "I used to think of being wrong as failure. But being wrong does not diminish you as a person." M3 `apt101-from-us-to-the-team`: "We built good things. We failed to share them well." M3 `apt101-where-you-go-from-here`: "I still make mistakes. More than I'd like." The guide's failures are on the page, and they sit at the right beats (cheap building, being wrong, sharing, the close). Each one is a framed epigraph with a byline and date, though, not a narrated experience. Between them the voice is an impersonal "you" plus a long roll of cited authorities (Cutler, Seiden, Christensen, Torres, Cagan, Bland, Fitzpatrick, Edmondson, Marquet, Klein, Nielsen, Ries, Kniberg). The reader meets the guide's scars but never watches him get one. 84.

## Stance

**A.** Positions it could lose a customer over, each defended by a mechanism or a scar:
- *A rule in context is not a rule in the output.* Defended by a scar, M6 `story-of-module-6`: "Same rule, same rules file, same task, four separate violations across four independent LLM instances."
- *The model gives you a forecast, not a measurement.* Defended by mechanism, M5 `what-packaging-is`: "a well-read average of what other people published about other repos, frozen at a cutoff ... A prediction is not a measurement."
- *Green is a claim, not proof.* Defended by mechanism, M5 `the-gate-is-a-claim`: "The agent is an optimizer aimed straight at your gate ... optimization pressure finds the cheapest path to green."
- *More reach without checks is less checking.* Defended in M2 `when-a-plan-is-good`: "Push reach past what you can check and you have not delegated more. You are checking less."

Against its own interest, it calls today's playbooks, its own included, "**candidates**" (M5 `what-packaging-is`), and it admits "nobody has that part figured out yet" (M6 `agents-that-build-agents`). 92.

**B.** Positions it could lose a customer over:
- *Agents amplify, they do not transform.* Defended by mechanism, M2 `apt101-why-it-agreed`: "A team that ships without asking whether it worked now ships more of it, faster, and still never finds out."
- *One agent per job, not one company brain.* Defended by mechanism, M1 `apt101-it-runs-overnight`: "a fix for one job shifts the answers for the others, and when something goes wrong nobody can say which job's instructions did it."
- *Re-asking is not checking.* Defended by mechanism, M2 `apt101-fluent-is-not-true`: "Large language models generate the next likely word. Not the next true word ... Later models will fabricate less; they won't stop."
- *An agent's instructions are not the agent.* Defended by mechanism, M3 `apt101-from-us-to-the-team`: "The work lives in the memory you built up ... in every correction you made".
- *Criteria rot into a dashboard.* Defended in M3 `apt101-what-good-means`: "A team that writes for its own criteria has rebuilt the feature factory with a dashboard."
- *Leave sources out.* Held at a cost, M1 `apt101-it-runs-overnight`: "It will cost you a source somebody wanted in. Leaving it out is still the right call".

Against its own interest, M3 `apt101-where-you-go-from-here` says "Cheap building helps your rivals too", and then: "What would change this training's mind: agents that start deciding well". That is close to the 100 rung. The mechanisms are mostly borrowed from cited authors rather than earned by a scar of its own, which keeps it below A. 87.

## The pair

**A.** It doubts its own tools and its own making (story-of-module-6, "the gate is a claim too", "candidates") and still holds hard positions. It reads as someone who has been there.

**B.** It doubts as well: the digest is wrong, the check is untested, the frame breaks against rivals, "I still make mistakes". It holds its ground on the feature factory, the company brain and the instructions-are-not-the-agent pitch. It reads as someone who has been there, but someone who has read more than they have shown. The doubt lands on the student's agent and on the field, and seldom on the training's own method. Both are present, with the stance carried by citation rather than by scar.
