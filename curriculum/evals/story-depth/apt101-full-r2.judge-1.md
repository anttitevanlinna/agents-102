## Frame

**A — AE101.** Frame in one sentence: *agentic engineering is engineering — a control loop where a check outside the session, not the agent's account, is what you trust, and every session compounds into the system.* Modules that only make sense through it:
- M1 the-machine-you-just-met: "A check from outside the session resets the chain. A failing test does not care how confident the answer sounded."
- M3 the-loop-half-filled: "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller**... Cut the feedback signal (a test, a check, a read) and it drifts."
- M5 what-packaging-is: "**A check is a position fix**. At a fix the wedge of possible states collapses to a point."
- Where it breaks, on the page: M5 the-gate-is-a-claim "Green is a claim about the check, not a fact about the work"; M6 agents-that-build-agents "The goal is giving the system the right information, and nobody has that part figured out yet."
Score 94: every module reads through the lens and the lens doubts its own instrument; the break is implied (bitter lesson) more than named as "here the frame fails".

**B — APT101.** Frame: *building got cheap, deciding didn't — so the trio's job is the call (outcome, bet, the no) that no analysis makes.*
- Day 1 apt101-building-is-cheap: "Agents make the slow step cheap. The hard steps stay where they were." and apt101-write-the-bet lecture "Agents make much of the third one cheap... The first two they cannot settle for you."
- Day 2 apt101-why-it-agreed: "When agents analyse wider, deeper and faster than you can, analysis stops being the scarce part. So what is the insight only your team can bring?"
- Day 3 apt101-three-jobs-rewritten: "Your insight is the choice it leaves you with... That is real strategy work, and it does not get cheaper when building does."
- Frame breaks, named: Day 3 apt101-where-you-go-from-here "This is where 'building got cheap, deciding didn't' stops being an edge on its own... What would change this training's mind: agents that start deciding well."
Score 91: the 100-anchor clause (frame names where it breaks) is met on the page; docked because the mechanics-heavy beats (apt101-build-your-product-memory, apt101-gather-the-evidence) read through A101's memory lens, not this one, and because the frame is lectured in full before the student has done anything (two opening lectures precede the box).

## Narrative

**A.** Five sentences: An engineer learns the agent's report is a hypothesis and that checks reset the chain. They plan, map, threat-model, and build a kit on short loops where they are the feedback. Then they send a real task off un-packaged and it comes back wrong in ways they could not see. They read the return through three lenses, package against their own worst failure, and re-send — and then the training shows its own author drifting through the same failures. The turn: M5 reading-the-return "The closing summary is not the artefact. The machine would rather produce something than admit nothing"; M4 set-the-markers-send-it-off "send it off plainly and you find out what you can't steer yet"; M6 story-of-module-6 "The agent I was working with had just finished writing those three modules. It still opened with the un-packaged shape." Score 93: a lived turn, and the training's own failure is on the page as a second turn.

**B.** Five sentences: A trio arrives with a product and a remembered feature nobody used. They paint their box, write a bet that can lose, and brief overnight agents in their own words — each writing a look-for line. Day 2 the digests come back agreeing with them, and they discover the agreement came from their own sentence. They build checks, choose a bet on evidence, imagine it failed, and flip tonight's question. Day 3 five outsiders use the slice, each person names the line of their own Day 1 hypothesis the users proved wrong, and the Day 1 question is asked again of Monday's plan. The turn as a sequence:
- seed, Day 1 apt101-send-it-off: "say in one sentence what the digest should look for in your material about it. Your words, the way you would brief a colleague."
- recognition, Day 2 apt101-read-the-digest Phase 3: "Does the headline say what your bet says? Which sentence of yours asked for it?"
- naming, Day 2 apt101-why-it-agreed: "The agreement came from your question, not from your customers."
- payoff, Day 3 apt101-five-users Phase 4: "says out loud the line in it the five users proved wrong. Your own line, not someone else's."
- ring, Day 3 close: "is anything in `team/monday.md` the next thing your team builds that nobody asked for?"
Score 86: a turn the student lives (80), seeded unannounced and recognised before named — clearly up from r1. Not 100: the turn is the student's failure the training designed, not the training's own.
