# Percentage-gain studies can't answer who pulls ahead

Pushback on the reading that Cui et al. (N=4,867 developers) and Brynjolfsson et al. (N=5,172 support agents) refute the amplification bet at individual level: the juniors-gain-more finding is valid but **not generalisable**, and a broad practitioner convergence holds that those who already know their craft are the ones who knock it out of the park.

Correct, and the mechanism is the metric axis. Both studies measure *percentage gain against each worker's own baseline* on *bounded task volume* — an axis that mechanically flatters a low baseline (30% on a small number can be fewer absolute tasks than 5% on a large one), and neither reports absolute spread. Deeper: the authors' own stated mechanism is that the tool disseminates able workers' tacit knowledge and moves newcomers down the experience curve. That measures **catch-up to known best practice, not who extends the frontier.** Only the first has been studied.

**Gap:** no **absolute-output** distributional cut exists for agentic tools — spread in tasks or PRs actually shipped, by prior skill level. Every published study is percentage-of-own-baseline, and every large-N source (METR, DX, DORA, Faros) either stops at aggregates or segments by adoption rather than outcome. The percentage axis cannot settle this at any N, so more of the same studies will not help.
