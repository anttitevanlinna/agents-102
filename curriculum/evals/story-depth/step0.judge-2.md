### 2. The agent's self-report is a hypothesis, not the ground truth
One-line claim: *what the agent tells me it did, or that it's done, is generated text shaped like a log — verify it against the artifact, not against its account of the artifact.*

- **Complicated (mechanism):** `curriculum/lectures/the-machine-you-just-met.md` (M1) — "The agent chat is an abstraction... Current state lives on disk... a fresh read, not from recall."

- **Complicated (long runs):** `curriculum/lectures/what-keeps-a-long-running-session-going.md` (M4) — "It declares done on the part it managed. Tests pass, the summary is confident, and the piece it quietly dropped goes unmentioned."

### 3. Trust is portable verification, not watching harder — and the verifier is itself unverified until proven
One-line claim: *earning trust in unattended agent work means building checks that run without me, but every check is itself a claim that needs its own verification, and building it erodes exactly the vigilance that would catch its blind spots.*

- **Complicated (direct tension):** `curriculum/lectures/ironies-of-automation.md` (M4 closer) — "Trust and vigilance move in opposite directions... Earned trust is exactly what breeds the miss." This is a genuine counter-voice to M3's Big Idea, not a restatement: it shows that building verification erodes the human capacity needed to catch what verification misses.
- **Complicated (bottleneck):** `curriculum/lectures/what-keeps-a-long-running-session-going.md` (M4) — names **backpressure**: "the push from a slower downstream stage... slow down, stop, or redirect."
- **Paid off (method):** `curriculum/exercises/diagnose-and-resend.md` + `curriculum/lectures/what-packaging-is.md` (M5) — the reference/plan.md/verifier three-pattern, built against a diagnosed failure, and the A/B contrast: un-packaged vs packaged.
- **Complicated recursively:** `curriculum/lectures/the-gate-is-a-claim.md` (M5) — "A gate only means what the gate can see. Green is a claim about the check, not a fact about the work." ## Audience fit

Fit. The learnings are built entirely from a software-engineer IC's own working surface: `CLAUDE.md`/`CLAUDE.local.md`, plan mode, git worktrees, ADRs, STRIDE, test-strategy skills, verifiers and hooks, PR review, rules-file pruning. Governors are things an IC does on their own keyboard between commits — "pick No, keep planning," "write a failing test," "cut the rule." Even the closing frame (`curriculum/lectures/agents-that-build-agents.md`, "Cross personal → team") stays keyed to an individual engineer's own practice growing into a team artifact via a normal PR, not to organizational strategy, leadership framing, or a CEO/CTO audience. No learning reads as written for someone managing engineers rather than being one.

## Weakest factor

Earned, not announced is the factor with the thinnest evidence, because the pattern that holds is narrower than it first looks: the training is disciplined about earning *names* (sycophancy arrives after four exercises in `curriculum/lectures/the-machine-you-just-met.md`; "Plan-mode approval inflation" arrives after two push-backs in `curriculum/exercises/push-back-on-the-plan.md`; the reference/plan.md/verifier three-pattern is explicitly designed to be "named after you have built each piece" in `curriculum/lectures/what-packaging-is.md`), but every module states its underlying *claim* as a `## Big Idea` header before the student has done anything in that module — M3's "Trust isn't watching harder" and M1's "The LLM mirrors your stance" are both asserted as thesis statements at the point of entry, with the exercises that follow demonstrating rather than generating them. The smallest concrete change that would strengthen this factor: move (or shadow) the Big Idea line to close a module instead of open it — state it as a question or omit it at open, and let `## Key Concepts` or the closing lecture supply the named claim after the exercises have run, the way `what-packaging-is.md` already does for the three-pattern.
