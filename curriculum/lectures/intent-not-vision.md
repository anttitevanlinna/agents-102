# Intent, not vision

## Say where you are heading, not what it looks like

A vision describes a destination nobody on your team has seen. It asks them to believe a picture, and the first week of real work usually contradicts it.

An intent names a direction you can run now: which of your two crux you are moving, which way, and what the team's week should look like if it works. It also says what you do not know yet. You declare it, you run it, and you revise it when the running teaches you something.

The idea is old. Moltke's armies ran on it: tell people the aim and why, and let them choose how. What held it back was the cost of coordinating between the aim and the work. Agents change that cost, and a paragraph of intent is something both your people and your agents can read.

## Two bets credible leaders are making

**Run the change on the hierarchy you have.** Fin (Intercom) and Ramp kept their structure and pushed agents through it: a public goal, visible progress, a team whose job is making everyone else faster. Fin's CTO put it as one line to the whole R&D org, [*"All technical work is becoming agent-first."*](https://ideas.fin.ai/p/2x-nine-months-later) That is an intent, not a vision: a direction, a priority, and room to find out how.

**Treat the hierarchy as routing agents can replace.** Block's CEO argues that layers of management exist to move information up and down, and that agents now do that job. His company is normalizing to three roles: builders, owners of a specific problem, and player-coaches who build and develop people. [*"There is no need for a permanent middle management layer."*](https://block.xyz/inside/from-hierarchy-to-intelligence) He also writes that parts of it will likely break before they work.

The first bet has results from teams that ran it. The second has an argument and early execution. Your own role sits inside the question, and you get to ask it as seriously as anyone. This training does not pick a side for you.

## What nobody knows yet

Most of the evidence comes from fast-moving software companies, and they chose aggressive stances. Whether the same moves work in a Nordic organisation with different norms about work and change is still open.

Nobody has tracked it far past the early adopters either. What leading this looks like once most of your team is fluent with agents has not been written yet.

Your intent is one way that gets written. It runs on your team, with your people, and what you learn is evidence nobody else has.

## Vision is what running earns

You will not have a vision at the end of this module, and you do not need one. You need a paragraph you can run: bound by your two crux, clear enough that someone can disagree with it, and short enough to change.

The picture fills in as you run it. Ninety days in, you will describe where the team is heading with far more confidence than you could invent now, because you will have seen it.
