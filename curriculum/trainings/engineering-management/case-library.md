## Ramp, the non-software-engineering-team lens

Source: [`continuous-research/observations/ramp.md`](../../../continuous-research/observations/ramp.md)

Ramp is the companion case to Fin (Intercom). Where Fin shows the eng-manager-of-software-engineers view (PR throughput, plugin marketplace, auto-approval), Ramp shows the eng-manager-adjacent view: software managers whose teams ship tools that non-engineers use, and the cultural infrastructure around it.

- **M1 (diagnose):** the L0–L3 ladder is the ADKAR/Chasm lens made legible to managers. Arguably the cleanest classifier in the research base for where a team member sits.
- **M2 (first move):** "Match the mandate to the tooling" + "The second best time to start is today" = the M2 posture.
- **M3 (coalition):** "Early converts" = the coalition. "Give people a stage" = how to make them visible. Spotlight + pairing mechanics are M3 moves.
- **M4 (vision):** "We will become the most productive company in the world" (Jan 2025 kickoff) + "All technical work is agent-first" (Fin) = two canonical vision statements with teeth.
- **M5 (conditions + exercise):** the leaderboard IS the verification exercise at org scale. All-hands demo slots are the exercise at team scale. "Remove every constraint" = the negative-space conditions, what the manager removes rather than adds.
- **M6 (signals + plan):** the eight moves map to signals a manager can watch in their own team. Ramp's measurement set (sessions, skills, apps, tools connected) is a candidate dashboard.

**Transfer caveats** (these stay on the research file too, because they qualify the case itself, not the teaching):

- Velocity-biased culture as a starting condition, and Charles is explicit about it. Not every company can adopt the playbook without accepting that some of the culture work is upstream of the AI work.
- Fintech B2B platform with a large product/engineering share of headcount. Easier to get non-engineers into a coding-agent workflow when the whole company works in a software product. Nordic manufacturing, retail and logistics may not transfer 1:1.
- *"If you're L0 you will most likely not be at the company"* is culturally aggressive for Nordic employers.

## Fin (Intercom), the change-model anchor

Source: [`continuous-research/observations/intercom.md`](../../../continuous-research/observations/intercom.md)

The strongest anchor case in the base, because it carries evidence at every stage of a Kotter-style change model rather than at one.

- **Diagnose:** the 5-dimension per-person tiering is exactly the ADKAR-style diagnostic artifact.
- **First move:** "Be at peace with potentially imperfect measures" = the operating stance. PR throughput = the one-imperfect-metric-that-creates-pressure example.
- **Coalition:** team-2x plus the plugin marketplace = the guiding coalition as a product.
- **Vision:** the 2x memo itself, plus the "all technical work is becoming agent-first" principle.
- **Conditions + exercise:** the auto-approval system as the verification exercise. PR mandate escalation (80% → 95%) as conditions made enforceable.
- **Signals + plan:** the nine metrics plus day-91-style transparency is the signal dashboard. Exponential spend against declining cost-per-PR is the compound signal.

**Naming:** the company renamed from Intercom to **Fin** on 2026-05-12; Intercom survives as the customer-service product label. Sources published before that date say Intercom and are correct for their date. Say "Fin (Intercom)" in student-facing body text.

## Block, thesis, not evidence

Source: [`continuous-research/observations/block.md`](../../../continuous-research/observations/block.md)

Use **sparingly, and as thesis rather than as evidence of what works.** The risk is specific: cited as "this is where engineering management is going," it makes the buyer's own role feel disposable. That is the wrong mood for M1–M3 and the wrong framing for M4.

Best-fit uses:

- **M4 (vision) lecture**, the historical arc (Roman → Prussian → railroad → Taylor → matrix → now) is a concise and intellectually respectable way to frame why the current moment differs. Sets up the "AI-first is not static" principle.
- **M6 (signals + 90-day plan) reflection**, Dorsey's qualifier ("what does your company understand that is genuinely hard to understand, and is that understanding getting deeper every day?") is a strong reflection prompt: *"What is your team learning that gets deeper every day?"*
- **M4 alternative vision statement**, *"We normalize down to three roles: ICs, DRIs, player-coaches. There is no need for a permanent middle management layer."* Offer as a canonical example of a vision statement with teeth, not as prescription.

Avoid:

- **Not as the M1 diagnostic baseline.** The ADKAR diagnostic assumes people stay in roles that exist; Dorsey's thesis collapses some of those roles. M1 is not the place to open that question.
- **Not as inevitable.** One CEO's thesis, early execution, explicit about its own likely failure modes. The training holds the tension rather than resolving it.
