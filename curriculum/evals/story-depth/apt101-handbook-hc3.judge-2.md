## Frame

**A — Agentic Engineering 101.** Frame in one sentence: *agentic engineering is engineering; an agent is a control loop that drifts until a check outside it fixes its position, and what you learn compounds only once it is on disk.* Three modules that only make sense through it:
- M1 `the-machine-you-just-met`: "A check from outside the session resets the chain. A failing test does not care how confident the answer sounded."
- M3 `the-loop-half-filled`: "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller**. ... Cut the feedback signal (a test, a check, a read) and it drifts. Signal quality is part of the law: a flaky test is a closed loop that still drifts."
- M5 `what-packaging-is`: "**A check is a position fix**. At a fix the wedge of possible states collapses to a point." And M5 `the-gate-is-a-claim`: "Green is a claim about the check, not a fact about the work."
Where it breaks, named: M5 `the-gate-is-a-claim`, "Sutton's **bitter lesson** ... Today's right procedure, your gates and workflow, yours or the agent's, is superseded too", and M6 `agents-that-build-agents`, "Writing things down is not the whole of it. The goal is giving the system the right information, and nobody has that part figured out yet." The frame carries every module and names its limit. **94.**

**B — Agentic Product Teams 101.** Frame in one sentence: *building got cheap and deciding didn't, so the outcome craft finally gets its experiments, and the trio's job becomes the insight and the call.* Three modules through it:
- M1 `apt101-building-is-cheap`: "Agents make the slow step cheap. The hard steps stay where they were." And M1 `apt101-building-rationed-it`: "The post-it was not naive. It was what the craft shrank to when building was expensive."
- M2 `apt101-why-it-agreed`: "When agents analyse wider, deeper and faster than you can, analysis stops being the scarce part. So what is the insight only your team can bring?"
- M3 `apt101-three-jobs-rewritten`: "Your insight is the choice it leaves you with. ... That is real strategy work, and it does not get cheaper when building does."
Where it breaks, named on its own slide: M3 `apt101-where-you-go-from-here`, "This is where 'building got cheap, deciding didn't' stops being an edge on its own. When rivals build as cheaply, speed is the entry price", plus "What would change this training's mind: agents that start deciding well." That earns the top rung's element. What holds it below A: the lens often arrives as a tagline bolted onto a borrowed method rather than as the reason the slide exists. M2 `apt101-each-of-you-makes-something`: "With agents, the finished look got cheap. Picking the one question a prototype should answer did not." M2 `apt101-widen-before-you-choose`: "Merging the pile now takes an agent seconds. Choosing which idea survives it takes the same people it always did." Many slides would teach the same method with the closing line removed. **87.**

## Narrative

**A.** You arrive expecting a clever-prompt trick and get shown that context steers everything. You learn to steer one session, then a plan, then a kit of skills, all while you can see each result. In M4 you send a real task off un-packaged and it underdelivers: "Session one goes now, un-packaged: no plan.md, no verifier, no reference artifact" (M4 `test-and-learn`). **The turn** comes in M5: "The un-packaged run was supposed to underdeliver. What came back is data, not blame" (M5 `diagnose-and-resend`), and the same task goes out packaged, "The task is the same, packaged this time, and the prompt shrank." M6 then shows the turn as the training's own failure: "The agent I was working with had just finished writing those three modules. It still opened with the un-packaged shape" (M6 `story-of-module-6`). The student lives the turn, and the authors lived it while making the training. **93.**

**B.** A product trio who always knew the outcome craft finds that building, once rationed, is now cheap: "A hypothesis can meet a working slice the same day" (M1 `apt101-building-is-cheap`). They write a bet that could lose and send a digest agent off overnight, already warned: "Some of those lines will be wrong" (M1 `apt101-it-runs-overnight`). The digest comes back agreeing with their favourite hypothesis. **The turn**: "Open the brief you wrote for the digest agent. ... If it does, the agent did as asked. ... So the agreement may come from your question, not from your customers" (M2 `apt101-why-it-agreed`). They go back to customers, put a slice in front of five users, and learn what the digest could never have said: "Often it could not have. The digest summarised what customers had already written and said, and nobody had used this slice yet" (M3 `apt101-bet-meets-five-users`). Then they carry a proposal, not a decision, to their team. The turn is designed and the student lives it with their own brief, so it clears 80. It is the student's failure, not the training's own. The plot also goes slack across stretches of M2 (`widen-before-you-choose`, `safe-to-say-its-wrong`, `each-of-you-makes-something`) and the second half of M3 (`three-jobs-rewritten`, `from-us-to-the-team`), which read as method cards between plot beats. **84.**

## Point of view

**A.** Mostly second-person instruction, and the guide shows in lines: "You're new to this country. A tourist runs an agent and hopes; a practitioner runs a test and reads the data" (M4 `test-and-learn`); "Everyone sees how this will bloat almost immediately" (M1 `compound-and-close`). Then a full first-person passage from a named narrator, with dated numbers and the narrator's own failure: "I pushed back several times on Claude saying it was 'done' before it actually was"; "Claude had written a paraphrase and presented it as attribution"; "I drifted in every one of the ways this story just walked. I fixed what I caught. The loop caught what I missed." Signed "Antti" (M6 `story-of-module-6`). The guide's own failure is on the page as narrative, and the student stays the hero ("Your turn."). **95.**

**B.** Second person throughout. A first-person guide appears in four set-off essay excerpts, each signed:
- "One of the first things we tried building was a local installer for developer tooling. It got working and it was used just a bit. Bad idea, as the need wasn't a daily one." (M1 `apt101-building-is-cheap`)
- "I used to think of being wrong as failure. ... My creations are not me and not everything has to be perfect." (M2 `apt101-why-it-agreed`)
- "We built good things. We failed to share them well." (M3 `apt101-from-us-to-the-team`)
- "I still make mistakes. More than I'd like. The difference is I make them faster now." (M3 `apt101-where-you-go-from-here`)
The guide's failures are on the page, so the top rung's element is present. They come as quotations, though, not as a narrator in the room: one or two sentences each, no session, no specifics of what broke or how it was caught, and nothing from the making of this training. The installer is the only one with a concrete story, and its stakes are low ("Lucky it did not take that long to build it"). A narrator with stated experience and thin, quoted scars. **85.**

## Stance

**A.** Positions it could lose a customer over, each defended:
- *The model forecasts; it does not measure.* "Ask for best practice and that is what answers: a well-read average ... what it holds about your next run is a forecast. ... **A prediction is not a measurement.**" (M5 `what-packaging-is`). Mechanism: training cutoff plus the absence of local evidence.
- *A rule in context is not a rule in the output.* "The rules were loaded every time ... Claude typed the banned word anyway" (M6 `story-of-module-6`). Defended by a scar.
- *Your own gate is suspect.* "A gate nobody has verified is a gate trusted on vibes" with Goodhart and a hold-out (M5 `the-gate-is-a-claim`).
- *Automation erodes the watcher.* "The trust is deserved. The watching still has to be engineered" (M4 `ironies-of-automation`). Mechanism: Bainbridge.
Against its own commercial interest: weaker. "This training stops short of the full system" (M2 `how-instructions-grow`) and the bitter-lesson retire clause concede limits. Neither tells the buyer to spend less on the training. **92.**

**B.** Positions, each defended:
- *Customers before agents, against the training's own sale.* "So a team that does not talk with its customers every week should start there, before agents. ... That sells less of this training. It is still where to start." (M2 `apt101-go-back-to-your-customers`). Mechanism: "Agents build them in days, and the team ships them before any customer can say a guess was wrong." This is the top rung's element, on the page.
- *Agents amplify; they do not transform.* "A faster feature factory is still a feature factory ... A team that ships without asking whether it worked now ships more of it, faster, and still never finds out." (M2 `apt101-why-it-agreed`)
- *No company brain; one agent per job.* "A fix for one job shifts the answers for the others, and when something goes wrong nobody can say which job's instructions did it." (M1 `apt101-it-runs-overnight`)
- *You cannot share the whole agent.* "The page was never where the work lived." (M3 `apt101-from-us-to-the-team`)
- *Fabrication is structural.* "This isn't a bug that gets patched in the next release. It's the shape of the technology. Later models will fabricate less; they won't stop." (M2 `apt101-fluent-is-not-true`)
- *Leave sources out at a cost.* "It will cost you a source somebody wanted in. Leaving it out is still the right call" (M1 `apt101-it-runs-overnight`).
Mechanisms throughout, plus one position against its own interest. What holds it under A: most positions are product-craft orthodoxy that a Torres or Cagan reader already holds, and they are carried by the borrowed authority. Few would cost a customer. The edgiest ones (no company brain, fabrication is structural, no shareable agent) are the field-facing ones, and they stay a slide each. Only the AE101-derived lines are defended by a scar rather than a citation. **88.**

## The pair

**A.** Doubt and ground together. It doubts its own verifier ("The check you built is itself a claim that wants verifying"), its own self-grade prompt ("The grade is biased by design ... same-window self-charity", M3 `author-test-strategy-skill`) and its authors, while holding firm on forecast-vs-measurement and rules-leak. **Reads as someone who has been there.**

**B.** Both are present. Ground: customers before agents, the feature factory, no company brain. Doubt: about the trio's own check ("A pass is a claim about a check nobody has tested", M3 `apt101-what-good-means`), the trio's own criteria ("A team that writes for its own criteria has rebuilt the feature factory with a dashboard"), and the frame itself (`where-the-frame-breaks`). The doubt points at the agent and at the student's machinery. It almost never points at this training's own instruments or its making. **Reads as someone who has been there,** but through quotations: closer to a well-read curator than a scarred practitioner, with no tilt toward either hedge or pitch.
