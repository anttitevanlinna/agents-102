### [Finding 5] MAJOR — Parallel Session Ceiling: First Empirical Data; BCG "AI Brain Fry" Names the Cliff; CHI 2026 Confirms Verification Load

**Source 1 (BCG survey — closest to measurement):** `https://builtin.com/articles/ai-brain-fry-software-developers` — [domain trade publication] (2026, citing BCG study ~1,500 workers)
**Source 2 (CHI 2026 verification load):** `https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3772318.3791176` — [academic/research — CHI 2026] (2026)
**Source 3 (CHI 2026 concurrent sessions gap):** `https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772318.3790850` — [academic/research — CHI 2026] (2026)
**Source 4 (Osmani — 3-4 threads):** `https://addyosmani.com/blog/cognitive-parallel-agents/` — [practitioner direct — Addy Osmani]
**Source 5 (Product Hunt forum cluster):** `https://www.producthunt.com/p/claude/how-many-claude-codes-do-you-run-in-parallel` — [practitioner direct]
**Source 6 (BCG/Cherny high outlier):** `https://x.com/bcherny/status/2017742743125299476` — [practitioner direct — Boris Cherny / Claude Code creator]
**Date:** August 17, 2026
**Agent level:** personal
**What:**

**BCG study (~1,500 workers):** Productivity increases with 1st, 2nd, and 3rd concurrent AI agents but declines when taking on a 4th. BCG named the overload state "AI brain fry" — insufficient working memory and attention to manage AI agent interactions. Survey statistics: 14% of workers experienced "mental hangover" distinct from ordinary tiredness; workers with AI brain fry 33% more likely to experience decision fatigue, 11% more likely to make minor coding/formatting errors, **39% more likely to make major blunders affecting safety or decisions.** Note: survey-based self-reported cognitive state, not controlled performance measurement.

**CHI 2026 — "When Help Hurts" (verification load):** Developers lack "mode-agnostic index" for process costs of checking AI output. Researchers developed verification-load composite (compile/test failures, time-to-first-compile, churn, pauses, interface switches) that statistically mediates repeated-use stress and fatigue trajectories. Confirms fatigue is measurable, not anecdotal. Does not give a session-count ceiling.

**CHI 2026 — "Code with Me or for Me?":** AI agents enable 35% more task correctness with ~50% less user effort vs. copilots. Explicitly notes: "Measurements of time-spent on each task are unreliable for the fraction of developers who use multiple AI agents concurrently" — **names concurrent session measurement as a methodological gap in the research literature.**

**Practitioner self-report cluster (all Level 1 — no measurement):**
- Osmani: "3-4 threads depending on complexity"; "scope per thread matters more than session count"
- Willison: 4 sessions, "depleted by 11am"; "human cognition is the bottleneck"
- Product Hunt forum: majority 1-2; Abdallah "4-5 before realizing cognitive limitations"; Zheng "2 optimal"; de Jong "max 2 different tasks"; Cheng "cognitive load is the main blocker, draining if round-robin through agents"
- Cherny (Claude Code creator, outlier): 10-15 sessions via combined terminal + browser + mobile; no cognitive load discussion
- The "30-40 sessions, 10-15 actively managed" claim (from 2026-08-08 user signal): NO corroboration found. Boris Cherny (the creator) is the highest named practitioner at 10-15. The 30-40 figure appears to be a specific community or individual claim not published publicly.

**Absorption bottleneck confirmation:** Research gap confirmed — no study examines whether merged outputs from parallel sessions are reviewed, integrated, or ignored. CHI 2026's methodological-gap statement is the closest acknowledgment.
**Evidence level:** Level 2 (BCG survey = self-reported cognitive state, ~1,500 workers; CHI 2026 = academic research but no session-count ceiling; practitioner cluster = Level 1 opinions; strong directional convergence on ~3-4 cognitive ceiling)
**Key claims:**
- BCG: productivity peaks at 3 concurrent sessions; declines at 4th; "AI brain fry" = 39% higher major-blunder rate
- CHI 2026: concurrent session measurement is a confirmed methodological gap in the research literature
- Practitioner cluster converges on 3-4 sessions as cognitive ceiling (Osmani, Willison, community)
- Boris Cherny (outlier) at 10-15: combines terminal + browser + mobile, structured differently
- "30-40 sessions" claim: no public corroboration; remains from user signal only
- Absorption bottleneck (merged outputs nobody takes up): confirmed absent from research literature
