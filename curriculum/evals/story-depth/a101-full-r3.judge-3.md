## Frame

**A — 93.** Frame: agentic engineering is engineering: a closed control loop whose quality is set by the checks outside it, and a kit that compounds. Three modules that only make sense through it: M2 "A plan is a check before implementation." (plan-mode-done-right, when-a-plan-is-good); M3 "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller**... Cut the feedback signal (a test, a check, a read) and it drifts." (earn-the-trust, the-loop-half-filled); M5 "**A check is a position fix**. At a fix the wedge of possible states collapses to a point" (learn-from-the-test, what-packaging-is). Where it breaks is named: "Sutton's **bitter lesson**: built-in human knowledge wins today and loses to the next model... Retire what the next model outgrows" (M5, the-gate-is-a-claim) and "today's playbooks are **candidates**" (M5, what-packaging-is). The six-phase map gives every module a place on one drawing.

**B — 80.** Frame: an agent is a set of parts (context, tools, goal, checks, boundary, loop, interface), and every part is one decision about what you give the agent and what you keep back. Planted M1: "Context is the first piece of the agent. Tools, goals, checks, boundaries, and loops come later and depend on it." (getting-going, context-is-king). Turned in M4: "You have given the agent more: context, memory, more stances. Now give it less." (security, practice-of-risk). Paid M6: "The full picture: model, context, tools, goal, loop, checks, boundary, interface. Each was one decision about what you give the agent and what you keep back." (evaluations, new-human-role-in-the-loop). Breakpoint named: "Which half of this is obsolete next year?... Nobody in this room knows yet" (M8, where-is-this-all-going). Below 85 because M3 and M7 read on their own lenses (Rumelt, Jobs-to-be-Done) more than through the parts frame. M7 says "An agent is context, a boundary, a set of checks and somebody who answers for what it does" (access-is-not-absorption). That adds a part the frame never listed before and doesn't fold it back in.

## Narrative

**A — 94.** Story: an engineer arrives with tricks, runs one short loop and learns the machine mirrors them. They learn to read a plan as a check and build checks outside the session. Then they send a long task off un-packaged, on purpose, and it comes back wrong. The turn: "send it off plainly and you find out what you can't steer yet." (M4, set-the-markers-send-it-off), then "An unchecked session arrives confident, and wrong... The success report comes from the wrong harbor." (M5, what-packaging-is). They diagnose, package and re-send, and learn that the gate itself is a claim. Then the training's own failure: "The agent I was working with had just finished writing those three modules. It still opened with the un-packaged shape." (M6, story-of-module-6). Close: "There is no last turn." (M6, agents-that-build-agents).

**B — 80.** Story: a leader arrives a chat user and makes a site they call "yes, this is me" (M1, personal-site-with-guardrails). They build a memory that sharpens and a scheduled agent that runs without them. Then they split work across agents and are left unable to tell whether the answer is right: "You can't yet tell if the answer is right. That is expected. Name the doubt; don't fix it here." (M3, three-minds-one-synthesis). The unease deepens: "Would you bet your job on it being safe?" (M4, practice-of-risk); "The risk remains. That is expected. Name it, accept it on record, and stop there." (M4, audit-your-agent). Rescue by measurement in M5, leverage in M6 ("Walk away"), then the human wall in M7: "The proposal got neither a yes nor a no." (personal-to-team, access-is-not-absorption). The turn is lived, because the M3 doubt is created in the student's own files. But the rescue beat is thin on the page: "Phase 5: Close, name the rescue" holds only "The winner is saved as `judges/groundedness-judge.md`" (M5, hallucination-bakeoff). The training doubts its own close ("nothing in the room checked it. Hold that doubt.", M8), which is close to the 100 rung. It stops short of showing the training itself failing.
