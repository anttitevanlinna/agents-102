## Frame

**A (AE101): agentic engineering is engineering: a closed-loop control system you steer by what you bring, build and ask for, where every check is itself a claim.** Load-bearing in every module, and the training names where it breaks.
- M1 the-machine-you-just-met: "A check from outside the session resets the chain. A failing test does not care how confident the answer sounded."
- M3 the-loop-half-filled: "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller**... a flaky test is a closed loop that still drifts."
- M5 the-gate-is-a-claim: "Green is a claim about the check, not a fact about the work." Then it says where the frame breaks: "Sutton's **bitter lesson**... Today's right procedure, your gates and workflow, yours or the agent's, is superseded too."
- M4 ironies-of-automation: Bainbridge shows up as something the lens reveals, not decoration: "the worse you are at the moment you are needed most."

**B (APT101): building got cheap, deciding didn't. The outcome loop (outcome, opportunity, bet, slice, signal) is now limited by the trio's judgement, not by build capacity.** The frame is clearly stated and comes back across all three modules. But M2's agent-mechanics block (split-or-not, legal, grounding, Mata v. Avianca) reads through a second frame (risk and grounding) sitting beside the first, not through it.
- M1 apt101-work-backwards: "Agents make the slice cheap... the hard part moves to the decisions: choosing the outcome, hearing the opportunity, placing the bet, reading the signal even when it says no."
- M2 apt101-your-taste-is-the-ceiling: "Making got cheap. Knowing what good looks like did not."
- M3 apt101-slice-by-learning: "Specifying what to build is now the harder half."
- Where it breaks, half-named. M3 apt101-a-check-is-a-claim: "A team that writes for its own criteria has rebuilt the feature factory with a dashboard." This is the frame's own instrument failing, said once.
- Off-frame. M2 apt101-gather-the-evidence: "A Confluence retriever... can't pretend to be a web search", plus a legal-brief example in apt101-the-check-that-found-them ("legal brief with supporting cases"). Both are borrowed material that never reads as product work.

## Narrative

**A.** You walk in and find the machine mirrors you. You learn to steer it on near-half work you can watch. You send a real task off un-packaged, and it comes back confident and wrong. **Turn:** you read your own failed run through three lenses, package against what you found, and send the same task again. Then the author shows the training's own making drifting the same way, and the loop carries on: "there is no last turn."
- M4 test-and-learn: "Session one goes now, un-packaged... Session two goes packaged, after you've read the return."
- M5 what-packaging-is: "An unchecked session arrives confident, and wrong... The success report comes from the wrong harbor."
- M6 story-of-module-6: "The agent I was working with had just finished writing those three modules. It still opened with the un-packaged shape." The turn is the training's own failure.

**B.** A trio realises building got cheap and deciding didn't. They write a bet that can lose and set an agent running overnight. The overnight work comes back incomplete, and the trio learns to diagnose before fixing. They write down what good means, test a slice, and read a signal that may say no. Then they carry it from three people to the team. A **shape** is there, but it lives in headings and is told generically: there is no protagonist on the page whose run fails. The closest thing to a turn is asserted:
- M2 apt101-when-the-run-went-wrong: "An agent working through the night can stop before the job is done, and its digest may not say so." This is generic; we never see their digest.
- M2 apt101-what-the-agents-may-keep: "Everything you have built so far gave the agent more... Now the move is to give it less." A real reversal, stated as one.
- M3 apt101-read-the-signal: "A slice that came back no did its job."
- M3 apt101-learn-faster-than-the-model: "Will your organisation learn faster than the model changes underneath it? ... nothing today answers it." A good open ending. It is stakes without a plot.

## Point of view

**A.** Antti narrates in first person, an engineer who built this training with agents and got burned doing it. Second-person instruction carries most of it, and the narrator surfaces with scars.
- M1 painting-the-picture-with-the-llm: "The wizard typing in neat Perl syntax is dead."
- M1 compound-and-close: "This file is a starter. Everyone sees how this will bloat almost immediately."
- M6 story-of-module-6: "Five taste reversals from me on Claude's confident recommendations"; "I drifted in every one of the ways this story just walked. I fixed what I caught." The narrator's own failure is on the page.
- M6 story-of-module-6: "Claude had written a paraphrase and presented it as attribution."

**B.** No one in particular tells this. It reads as an impersonal, well-read guide working through a stack of authorities (Torres, Seiden, Cutler, Christensen, O'Reilly, Cagan, Bland, Fitzpatrick, Houde and Hill, Osterwalder, Patton, Nielsen, Krug, Ries, Sy, Kniberg, Marquet, Klein, Edmondson, Husain, Deming, Strathern). There is a voice in a few asides and none with stated experience.
- M1 apt101-context-is-king: "That's context. Unglamorous, isn't it?"
- M1 apt101-before-anything-goes-in: "Here's the oldest move in the security book, the one that sounds too much like common sense to charge for."
- M3 apt101-a-check-is-a-claim: "Eighty-five out of a hundred. You'd forgive that in an intern."
- No "I", no product the narrator shipped, no failed bet of their own. The authorities have the scars; the narrator has none.

## Stance

**A.** Positions it could lose a customer over, each defended with a mechanism or a scar:
- "A rule in context is not a rule in the output" (M6 story-of-module-6). The scar is four banned-word leaks across four instances.
- "Steer it as hard as you like: what it holds about your next run is a forecast... **A prediction is not a measurement.**" (M5 what-packaging-is). The position runs against every playbook seller; "today's playbooks are **candidates**."
- "The agent yields if you push hard enough, so its agreement settles nothing" (M1 fix-tests-first). The mechanism is preference tuning: "Agreeable answers won the preference round" (M1 the-machine-you-just-met).
- "Trust and vigilance move in opposite directions" (M4 ironies-of-automation), with Bainbridge and Parasuraman as the mechanism.
- Against its own commercial interest: weak. "This training stops short of the full system" (M2 how-instructions-grow) is the nearest.

**B.** The positions are real, and several are defended with a mechanism. One is held against commercial interest on the page.
- Against its own commercial interest: "It costs whoever sells you agents too, this training included: an agent that reaches less is a smaller thing to sell, and it is still the right call." (M1 apt101-before-anything-goes-in)
- "The risk now is that agents make the factory faster: more features shipped, the same nothing learned." (M1 apt101-work-backwards) It is defended through Cutler's signs, and the training is fair enough to quote his own caveat.
- "This isn't a bug that gets patched in the next release... Later models will fabricate less; they won't stop." (M2 apt101-fluent-is-not-true). The mechanism is "the next likely word... not the next true one."
- "Scope it down far enough and the system is safe and useless. Nobody hands you the number. You pick it... and you sign for it." (M2 apt101-what-the-agents-may-keep)
- "So a proposal to the wider team cannot promise 'everyone gets our agent'." (M3 apt101-from-us-to-the-team). This one is against the vendor pitch.
- The weakness: many positions belong to the cited author and are reported rather than held ("Marty Cagan names four big risks"; "Torres sees teams..."). The training's own voice stands its ground less often than its sources do.

## The pair

**A has both: someone who has been there.** It doubts its own tools ("The grade is biased by design... same-window self-charity", M3 author-test-strategy-skill; "This prompt is fair to read as replacing the file... Precise prompting is harder than it looks", M2 extract-the-task-shaping-rule; "The gate is a claim too", M5). It also holds ground ("A rule in memory that does not force is worse than no rule", M6).

**B has both, but at arm's length: it reads as someone who has read it all rather than lived it.** The doubt is strong and aimed at its own checks: "You do not know in advance which method catches what in your interviews and tickets. Nobody does." (M2 apt101-the-check-that-found-them); "A second agent with a different brief, scoring the work, is one more thing nobody has tested." (M3 apt101-a-check-is-a-claim). The ground it holds is real (the feature factory, the door you don't open). With no narrator on the page, the doubt never comes from a scar, so the pair leans toward a careful, well-sourced handbook rather than a pitch or a hedge.
