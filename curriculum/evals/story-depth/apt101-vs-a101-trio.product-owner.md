## A, as I read it.

This one teaches me to build agents. Each module adds one piece: a site, a memory, retrievers, a security check, a judge, a loop, a sharing plan. It would close my biggest gap, which is that I have never seen an agent read files, run for an hour and check its own work. But it is built around *my* challenge and *my* memory. My team is mostly absent until Module 7, and my actual job (tickets, priorities, what gets built) never really comes up. I would come back a better individual operator, not a better product owner.
Slides I'd remember on Monday: **"Mostly right, ten times over, is mostly wrong"** (85% per step across ten steps lands near 20%; I'll use that in the next planning meeting). **"Groundedness protects the floor. Steering raises the ceiling."** **"Would you let it send the mail?"** That one is the right question for our weekly stakeholder update.
Parts I'd tune out of: the LinkedIn personal-branding site (StoryBrand plus anti-branding about myself is not why my employer pays for three days). The four-window retriever choreography, which the handbook itself admits is "for the feeling". The skill-install mechanics. The closing line where the sponsor tells the room "You are now agent builders", which would get polite silence here.

## B, as I read it.

This one starts from something I recognise: the opportunity tree on the wall on Friday and the feature roadmap on Monday. Its claim is that building got cheap and deciding didn't, so the outcome craft I've only seen at conferences finally gets the experiments it always asked for. One bet carries all the way through. I write it with a signal that could say no, an overnight digest agrees with me, I find out why it agreed, and then five users show me what the digest couldn't. It speaks to the trio, not to me alone, and it gives me a reason to stand in the room. My job becomes deciding which customer, which bet, and which no, not just ordering the backlog.
Slides I'd remember on Monday: **"An opportunity tree on Friday, a feature roadmap on Monday"** (painfully accurate). **"It found what you asked it to look for"**: the digest agreed because my brief asked for evidence *for* my hypothesis. That one stings in a useful way. **"Agents get checked, people don't get watched"** is the sentence I'd take to our employee rep before anyone asks.
Parts I'd tune out of: the citation parade. Torres, Cagan, Bland, Osterwalder, Seiden, Cutler, Fitzpatrick, Nielsen, Ries, Patton, Kniberg, Klein, Deming and Strathern, one per slide, start to feel like a reading list read aloud. I also noticed the trainer quoting his own blog four times. A couple of slides are the same as A's (the Italy/Finland dinner, "Are you sure?"), and for a daily chat user that dinner example is old news.

## Which one I'd go to.

B. It is about the work I'm actually paid for and the work I want a say in. A teaches me to build a personal system around one challenge. B teaches the three of us how to decide better when building stops being the bottleneck, and that is where my standing comes from. It also handles the things my organisation will raise in the first meeting. Customer names stay out of the agent ("the door you don't open"). The working agreement is drafted by the team lead and decided by the team, not pushed down from above. Checking the agent is kept separate from watching the person. And it is honest in a way I trust. It tells teams that don't talk to customers weekly to "start there, before agents", and admits "That sells less of this training". That understatement is why I believe the rest.

What A does better: it actually makes me *do* agentic work. B's handbook is all lectures. I can see that an overnight digest exists, but I can't see from what I read how I'd learn to build and run one. A would get me from "I've seen demos" to "I've run a loop and checked it". A also goes much deeper on trust in the output. It runs a real benchmark of four detectors against a claim pool, builds a judge, and then checks the judge against your own verdicts. B covers that in two slides. If our trio came back from B and couldn't build the digest agent, A would have been the better three days.
