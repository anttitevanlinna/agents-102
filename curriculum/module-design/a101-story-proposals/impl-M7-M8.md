## NEW `curriculum/lectures/where-is-this-all-going.md`

6 minutes, linked from `agents-building-agents.md` after the proposal forum and before Debrief.

### Headers now present

```
# Lecture: Where is this all going?
## You described it; something else built it
## Count the folders the kernel cites
## The parts hold; the model rotates
## Which half of this is obsolete next year?
## Will your organisation learn faster than the model changes underneath it?
## What would change our mind?
## A flywheel, not a graduation
```

### Lines added

The refrain's last turn (braid § 5.A, *Owed, M8*), placed as the opening slide's body:

> You wrote down what an agent should do and you did not build it. Something else read your files
> and handed it back running.
>
> The parts did not change. Context, a goal, tools, checks, a boundary, a loop. Every one of them
> is still a piece you can name and still a piece you decide. What changed is who assembles them.

The absorption callback, braid § 5.D, *Paid off, M8*:

> Everyone in the room had access to every other folder. Nobody was blocked from anything, and it
> took about a minute to set up.
>
> Now open the kernel and count how many folders it actually cites.
>
> That ratio is your rollout, in miniature, with the politics removed.

The frame's third break, braid § 1 Break 3, with the count dropped:

> The list of parts is stable. Context, tools, boundary, checks, loop, interface. That list would
> have been the same list a year ago and it will probably be the same list a year from now.
>
> What the model can do with each part is not stable. It moves without asking you, on somebody
> else's release schedule.
>
> So the assembly is the durable half, and the assembly is what you spent your time on.

E's counter-voice, braid § 5.E:

> Some of what you built here will be obsolete within a year. … The interesting question is which
> half. … Nobody in this room knows yet, and the people running this training do not know either.

The two closing questions, held open:

> The model gets better on its own, for free, whether or not you do anything at all. What your
> company knows about its own work gets better only when somebody writes it down and somebody
> else picks it up. Those two curves are running at once, and only one of them responds to
> anything you do.

> Ask it of the kernel your room just produced. *What would have to show up for us to drop this?*
> If nobody can answer, what you have is a belief with a schedule attached.

And the close, which restates nothing and does not tidy:

> Nothing here closed. The memory is still short. The judge is still yours, and still unchecked in
> the places you have not looked. The agent set your room produced has a first item, not a last
> one. … That is not a graduation. It is the first turn.
