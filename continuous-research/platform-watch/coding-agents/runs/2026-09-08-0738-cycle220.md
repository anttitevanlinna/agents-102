# Learning surfaces and practitioner practice OODA Cycle — 2026-09-08 07:38

**Focus area:** What a learning coding agent may change, and which named practitioners are changing each surface in real work.
**Research mode:** People-first practitioner synthesis plus current repository inspection.

## Findings

### Every now implements capture, historical-session harvest, retrieval, and retirement for a low-authority solution store

**Sources:** https://github.com/EveryInc/compound-engineering-plugin/blob/8df67793b9733d2220fa9a7fc37139931471af62/docs/guides/ce-compound.md; https://github.com/EveryInc/compound-engineering-plugin/blob/8df67793b9733d2220fa9a7fc37139931471af62/docs/guides/ce-compound-refresh.md — [practitioner direct]
**Date:** Current implementation checked 2026-09-08
**Agent level:** Personal / team
**What:** Kieran Klaassen and Dan Shipper's maintained Compound Engineering implementation has become much closer to the proposed Module 9 mechanic. `ce-compound` can inspect current and selected historical Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Pi, and oh-my-pi sessions; capture one verified, durable, non-obvious learning into `docs/solutions/`; validate it against the tree; and make it retrievable by later planning and ideation. A standing instruction can trigger non-interactive capture automatically. `ce-compound-refresh` then gives the store five lifecycle outcomes—Keep, Update, Consolidate, Replace, Delete—while marking ambiguous unattended cases stale or leaving them for human judgment.
**Evidence level:** L2 single-team implementation; no controlled later-task outcome.
**Key claims:** Every permits automatic writes to a structured solution store, but not silent non-interactive edits to `AGENTS.md` or `CLAUDE.md`; machine-enforceable behavior is directed to tests, types, or assertions; ambiguous knowledge maintenance is separated from the safe automatic subset. This is governed knowledge maintenance, not automatic promotion into permissions or organizational policy.

### Accepted human review comments are becoming versioned behavioral rules in one production team

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13091 — [academic/research]
**Date:** 2026-07-13
**Agent level:** Team
**What:** Aditya Aggarwal and Nahid Farhady Ghalaty report a 35-plus-service team turning generalizable, accepted review comments into version-controlled behavioral rules, code standards, anti-patterns, workflow triggers, and a self-review checklist. The accepting engineer decides whether feedback is a recurring class or a one-off; rule changes go through the team's existing pull-request governance.
**Evidence level:** L2 single-team field report.
**Key claims:** Over four weeks and 11 recorded sessions, the store grew from five to 18 behavioral rules plus 15-plus language standards and a 15-item checklist. The authors observed no recurrence across nine tracked error classes and 74 post-rule exposures, but had no A/B control, one primary language, and no long-term saturation result. They explicitly treat automated rule proposal, multi-team governance, and controlled temporal evaluation as future work.

### Other practitioners put different kinds of lessons into different artifacts

**Sources:** https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals-faq/how-many-examples-do-i-need-for-an-eval.html — [practitioner direct]; https://www.martinfowler.com/articles/sensors-for-coding-agents.html — [practitioner direct]
**Dates:** Hamel Husain and Shreya Shankar, modified 2026-09-01; Birgitta Böckeler, 2026-05-27
**Agent level:** Personal / team
**What:** Husain and Shankar turn confirmed human trace judgments into failure taxonomies, labeled cases, and repeatable evaluators; agent suggestions remain proposals that a human accepts or rejects. Böckeler turns objective maintainability expectations into executable sensors—tests, linters, structural checks, mutation tests, and a sidecar history—while leaving semantic design judgment with humans and probabilistic review.
**Evidence level:** L1 maintained practitioner method plus L2 exploratory single-practitioner implementation.
**Key claims:** A lesson about observable failure belongs in an eval case; a mechanically enforceable rule belongs in a sensor; neither belongs only as free-floating prose. Böckeler's history can reveal sensors that never fire or fire too often, but her unsupervised harness integration remained unfinished.

### File-native compounding is spreading, but measured learning remains rare

**Sources:** https://www.productcompass.pm/p/claude-md-snippets — [practitioner direct]; https://lethain.com/everyinc-compound-engineering/ — [practitioner direct for Imprint implementation; practitioner analysis of Every]
**Dates:** Paweł Huryn, 2026-03-31; Will Larson, 2026-04
**Agent level:** Personal / team
**What:** Huryn reports a personal observation → hypothesis → rule setup that produced 24 project rules over a month, plus a decision journal and quality gate. Larson reports implementing the Compound Engineering commands and instructions in Imprint's frontend and backend monorepos in about an hour, standardizing a pattern engineers there had been applying manually.
**Evidence level:** L2 single-practitioner reports.
**Key claims:** These are real file-native practices, not proof of a complete learning system. Huryn supplies no controlled comparison for the claim that the system became measurably better; Larson reports implementation friction and adoption, not later-task quality outcomes.

## Resolved Boundary

| Learning surface | Safe default | Practitioner evidence this round |
|---|---|---|
| Raw trace, outcome, human comment | Capture automatically; immutable provenance | Every; Husain and Shankar |
| Candidate observation, hypothesis, solution note | Agent may write or propose; keep scope and source | Every; Huryn |
| Active rule, skill, workflow trigger | Human accepts; version and review like code | Aggarwal and Ghalaty; Huryn |
| Test, eval case, deterministic sensor | Agent may propose; human owns expected behavior and merge | Husain and Shankar; Böckeler |
| Harness or orchestration code | Normal code-change controls plus comparative evaluation and rollback | No practitioner in this round demonstrates the complete promotion loop |
| Permission, credential, approval authority | Non-learnable; external policy only | No contrary practitioner evidence found |

## What I Looked For But Did Not Find

I found no named practitioner who lets a trace autonomously promote itself all the way into an active organizational rule, an executable verifier, and broader action authority, then proves the combined change on comparable fresh tasks. I also found no public multi-team governance model for composing local rules across a 1,000-person software unit, and no practitioner who treats permissions as a learnable memory surface. The strongest evidence remains one-team and one-person practice, not 10–20-practitioner convergence.

## Orientation

The first Module 9 question is resolved enough to teach as a design law: **learning surfaces form an authority ladder**. Automate evidence capture and candidate generation; make activation harder as an artifact gains force. Knowledge notes may be maintained automatically inside a conservative boundary, active procedural rules and evaluators need human-owned promotion, harness changes need comparative proof and rollback, and permissions do not learn.
