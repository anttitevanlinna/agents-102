## Frame

**A (AE101): 95.** Frame: *agentic engineering is engineering* — a closed control loop, checks from outside the session, evidence that compounds. Three modules that only make sense through it:
- M1 the-machine-you-just-met: "A check from outside the session resets the chain. A failing test does not care how confident the answer sounded."
- M3 the-loop-half-filled: "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller**. ... Cut the feedback signal (a test, a check, a read) and it drifts."
- M5 what-packaging-is: "**A check is a position fix**. At a fix the wedge of possible states collapses to a point."
- Where it breaks: M5 the-gate-is-a-claim, "Sutton's **bitter lesson** ... Today's right procedure, your gates and workflow ... is superseded too." and M5 what-packaging-is, "Whether this field ever settles into a real best practice is an open question."
It sits just under 100 because the break is named as a caution, and the training does not stake out where its own lens stops paying.

**B (APT101): 92.** Frame: *building got cheap, deciding didn't*. The agent takes over the slow step, and the product craft (outcome, bet, signal, insight) is what's left to the trio.
- Day 1 apt101-building-is-cheap: "Building got cheap, deciding didn't ... Every other step is a decision ... None of those got cheaper."
- Day 1 apt101-building-rationed-it: "The post-it was not naive. It was what the craft shrank to when building was expensive."
- Day 2 apt101-why-it-agreed: "A faster feature factory is still a feature factory ... Agents amplify the way a team already works."
- Day 3 apt101-three-jobs-rewritten: "Your insight is the choice it leaves you with ... That is real strategy work, and it does not get cheaper when building does."
- Where it breaks, stated on the page (the 100 element): Day 3 apt101-where-you-go-from-here, "This is where 'building got cheap, deciding didn't' stops being an edge on its own ... What would change this training's mind: agents that start deciding well".
Not higher because several Day 1 beats are agent-literacy units that don't read through the frame. "A chat runs on one part; an agent needs six" and "Sharpen the insights, never the sources" (apt101-it-runs-overnight, apt101-your-material-is-the-moat) would sit just as well in A101.

## Narrative

**A: 94.** An engineer brings their own repo and learns that the agent's account is not the repo. They build checks and rules on short loops. Then, at M4, they send a long task off un-packaged on purpose and it underdelivers ("The un-packaged run was supposed to underdeliver. What came back is data, not blame." — M5 diagnose-and-resend). The turn: the return is read through three lenses, packaging is built against the failure they actually saw, and the same task goes out again ("The diagnose-and-re-send you just ran was exactly this move" — M5 what-packaging-is). M6 diffs the two sessions, cuts a rule, and the narrator shows the training's own making failing the same way ("It still opened with the un-packaged shape." — M6 story-of-module-6). Ending: "There is no last turn." (M6 agents-that-build-agents). The turn is lived, and the training's own failure is on the page.

**B: 90.** A trio arrives with its own product and a feature nobody asked for ("what is the last thing your team built that nobody asked for?" — Day 1 module). They paint a box, write a bet that can lose, and send three agents off overnight, each with a look-for line in their own words and an expectation the run never reads (Day 1 apt101-send-it-off: "write it word for word ... into `team/<my-name>/expect.md`, outside the brief so the run never reads it"). The turn comes on Day 2. Four lines side by side, then the same run with the line turned round, and the headline turns out to be their own question handed back. The exercise asks "Did your line ask for support, a test, or a frame?" (apt101-read-the-digest), and only afterwards does the lecture name it: "The headline came from your question as much as from your customers." (apt101-why-it-agreed). They rebuild checking: retrievers that must bring back an AGAINST finding, a judge that won a benchmark and states its limit, a pre-mortem. On Day 3 five outsiders use the slice, and each person says aloud whether their own Day 1 line held or broke (apt101-five-users). The close reads `team/caught.md` aloud and re-asks the Day 1 question: "is anything in `team/monday.md` the next thing your team builds that nobody asked for?" (Day 3 module). The ending is open on purpose: "The bet is still open, and the next slice is yours to place."
The turn is lived, so this clears 80. It is not the training's own failure: the guide's failures are quoted from essays, not shown inside the training. And the turn still leans on the lecture where a student's look-for line was test-shaped rather than support-shaped ("A line that asked for support got support").

## Room

The exercises now carry the turn. The look-for line (Day 1) feeds the four-line reading and the reversed rerun (Day 2), then `caught.md` and the five users (Day 3).

**Strongest beats**
1. **Day 2 apt101-read-the-digest, Phases 1, 3 and 4.** The expectation was sealed on Day 1 ("outside the brief so the run never reads it"). Now it is quoted beside the headline and the look-for line. Then: "Did your line ask for support, a test, or a frame?" and "with only my `## Look for` line replaced by 'find what in my material argues against:'". The student sees the agreement come from their own question before apt101-why-it-agreed names it.
2. **Day 3 apt101-five-users, Phases 2–4.** "No Claude in the room for this phase." Then "for each moment, say whether the digest said it, could have said it, or could not have," and "each of you reads your own Day 1 hypothesis ... says out loud the line in it the five users touched, and whether it held or broke. If none of your lines broke, say what result would have broken it." Each seat has a move, and the bet learning pays off in the student's own words. (Close runner-up: Day 3 take-it-to-the-team Phase 2, "run the file they received, as written ... The three of you read what came back. Then tell its sender.", which lives "an agent's instructions are not the agent" before the lecture.)

**Weakest beats**
1. **Day 1 apt101-what-goes-in, Phase 3.** The door is written as a policy, and the cost is announced, not felt: "It will cost a source somebody wanted in. That is the door working." No exercise ever shows something going wrong because of a source that got in, or a loss because of one that stayed out. Its Day 2 echo ("listing each source skipped for that reason") is never read aloud.
2. **Day 3 apt101-write-what-good-means, Phase 2.** The loop runs while the trio steps away: "Now step away from the screen ... The judge never moves." The story sits in borrowed prompts (`{{prompt:eval-loop-*}}`) and operating notes ("If the judge file moved, the loop did not hold: `module-6/eval-notes.md` records whether it changed"). Phase 3 recovers it, but the 25-minute middle is machinery. Day 1 apt101-build-your-product-memory is close behind: 45 minutes where the "soft pages" audit is Claude grading itself, and nothing ties forward to the turn except "don't settle it yet".
