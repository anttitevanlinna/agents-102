## Frame

**A · AE101 — one sentence:** agentic engineering is engineering — a closed control loop whose ceiling is the quality of the checks outside the session, and whose rules compound only when evidence earns them.
- M2 · lectures/when-a-plan-is-good: "Push reach past what you can check and you have not delegated more. You are checking less." — the plan read only makes sense as a calibration move inside the frame.
- M3 · lectures/the-loop-half-filled: "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller**... Cut the feedback signal (a test, a check, a read) and it drifts. Signal quality is part of the law: a flaky test is a closed loop that still drifts."
- M5 · lectures/what-packaging-is: "**A check is a position fix**. At a fix the wedge of possible states collapses to a point" — packaging only makes sense through the loop frame.
- Where it breaks, on the page: M5 · lectures/the-gate-is-a-claim: "Green is a claim about the check, not a fact about the work"; "Sutton's **bitter lesson**... Today's right procedure, your gates and workflow... is superseded too." M2 · how-instructions-grow: "Rules have a ceiling."
- Score 95: every module reads through the lens, and the lens turns on its own instrument (the gate) and its own shelf-life.

**B · APT101 — one sentence:** building got cheap, deciding didn't — so the trio's job moves from rationing the build to choosing the outcome, the bet and the no, which is strategy work no analysis does for them.
- M1 · lectures/apt101-building-is-cheap: "Agents make the slow step cheap. The hard steps stay where they were." and M1 · apt101-building-rationed-it: "The post-it was not naive. It was what the craft shrank to when building was expensive."
- M2 · lectures/apt101-why-it-agreed: "When agents analyse wider, deeper and faster than you can, analysis stops being the scarce part." — the digest module only makes sense through the frame (analysis cheap too).
- M3 · lectures/apt101-three-jobs-rewritten: "Your insight is the choice it leaves you with... That is real strategy work, and it does not get cheaper when building does."
- Where it breaks: M3 · lectures/apt101-where-you-go-from-here: "This is where 'building got cheap, deciding didn't' stops being an edge on its own. When rivals build as cheaply, speed is the entry price... What would change this training's mind: agents that start deciding well".
- Score 88: the frame is clean, load-bearing in all three modules and names its own break — but the break is one closing slide rather than a recurring doubt, and a band of M2 slides (double diamond, 1-2-4-All, psychological safety, Klein pre-mortem) read as product-craft catalogue first and frame-cases second.

## Narrative

Asymmetry the orchestrator must read the gap through: AE101's handbook manifest includes exercises, so the student *lives* the turn on the page; APT101's handbook is lectures only, so its turn is narrated to the student.

**A · AE101 in five sentences.** An engineer learns the machine mirrors their stance and that a check outside the session resets the chain (M1). They learn plans are gates and rules compound, until rules hit a ceiling (M2–M3). They send a real task off un-packaged and it comes back short. They diagnose it, build a verifier against their own worst failure and re-send it packaged — then learn the gate itself is a claim. The guide confesses the module itself was made by an agent that opened un-packaged after writing the modules that teach packaging, and hands over: "Your turn."
- Turn, M4 · lectures/test-and-learn: "Session one goes now, un-packaged... Session two goes packaged, after you've read the return."
- M5 · exercises/diagnose-and-resend: "The un-packaged run was supposed to underdeliver. What came back is data, not blame."
- The training's own failure, M6 · lectures/story-of-module-6: "The training teaches this pattern across three modules. The agent I was working with had just finished writing those three modules. It still opened with the un-packaged shape."
- Score 92: the turn is lived and the top-rung version (the training's own failure) is on the page.

**B · APT101 in five sentences.** A product trio learns their craft was always right but rationed by expensive building, and that now building is cheap. They write a bet, a product box and a digest agent that runs overnight. The digest comes back agreeing with their favourite hypothesis — and the turn is that it agreed because their brief asked it to. They write down what good means, test with five users, and learn a no is a result. They leave with a proposal the team decides on Monday and an open question about their own insight.
- Turn, M2 · lectures/apt101-the-digest-is-back: "The overnight digest is back, and its headline is the hunch your team liked most... It is also the moment you are least likely to check".
- M2 · lectures/apt101-why-it-agreed: "It found what you asked it to look for... So the agreement may come from your question, not from your customers."
- Callback, M3 · lectures/apt101-what-good-means: "Hold the overnight digest that agreed with your favourite hypothesis against those lines, and you can see where it agreed on thin evidence."
- Score 76: a real turn with stakes and a two-module callback, but on the handbook surface it is a narrated scenario ("customers stall at the import step"), not the trio's own run, and the turn is never the training's own failure.

## Stance

**A · AE101 — positions it could lose a customer over, defended:**
- "a rule in context is not a rule in the output" — defended by scar, M6 · story-of-module-6: "The rule was loaded into context. Claude used it twice in the third turn."
- Best practice from the model is a forecast — M5 · lectures/what-packaging-is: "today's playbooks are **candidates**"; "**A prediction is not a measurement.**" (mechanism: weights frozen at a cutoff). Against its own commercial interest: the training is itself a published playbook, and "nothing published can run that local test for you."
- Self-reports are hypotheses — M1 · lectures/the-machine-you-just-met: "Agreeable answers won the second round... The report is a hypothesis to check, not ground truth." (mechanism: preference tuning).
- "Don't make general what you don't practice yourself." M3 · lectures/skills-from-the-frontier, defended by the two-curated/one-authored split.
- Score 92.

**B · APT101 — positions it could lose a customer over, defended:**
- Fluency is the shape of the tech, not a bug — M2 · lectures/apt101-fluent-is-not-true: "This isn't a bug that gets patched in the next release. It's the shape of the technology. Later models will fabricate less; they won't stop." (mechanism: next likely word).
- You cannot hand over an agent — M3 · lectures/apt101-from-us-to-the-team: "An agent's instructions are not the agent... a proposal to the wider team cannot promise 'everyone gets our agent'." and "Access is easy; absorption is scarce... Many companies have rolled out access far ahead of trust." (against vendor rollout narratives).
- Leave sources out even when wanted — M1 · lectures/apt101-it-runs-overnight: "It will cost you a source somebody wanted in. Leaving it out is still the right call".
- Speed is no edge — M3 · apt101-where-you-go-from-here: "Cheap building helps your rivals too... speed is the entry price" — partly against the training's own pitch (agents make you faster).
- Faster factory — M2 · apt101-why-it-agreed: "Agents amplify the way a team already works. They do not transform it."
- Score 79: real positions with mechanisms and one against its own selling point, but most edges are borrowed from a named authority and the training's own scar defends almost none of them (the installer story is the one exception).

## The pair

**A:** doubts its own tools (the gate is a claim; its own fork prompt misleads; its own rules leaked in M6) AND holds ground (rule in context ≠ rule in output; playbooks are candidates). Reads as someone who has been there.
**B:** holds ground (fluent is not true; instructions are not the agent; door you don't open) and doubts the student's tools (the digest, the check, the criteria-as-target), and once doubts its own frame (where-the-frame-breaks) — but rarely doubts its own method or shows itself failing at agent-era product work. Reads closer to someone who has been there than to a pitch, held up by borrowed authority more than by its own scars.
