## Frame

**A — AE101.** Frame: agentic engineering is engineering — a feedback-control loop you steer with checks, and whatever you learn goes to disk so it compounds. Three modules that only make sense through it:
- M2 · lectures/when-a-plan-is-good: "Push reach past what you can check and you have not delegated more. You are checking less."
- M3 · lectures/the-loop-half-filled: "The agent loop is a **closed-loop controller**. ... Cut the feedback signal (a test, a check, a read) and it drifts."
- M5 · lectures/what-packaging-is: "**A check is a position fix**. At a fix the wedge of possible states collapses to a point".
- Where it breaks: M5 · lectures/the-gate-is-a-claim: "Sutton's **bitter lesson**: built-in human knowledge wins today and loses to the next model. Today's right procedure ... is superseded too." and M4 pre-read: "Mollick does not call the winner. Neither does this training."
Score **93**: every module reads through it, and the training names where it breaks. That break is a doubt about how long the procedure lasts, not about the lens itself, which is why it stops short of 100.

**B — APT101.** Frame: building got cheap and deciding didn't, so a product trio's work is the outcome, the bet and the signal, and agents speed up whichever way the team already works.
- Day 1 · lectures/apt101-building-is-cheap: "For years the slice was the slow step. Now an agent can build it. Every other step is a decision".
- Day 2 · lectures/apt101-why-it-agreed: "Agents amplify the way a team already works. They do not transform it." / "A faster feature factory is still a feature factory".
- Day 3 · lectures/apt101-slice-by-learning: "Specifying what to build is now the harder half".
- Where it breaks, on the page: Day 3 · lectures/apt101-where-you-go-from-here: "This is where 'building got cheap, deciding didn't' stops being an edge on its own." + "What would change this training's mind: agents that start deciding well".
Score **91**. The frame is load-bearing and its break is written down, which is close to the 100 anchor. It loses points mid-Day 2: the hallucination bakeoff and "Safe to say it's wrong" read through a verification lens imported from AE101 more than through "deciding". The frame holds those beats but does not produce them.

## Narrative

**A.** In five sentences: an engineer runs short loops in which the agent's account is partly wrong and the checks keep them honest (M1–M3). Then they send a long task off unpackaged and it comes back drifted, plausible and wrong (M4). They read the return through three lenses and package the same task against the failure they saw, and their own gate turns out to be a claim as well (M5). The author then shows his own session, the one that wrote the module, failing in every one of those ways (M6). The turn is the unpackaged send-off coming back.
- M4 · exercises/set-the-markers-send-it-off: "send it off plainly and you find out what you can't steer yet."
- M5 · exercises/diagnose-and-resend: "The un-packaged run was supposed to underdeliver. What came back is data, not blame."
- M6 · lectures/story-of-module-6: "Turn one. Claude opened the session with a plan. ... The agent I was working with had just finished writing those three modules. It still opened with the un-packaged shape."
Score **94**. The student lives the turn, and the training's own making fails on the page.

**B.** In five sentences: a trio arrives with the feature nobody asked for and writes a bet that can lose. Each person briefs an overnight digest in their own words (Day 1). The digest comes back agreeing with their favourite hypothesis, because their own look-for line asked it to (Day 2). They build a judge, choose a bet with evidence behind it and tell tonight's run to look for what argues against it (Day 2). Five outside users then do things no digest could have said, and each person reads aloud the line of their own Day 1 hypothesis that was proved wrong, before the close asks the opening question again of their Monday plan (Day 3).
- The turn, lived: Day 2 · exercises/apt101-read-the-digest: "Does the headline say what your bet says? / Which sentence of yours asked for it?"
- Named after: Day 2 · lectures/apt101-why-it-agreed: "The agreement came from your question, not from your customers."
- Second turn: Day 3 · exercises/apt101-five-users: "says out loud the line in it the five users proved wrong. Your own line, not someone else's."
- Ring close: Day 3 close: "is anything in `team/monday.md` the next thing your team builds that nobody asked for?"
Score **87**. The student lives the turn twice, and the five-users payoff answers the Day 1 plant. Two things hold it back. The turn is the student's failure, not the training's. And it depends on what happens in the room: "Why it agreed" asserts "Your look-for line asked the agent to find what supports your hypothesis". A trio that wrote a neutral line on Day 1 gets told this turn instead of living it.

## Stance

**A.** Positions it could lose a customer over, each defended:
- "A rule in context is not a rule in the output." (M6 · story-of-module-6), defended by a scar: four subagents leaked the banned word.
- "Ask for best practice and that is what answers: a well-read average ... **A prediction is not a measurement.**" (M5 · what-packaging-is), defended by a mechanism (weights frozen at a cutoff; missing local evidence).
- "Don't make general what you don't practice yourself." (M3 · skills-from-the-frontier), defended by the two-curated-one-authored split.
- "The cure is not better rules; it is where the rules live." (M2 · how-instructions-grow), defended by the attention-budget mechanism.
- Against its own commercial interest: "This training stops short of the full system ... How those parts run is for your team to automate" (M2), a weak form.
Score **92**.

**B.** Counting only positions the training asserts itself (Cutler, Torres and Bland are borrowed authority):
- Against its own commercial interest: Day 2 · lectures/apt101-go-back-to-your-customers: "a team that does not talk with its customers every week should start there, before agents. ... That sells less of this training. It is still where to start." Defended by a mechanism: "With no fresh evidence from customers, that something is the team's guesses. Agents build them in days".
- "Agents amplify the way a team already works. They do not transform it." (Day 2 · apt101-why-it-agreed), defended by a scar the student just earned (the agreeing digest).
- "An agent's instructions are not the agent" (Day 3 · apt101-from-us-to-the-team), against the "share the agent" pitch, defended by a mechanism: "The work lives in the memory you built up ... in every correction you made".
- "Access is easy; absorption is scarce" (Day 3). Asserted, thinly argued.
- "This isn't a bug that gets patched in the next release. ... Later models will fabricate less; they won't stop." (Day 2 · apt101-fluent-is-not-true). Held ground, though phrased as a prediction.
Score **90**. It includes a position held against its own commercial interest, defended with a mechanism. It loses points because the most-repeated positions are borrowed from the cited canon.
