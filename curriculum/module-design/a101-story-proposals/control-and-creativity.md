## 1 · Frame

**Every piece you add to an agent does one of two jobs: it sets a floor under what the work cannot
fall below, or it raises the ceiling on what the work can reach, and leading an agent system is
knowing which of the two you are doing at any moment.**

This is the floor/ceiling pair stated as a mechanism rather than as a claim about leadership. It
does not replace the frame the judges already found on the page (the *full agent picture* refrain,
one anatomical piece per module). It says what the anatomy is **for**. Context, shelf life, other
agents, boundary, checks, loop, interface: each piece is an instrument, and each instrument has a
job. The refrain assembles the machine; the frame says which half of the machine each piece serves.

### The eight modules as cases

- **M1 Getting Going.** Pure ceiling. Nothing you add makes the output safer and the output gets
  better anyway, because the only floor in the room is a person: *"You are the world's best
  evaluator of your own profile."* The guardrail is a ceiling instrument that the module correctly
  calls a control (*"The guardrail IS the control"*), which is where the frame's tension starts.
- **M2 Building Agent Systems.** The ceiling gets shelf life. The memory carries reach across a
  closed session, and the first floor that survives a closed session arrives at the same beat: the
  rules file written at the Debrief.
- **M3 Multi-Agent Systems.** The ceiling instrument at full volume, with no floor built yet.
  Three dialects, three stances, the Rory seat. Also its own failure: *"the synthesizer averages
  everything into beige."* You end the module holding the highest-reaching output of the training
  and nothing that can tell you which half of it is true.
- **M4 Security.** The first pure floor, and the first one that costs. Every mitigation is a
  subtraction: *"Every door you don't open is a residual risk you don't have to name."* The doors
  you closed were also sources the system will not read.
- **M5 Grounded Output.** The first floor you can measure, built by the ceiling's own method. Four
  candidates, one scoreboard, keep the winner. Variety generated on purpose so that selection has
  something to select.
- **M6 Evaluations.** The two instruments named side by side in the training's own words:
  *"Groundedness protects the floor. Steering raises the ceiling."* Then the loop shows they are
  stages of one mechanism, not two objects on a shelf.
- **M7 From Personal to Team.** Both instruments, handed to someone else, and neither transfers.
  *"Access is easy; absorption is scarce."* What a teammate can absorb is their floor, and you did
  not build it.
- **M8 Agents Building Agents.** The room generates the variety and one human sets the floor:
  *"The agents produced the options. The human picks."* The ceiling moves on its own; the floor
  still has a name on it.

### Where the frame breaks

Named on the page at M4, where the student can feel it, and again at M6 where it resolves into
something stranger than the pair.

Drafted, as the training would say it to a student (M4, after the mitigation lands):

> The frame has been clean so far. Some things you build raise what the system can reach. Some
> things you build stop it falling. You just did the second kind, and it cost you the first kind.
> The door you closed was a source. The tool you took away was a capability. The split you made
> means two agents now know half of what one agent knew.
>
> There is no setting where the system is both as safe as you can make it and as good as it can
> get. Scope it down far enough and it is perfectly safe and useless. Open it all the way and it
> is brilliant on Tuesday and a breach on Thursday. Nobody hands you the number. You pick it, you
> write down what is left, and you sign for it.

Second break, at M6, deliberately not resolved into comfort:

> Two instruments is the useful picture and it is not the last one. Watch what the loop actually
> did. The generator made more than you needed. The judge threw most of it away. The tactic file
> kept what survived. Generate, select, keep. That is one mechanism with three stages, and the
> floor is a stage inside it, not a separate tool on a shelf. You will outgrow the pair. Keep it
> until you do.

Break test, per the 100 anchor: the frame states the condition under which it stops being true
(the two instruments are the same instrument at a different zoom) and states the cost it cannot
remove (floor subtracts from ceiling, always).

## 2 · Narrative

The mood arc is fixed and the plot below lives inside it: joy, compounding, unease, deeper unease,
rescue, leverage, generosity, awe with forward hunger. M3 and M4 stay unresolved until M5. M8 ends
open.

**Five sentences.**

1. You arrive able to talk about agents and unable to tell whether one is doing anything, and the
   first thing the training has you build is a page about yourself, because you are the only
   evaluator in the room the model cannot flatter into agreement.
2. It works, so you give it shelf life, then a pulse, then six agents, and every single move you
   make raises what the system can reach.
3. Then the training hands you an answer built by its own method and tells you not to fix it:
   *"the answer sits at that uneasy distance where you'd stake your reputation on some of it and
   not all of it, and you can't yet say which is which."*
4. Module 4 makes it worse by naming what that system can already touch, and Module 5 takes the
   same untrusted briefing the training told you to make and measures it, four ways, on a
   scoreboard.
5. By Module 6 the check runs while you are not in the room and the question has changed from
   whether you trust the agent to whether you would sign what it sends, and Module 8 leaves you
   with twenty agents, one kernel, and one decision that did not move up a level with you.

**The turn, quoted.** Existing line, `exercises/three-minds-one-synthesis.md`, § Close:

> *"You can't tell yet. Three retrievers read plainly, three stances pushed, a framework held the
> synthesis together, and still, the answer sits at that uneasy distance where you'd stake your
> reputation on some of it and not all of it, and you can't yet say which is which. That feeling
> is correct. Hold the doubt."*

**Why this reaches the top narrative anchor.** The anchor asks for a turn that is the training's
own failure. It already is one, and the training has never said so. The unverifiable briefing at
M3 was not produced by the student going off-piste. It was produced by the method the training
just taught, at the module that celebrates the method, using the memory the training built in M2.
And M5's benchmark runs on that exact artifact: *"Your target is the ungrounded briefing from
Module 3."* The training breaks its own output on purpose and then measures it. One sentence at
the M3 close converts a good turn into the top rung. Drafted:

> Nothing went wrong here. You ran the move exactly as taught, on the memory you built, and the
> result is an answer you cannot check. That is not a gap in the exercise. That is what the move
> produces on its own, and it is why there are five more modules.

**Mood safety.** That sentence deepens unease, does not resolve it, and hands nothing to fix. It
is compatible with M3's contract (*"Hold the doubt. Name it to yourself. Don't fix it here."*).

## 3 · Point of view

### Who is telling this

Someone who built an agent system that worked, then tried to make an organisation use it, and was
wrong about which half was the hard half. Not an engineer narrating an inner loop. A person who
has already paid the absorption bill.

That narrator has three things on the page today and one thing missing. They have a voice in
asides (*"Damn, this is complex stuff"*), an admission of interest (*"This is a self-audit of a
live agent round. Convenient, not neutral"*), and a refusal to close a doubt (*"Hold the doubt"*).
They have no incident. The quartet gap (80 vs 95) is exactly that: A101's narrator has admitted
uncertainty and never a scar.

### The scar slot

**Not invented here.** What follows is the slot, not the story.

**Module:** M7, From Personal to Team. Placed in the new `Access is not absorption` lecture, after
all three exercises have run and before the Debrief.

**Why M7 and not M3 or M4.** Three reasons, in order of weight.

1. **Mood.** M3 and M4 must stay unresolved, and a trainer's scar is a rescue by construction:
   an incident told from the far side of it says someone survived. M5 is the rescue and does not
   need help. M7's mood is generosity, and generosity has an underside that costs the arc nothing
   to name.
2. **It pays off the weakest learning.** *Access is not absorption* is the step-0 finding's
   one-module headline, scored 30 to 60 on depth, and the reason is that it has no law and no
   scar under it. A position defended by an incident is worth more here than anywhere else in the
   corpus.
3. **Audience.** The leader's scar is organisational. An incident about a rollout nobody took up
   belongs to this buyer. An incident about an agent that leaked belongs to M4 and reads closer to
   the engineer's story.

**Shape of the incident.** A thing the narrator built, that worked, that was given to a named
number of real people who had every reason to want it, and that was not used. The narrator has to
have been confident about the wrong variable. The useful shape is: *I was sure the problem was
quality, so I made it better, and better was not the problem.*

**What it must show, non-negotiable.**

- A decision the narrator made and would now make differently. Not a thing that happened to them.
- One number that is checkable in their own memory: how many people, how many weeks, how many used
  it.
- The moment they found out, and how (someone said it out loud, or a log was empty).
- What they thought the cause was, and what it was.
- No recovery arc. The lecture is not about how they fixed it.

**What it must not do.** It must not end on a lesson sentence. The lecture's own prose carries the
law; the incident carries the credibility. If the incident ends by stating *access is not
absorption*, the naming beat gets spent twice and the scar becomes a parable.

**Length.** 12 to 18 lines, first person, its own `##` slide. Shorter than `story-of-module-6`
(that one is a whole lecture; this is one beat inside one). Long enough to carry a number and a
date and a wrong belief.

### The surrounding beat, with the placeholder

Drafted student-facing text. The placeholder is the only part Antti writes.

> ## You cannot share an agent
>
> Four shapes are on the list. Share the context. Share a skill. Share the output. Share an
> interface. *Share the whole agent* is not on the list, and it is worth being plain about why.
>
> The agent file is a page of instructions. It is not where the work lives. The work lives in the
> memory you curated over six modules, in the corrections you made when the retro was kind to you,
> in the judgement you applied every time you pushed back on a summary that rounded a corner.
> Hand someone the agent file and you have handed them the shell of a thing whose contents are
> still in your head and on your disk. They will run it, get a generic answer, and quietly stop.
>
> ## [PLACEHOLDER: Antti's own incident]
>
> *Slot per the spec above. First person. One artifact he built, the number of people who were
> given it, how long, how he found out it was unused, what he was sure the cause was, and what it
> turned out to be. No fix, no lesson line at the end. 12 to 18 lines.*
>
> ## Access is easy. Absorption is scarce.
>
> People can only take up what they already half know. That is not a statement about willingness.
> It is the plain shape of how anyone absorbs anything: you recognise the new thing by what it
> resembles in the work you already do, and if it resembles nothing, you have nowhere to put it.
>
> You felt this an hour ago. Your technical plan filled quickly. Your people plan stalled on
> names. That was not the exercise being hard. That was the exercise being accurate.

### Register, in three existing A101 lines

The narrator's voice is already on the page. Three specimens, each doing a different job.

1. **The flat admission of cost.** `lectures/practice-of-risk.md`: *"Damn, this is complex stuff.
   It will still be complex when the next agent gets built. The discipline is what carries."*
   Names the difficulty without offering relief. This is the register the scar sits in.
2. **The order not to resolve.** `exercises/three-minds-one-synthesis.md`: *"Hold the doubt. Name
   it to yourself. Don't fix it here."* Three short imperatives, no comfort. This is the register
   every counter-voice beat should use.
3. **The declared interest.** `trainings/agents-101/agents-building-agents.md`: *"This is a
   self-audit of a live agent round. Convenient, not neutral."* The narrator flagging their own
   position before the reader has to. This is the register the against-interest stance line uses.

A fourth, if a fourth is wanted for sharpness rather than for the scar:
`lectures/when-to-split-an-agent.md`: *"If you don't need three different views, please, for the
sake of your sanity, write the one prompt."*

## 4 · Stance

Positions the training holds that could cost it a customer, each with where it appears today and
the mechanism or scar that defends it. Drafted defences are one or two sentences, marked.

**S1. "Share the whole agent" is a vendor pitch.**
Appears: `personal-to-team.md` § Pick the sharing shape, and `exercises/share-your-work.md`.
Today it is asserted, alone among the stance lines. Defence drafted above in § 3 (the agent file
is the shell; the memory, the corrections and the judgement are the contents). Mechanism, not
opinion, and it is checkable by the student: hand the agent file to a neighbour in the room and
watch the answer come back generic.

**S2. Certainty is a fantasy you inherited.**
Appears: `lectures/practice-of-risk.md`. Already defended by mechanism, three properties named:
non-determinism, the instruction set as attack surface, emergent capability. This is the corpus's
best-argued position and needs nothing.

**S3. Start with don't.**
Appears: `lectures/when-to-split-an-agent.md`. Defended by coordination cost plus the bluff test
(*"can I write one prompt that produces the same quality of output?"*). The stance costs the
training a customer who came to buy multi-agent and is told at the multi-agent module not to do it.
Needs nothing.

**S4. A yardstick you rewrite is not a yardstick.**
Appears: `evaluations.md` § Key Concepts, `lectures/evals-as-steering.md`, and the exercise's
integrity check. Defended by a mechanism on disk: the exercise proves it by file comparison and
fails the run if the judge moved. Strongest defence in the corpus because the student watches it
hold.

**S5. Reading about this is not the same category of activity as doing it.**
Appears: `lectures/what-just-happened.md`: *"Reading a McKinsey report on the agentic enterprise is
not the same category of activity as what you just did."* Defended by the felt-model argument in
the same lecture. Loses the customer who wanted a briefing. Worth keeping verbatim.

**S6. The gate is a claim too.** Not on any A101 surface today; the one control position A101
lacks outright. Wanted at M6 because L2 needs a fifth beat and because the training currently
turns scrutiny on every component except the one it built last. Drafted:

> Green means what the judge can see. Nothing else. A briefing passes for three reasons that look
> identical from where you sit: the judge is calibrated, the judge is miscalibrated, or the
> generator learned what the judge rewards and stopped there. You spent Module 6 watching a
> generator get better at passing a fixed test. That is the mechanism working and it is also the
> mechanism you would use to game it.

**S7. Against the training's own commercial interest.** The rung nothing in A101 reaches today.
Home: M7, immediately after the absorption beat, in the narrator's declared-interest register.
Drafted:

> Anyone selling you an agent rollout is selling you access, and this training is one of them.
> Access is the cheap half and it is the half that shows up in a plan: licences, a folder, a
> kickoff, a number of seats. Absorption is the expensive half and it does not scale with the
> size of the engagement. If your organisation cannot absorb this yet, the correct move is fewer
> people doing it properly for longer, which is a smaller sale than the one you were about to be
> offered. Ask whoever is selling it what they would have to give up to tell you that.

Register check owed at implementation: this must read as a claim about the buyer's own rollout
decision, not as positioning for Bosser. `check_writing §13` binds. The test: cut the words *this
training is one of them* and the paragraph must still be a mechanism. It is. The clause is what
makes it against-interest rather than a competitor swipe, so it stays, and it is the sentence a
card should be written for.

**Alternative S7, if the above reads as a pitch in the room.** Placed at M8, replacing the
five-step sequence in § Next:

> You do not leave here with a deployed agent. We could have built you one in an afternoon and you
> would have gone home with a working system and no way to tell whether it was working. That is
> the trade the eight modules make, and it is the one worth naming out loud, because the other
> version is easier to sell.

**The pair.** Stance plus self-doubt, per the rubric's reporting requirement. A101 already reads as
someone who has been there on two of three judges. The additions above keep the balance: S6 and S7
are both doubt-shaped, and S1's new mechanism is ground-holding, so the ratio does not tip into
hedging.

## 5 · The learning-set as consequence

Five learnings, braided. Five rather than six on purpose: the goal spec's prior says buy
forward-looking and pay in applicable or in breadth, never in depth. Two of the six step-0
learnings stop being learnings under this frame and become beats inside others (*context makes it
yours* folds into L1 and L3; *systems compound* folds into L5).

### L1 · Floor and ceiling are different jobs

**Question carried:** *Am I setting a floor or raising a ceiling right now, and what did it cost
the other one?*

- **Planted, M1.** `exercises/personal-site-with-guardrails.md`: *"The guardrail IS the control:
  average output becomes great not because you prompted better, but because the context became
  specific enough to steer it."* Steering named as control at the beat where there is no check in
  the room. The plant is the confusion, deliberately.
- **Complicated, M3.** `multi-agent-systems.md` § Key Concepts: *"the synthesizer averages
  everything into beige."* You built four instruments, all of them ceiling, and the output is
  unverifiable. **New slide wanted:** *Nothing here checks any of it.* Deepens unease, plants the
  absence.
- **Complicated, M4.** `lectures/practice-of-risk.md`: *"Every door you don't open is a residual
  risk you don't have to name, mitigate, monitor, re-test, or apologise for."* **New line wanted:**
  the break, drafted in § 1. The door you closed was a source.
- **Complicated, M5.** `exercises/hallucination-bakeoff.md`: four detectors, one scorer, keep the
  winner. The floor built by the ceiling's own method.
- **Paid off, M6.** `lectures/evals-as-steering.md` and `lectures/new-human-role-in-the-loop.md`,
  both carrying: *"Groundedness protects the floor. Steering raises the ceiling. Do not collapse
  them."* Named as the spine at the beat where the student has built one of each.
- **Governor:** *Which one is this, and what did it cost the other?*
- **Counter-voice:** the frame's own break at M6 (one mechanism, three stages). The pair is a
  teaching device the student is told they will outgrow.
- **Future:** a question, not a prediction. *A better model raises your ceiling for free. Which of
  your two instruments does it build for you, and which one stays yours to build?* Seeded at M2
  with *The model will change under this; the memory won't*, echoed here.

### L2 · Confident is not verified

**Question carried:** *What would I have to touch to know this is true?*

- **Planted, M1.** `getting-going.md` § Push back: *"The LLM is generous to a rules file it just
  helped write."* And the cold-critic beat: *"Asked the most-uniquely-you line first, the LLM warms
  into appreciation."* **New closer slide wanted:** *Why the retro was kind to you* — agreeable
  answers won the round the model was tuned on, and the self-report is the same kind of output.
- **Complicated, M3.** `multi-agent-systems.md` § Debrief: *"Diplomatic is the failure mode at a
  handoff seam. The place where things actually go wrong is exactly where the model rounds the
  corner."* Same tendency, now at the seam between agents.
- **Complicated, M4/M5 pre-read.** `lectures/module-5-prework.md`: *"Asking the same model 'are you
  sure?' is not a check on the world; it is another fluent answer."* Verification has to leave the
  loop.
- **Complicated, M5.** `lectures/grounded.md`: mostly right, ten times over, is mostly wrong. The
  single number a CTO takes home.
- **Paid off, M6.** `lectures/when-the-score-stops-moving.md`: *"A flat score is real information,
  but only about the frame the judge can see."* Plus **S6, new**: the gate is a claim too. The
  scrutiny turns on the check the student built themselves.
- **Governor:** *"I can't tell" is a real answer. What would close it?* Already on the page at M4
  and already re-firing at M5; it earns the 100 rung if the M6 gate beat reuses it unprompted.
- **Counter-voice:** S6. The training doubts the instrument it just sold hardest.
- **Future:** already stated as a mechanism at M5 (*"Later models will fabricate less; they won't
  stop"*) and never asked as a question. **New line wanted, M6:** *If the model stops fabricating,
  what is your judge for?*

### L3 · Variety is the ceiling, and something has to select

**The orphaned arc, named.** Every beat exists; no law does.

**Question carried:** *Who disagrees here, and who picks?*

- **Planted, M1.** `exercises/personal-site-with-guardrails.md` Phase 4: *"invert the easy thing"*,
  two frameworks layered, the move named three times (*name the framework, tune it, ask Claude to
  run it*). Variety generated on purpose, selected by the one person who can.
- **Complicated, M3.** Three dialects, three stances, the Rory seat; *"Frameworks are the
  synthesizer's spine. Without one, it tends to summarise. With a framework, it picks."* First time
  generation and selection are separate jobs on the page.
- **Complicated, M3, the same beat.** `lectures/when-to-split-an-agent.md`: *"More agents is not
  more rigour."* The counter-voice arrives inside the module that sells the move.
- **Complicated, M4.** The mitigations cut variety on purpose. Scope, split, filter: three of the
  five are subtraction. The ceiling instrument, used in reverse.
- **Complicated, M5.** **New line wanted:** *You have done this before. Three stances, one
  framework. Four detectors, one scoreboard. Make more than you need, then choose on evidence
  rather than on which one sounded best.*
- **Paid off, M6.** **New slide wanted:** *Variety in, selection out, memory keeps.* The loop runs
  the same shape a third time and this time without the student in the room.
- **Paid off, M8.** `exercises/joint-double-diamond.md`: *"The agents produced the options. The
  human picks."* Twenty agents of variety, one human selecting.
- **Governor:** *Who disagrees, and who picks?* Fires in a meeting about agents the leader will
  never run, which is the theory plan's test for a leader's governor.
- **Counter-voice:** *start with don't*, already on the page and already argued.
- **Future:** *When the model can generate a hundred good options for the price of one, what in
  your company decides between them?* This is the leader's version of the open question and it is
  organisational, not technical.

### L4 · The human moves up a level, and that is where the watching stops

**Question carried:** *When did I last read one of these myself?*

- **Planted, M1.** You are inside every loop and you are the only evaluator. Not stated; enacted.
- **Complicated, M2.** `exercises/personal-agent-homework.md`: *"Don't retune for the first three
  days. Watch what it produces as-is."* The first time the student is told to stand back.
- **Complicated, M5.** `output-quality.md` § Agent Actions: *"Propose, double-check, apply"* and
  *"The question is which rung this action has earned."* The human placed deliberately rather than
  by default.
- **Paid off, M6.** `lectures/new-human-role-in-the-loop.md`: *"The destination is not 'the human
  disappears.' The better story is stranger: the human moves one level up."* Plus *"Would you let
  it send the mail?"*
- **Counter-voice, missing today, and the one gap that most needs filling.** Step 0 scored this
  learning 20 to 40 on self-challenging, the quietest counter-voice in the corpus. **Drafted, M6,
  immediately after the level-up beat:**

  > Here is the part that does not resolve. Every clean week is real evidence the loop is working
  > and real erosion of your ability to tell when it stops. Those are the same weeks. The person
  > who designs the loop and never runs one loses the eye that made them worth putting in charge
  > of it, and they lose it slowly enough that nothing announces it.
  >
  > There is no instrument for this. There is a question, and it has a date on it: when did you
  > last read one of these yourself, start to finish, without the agent summarising it first?

- **Complicated again, M8.** `joint-double-diamond.md`: the sponsor picks which assumptions the
  company commits to. Authority moved up. Accountability did not move at all.
- **Governor:** *When did you last read one of these yourself?*
- **Future:** *The level you moved up to is a level someone can move up from. What is the highest
  level at which a person still has to be accountable for what the system did?*

### L5 · Access is not absorption

**Question carried:** *Will this organisation learn faster than the model changes underneath it?*

- **Planted, M2.** `lectures/compounding.md` already argues that the system gets better by being
  edited. **New slide wanted:** *What you haven't written down, it can't use.* The memory is the
  student's own absorptive capacity, made literal and visible, three modules before the word for
  it arrives. Most of what a leader knows about their company is not written down anywhere, and
  that is the whole of why M7 is hard.
- **Complicated, M4.** `security.md` § Build More Skills: *"Give a teammate the skill, install it
  in their runtime, and they can call the same method without reconstructing your prompt history.
  That does not make it a deployed service or solve ownership."* The first time sharing is named
  and immediately limited.
- **Complicated, M7.** *"Access is easy; absorption is scarce. The technical plan fills quickly;
  the people plan stalls on names."* Plus Martin's replacement test, already attributed. Plus the
  scar (§ 3) and S7.
- **Paid off, M8.** The callback all three step-0 judges asked for. **Drafted:** *Twenty people got
  access to the shared folder in about a minute. Count how many of the twenty changed their mind
  because of something in someone else's file. That number is your absorption rate, and it is the
  only number from today that transfers.*
- **Paid off, M8, close.** **New:** *Will your organisation learn faster than the model changes
  underneath it?* Not answered. Seeded at M2 (*the model will change under this; the memory
  won't*), echoed at M6 (Mollick's question, already on the page and currently dropped), asked at
  M8.
- **Governor:** *Who absorbs this, and what do they already know?*
- **Counter-voice:** S7, and the training's own unanswered question. `evals-as-steering.md` asks
  whether the bitter lesson applies inside a company and does not claim to know. **New line
  wanted, M8:** *What would change our mind about this?* The forward-looking top rung, which no
  A101 learning reaches today.
- **Future:** the entire thread, held open. A question about the organisation, carried out of the
  room.

### The braid

L1 is the spine and L2 and L3 are its two columns: L2 is what a floor is made of, L3 is what a
ceiling is made of. They fuse at M6 where the loop shows them to be one mechanism.

L4 is what happens to the person who owns both instruments and stops handling either, and it
fuses with L2 at *would you let it send the mail?*, where trust and vigilance are the same
question asked twice.

L5 reframes L1 one zoom out and is the reason the frame is about leadership rather than about
tools: access is a ceiling move, absorption is a floor, and the leader who ran seven modules has
been building only ceilings for other people. That is the sentence M7 exists to deliver, and the
scar is what makes it land as a finding instead of an aphorism.

Carry share sits with L1 by design. L5 carries the future thread almost alone, which is the same
structural bet AE101 made with its two frontiers, with one difference: L5 also has a governor and
a scar, so it should not score 80 on forward-looking and the floor on everything else.

## 8 · Cost

**What this story gives up, and where.**

**Applicability, at two beats.** M8 currently closes on a flywheel the student can feel and a
concrete next move. This story closes it on two questions with no move under them
(*will your organisation learn faster*, *what would change our mind*). M6 loses a slot to a
counter-voice that hands over no instrument (*there is no instrument for this; there is a
question*). Both are the forward-looking purchase the goal spec's prior asks for, paid where the
prior says to pay it.

**Mood, at M7.** S7 puts a line in a generosity module that says the thing the student may be
about to buy is the cheap half. The room will have someone in it who is mid-rollout. That is the
cost of the against-interest rung and there is no way to buy the rung without paying it. The
mitigation is placement: after the scar, in the declared-interest register, so it reads as the
narrator naming their own position rather than as a swipe.

**Breadth, twice.** Five learnings instead of six. *Context makes it yours* stops being its own
learning and becomes L1's plant and L3's first beat; *systems compound* becomes L5's plant. Both
still happen on the page; neither gets named as a thing the student carries. A judge doing a cold
read may name six and score the sixth thin. That is the trade and it is the right one under the
depth anchors.

**Minutes.** The cuts in § 7 fund the additions. Net, this story asks M4 and M6 for roughly one
slide each beyond what the cuts return, and gives M5 back a lecture's worth.

**Which factor I expect to move down.** **Applicable in practice**, from 83 toward the high
seventies, concentrated at M6 and M8. The governors survive and re-fire (*which rung*, *what would
close it*, *who absorbs this*, *when did you last read one*), so the drop should be a few points,
not a collapse. **Earned, not announced** is the second risk and it is structural rather than
mine: every module still opens on a `## Big Idea`, and if M1's *Context is King* does not move to
after Phase 1, the naming beats added here arrive after a thesis the module already announced,
and the factor stays where step 0 found it.

**What I expect to move up.** Forward-looking, from the floor, because the thread is now seeded at
M2, echoed at M6 twice and asked at M8 as a question about the organisation. Self-challenging,
because S6 and L4's counter-voice are the two places the training currently goes quiet. Frame,
because the pair is stated as a mechanism and its break is on the page. Point of view, but only if
the scar slot gets filled: nothing else in this proposal moves that number.

**The risk I cannot price.** The frame's break at M4 asks a leader to accept, in the module
designed to deepen unease, that safety and usefulness trade against each other and nobody supplies
the number. Done well, it is the sentence they repeat to their board. Done flat, it reads as a
consultant declining to answer.
