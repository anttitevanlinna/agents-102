## Frame

**Stated in one sentence:** every module is the same move — shape the context that surrounds the agent — applied at a widening scope: prompt (M1) → memory (M2) → distributed team (M3) → boundary on what context may be read/changed (M4) → evidence-grounding of the context's output (M5) → a fixed judge that holds the context's standard still while the generator improves (M6) → handing the context to someone else (M7) → the agent generating its own context and its own agents (M8).

Three modules that only make sense through it:
- compounding.md (M2): "generic AI becomes your AI when you shape the context that surrounds it. Guardrails did this at the prompt level in Module 1. A memory does it at the knowledge level now. The mechanism is the same; only the shelf life changed." — states the frame almost verbatim as the module's own thesis.
- grounded.md (M5): "Grounded means *traceable to a real piece of evidence*... Traceability is a mechanical discipline, and an agent CAN be forced into it." — groundedness IS the frame's evidentiary half, made checkable.
- practice-of-risk.md (M4): "What may the agent read? What may it change?" — security is the frame's negative space: who controls the context and its boundary.

Where it strains, on the page: M6's when-the-score-stops-moving.md shows the frame's own instrument (a judge built from evidence-context) going blind once the generator stops floating claims — "The work kept getting better in a direction the judge was never built to see... A grounded claim can still be vague, obvious, or useless, and this judge will pass it every time." The training names the limit of its own context-shaping machinery, though it frames this as the judge's scope rather than an explicit break in "context is king" itself — the frame bends without the training calling it a break by that name.

## Narrative

**Five sentences, with the turn:** A student arrives able only to chat, producing generic output because their context is thin. They build a memory that remembers and compounds instead of resetting, then split it into agents whose synthesized answer leaves them at "an uneasy distance where you'd stake your reputation on some of it and not all of it" (three-minds-one-synthesis.md, M3). They build a discipline to bound the risk and a benchmark to catch fabrication, and watch the fabrication rate collapse under a fix loop — "10% fabricated → 1% after fix... The same compounding that destroys single-pass reliability *builds* loop-based reliability" (grounded.md, M5). Then the score goes flat and they read it as done, until the training corrects them mid-turn: "It is tempting to read that as 'the work is done.' That is not what happened... The judge told you what it could. The rest is yours to name" (when-the-score-stops-moving.md, M6) — the training's own central teaching artifact (the fixed judge it spent a module getting the student to trust) shown as structurally blind, on the page, right after the student leaned on it. The training closes not with mastery but by refusing the frame of completion itself: "You do not graduate. You have a flywheel" (joint-double-diamond.md, M8).

## Point of view

**Who is speaking:** a second-person coaching voice, mostly plural-collective ("we," "you," occasional "Nobody"), that repeatedly steps out of instruction to admit uncertainty rather than authority.

Three places the narrator shows:
- what-just-happened.md (M1): "Nobody knows where agents are going. Not Anthropic, not your board, not the people running this training." — the narrator places itself inside the same not-knowing as the reader.
- practice-of-risk.md (M4): "Damn, this is complex stuff. It will still be complex when the next agent gets built. The discipline is what carries." — a register break into plain first-person exhaustion, not polish.
- when-the-score-stops-moving.md (M6): "The judge told you what it could. The rest is yours to name." — the narrator naming the limit of a tool it built and taught, in the same breath as handing responsibility back.

**Is the narrator's own failure on the page?** Not as an "I got this wrong" confession, but close: the narrator's own teaching instrument (the M5 judge, the training's centerpiece artifact) is shown failing at exactly the moment the student is most likely to trust it, and the narrator calls this out itself rather than letting the trainer misread it. That is the narrator's construction failing, named by the narrator, not a hedge — but it stops short of the narrator saying "I was wrong to teach this" outright.

## Stance

Positions the training could lose a customer over, each with its defense:
- **"Single-agent is the plain default."** Defended by mechanism, not assertion: "Splitting adds coordination cost: handoffs to manage, mis-framing to watch for, prompts to tune per agent, a synthesis step that lies if you let it" (when-to-split-an-agent.md, M3). Argued right after a whole module showcased multi-agent working — "A whole module just showed you multi-agent works. Next Monday, you will be tempted to apply it to everything. Don't." This undercuts the more impressive, more sellable claim it just demonstrated.
- **"'Share the whole agent' is a vendor pitch and is not on the list."** (personal-to-team.md, M7) Defended by the absorption-vs-access mechanism run through the whole module ("access is not absorption... the people plan stalls on names"), refusing the simplest, most marketable sharing story in favor of a harder four-shape taxonomy.
- **"Certainty is not on offer... Some of your agents are going to be wrong, in ways you won't catch, and ship output you won't love. That's true for every agent in production anywhere in the world right now."** (practice-of-risk.md, M4) Defended by mechanism (non-determinism + instruction-set attack surface + emergent capability, named explicitly), not softened into a caveat.

Position held against the training's own commercial interest: the single-agent default is the clearest case — a training built to sell agentic capability spends a module lecture talking students out of building more agents than they need ("please, for the sake of your sanity, write the one prompt"), and the M7 vendor-pitch rejection similarly declines the easiest thing to sell (a whole shareable agent) in favor of a harder, truer taxonomy.

## The pair

Stance and self-doubt sit next to each other throughout, not sequenced apart: M3 closes on unresolved doubt about a synthesized answer the student is told to hold, not fix ("That feeling is correct... Hold the doubt"); M4 names a residual risk that mitigation never fully removes; M6 shows the training's own trusted judge going blind mid-loop. None of these get papered over by the stance-holding — the training argues hard positions (single-agent default, vendor-pitch rejection, no-certainty) while simultaneously admitting its own artifacts' limits on the page. It reads as someone who has been there: doubt is present but doesn't collapse into a hedge (positions are still defended with mechanisms, not walked back), and stance is present but doesn't collapse into a pitch (the training repeatedly declines its easiest, most sellable claims).
